# Part IX — Governance

## 41. Institutional form

The benchmark should be governed by an independent nonprofit, standards-oriented consortium, or fiscally sponsored public-interest project.

No model provider, software vendor, audit firm, or submitter should control official scoring, hidden data, or dispute resolution.

## 42. Governing bodies

### Program Council

Responsible for charter, budget, appointments, and strategic direction.

Recommended composition:

- independent chair;
- preparer and controller representatives;
- audit and internal-control representatives;
- accounting academics;
- evaluation and statistics experts;
- security and privacy experts;
- technology developers;
- enterprise users;
- public-interest representative.

A majority should be independent of active submitters.

### Accounting Practice Board

Defines task relevance, professional realism, reviewer standards, and domain coverage.

### Measurement Science Committee

Owns statistical models, sampling, uncertainty, human baselines, score validity, and benchmark-health analysis.

### Evaluation Integrity Board

Owns hidden data, contamination controls, red teaming, sandboxing, audits, and incident response.

### Technical Working Groups

Build tracks, environments, graders, schemas, and reference implementations.

### Submitter Forum

Provides operational feedback but does not adjudicate its own disputes or control hidden test content.

### Appeals Panel

Independent members with no conflict in the appealed result.

## 43. Conflict-of-interest rules

- Task authors disclose affiliations and financial interests.
- Reviewers recuse from results involving their employer or product.
- Submitters cannot vote on their own compliance dispute.
- Hidden-test access is role-limited and logged.
- Individuals with hidden-test access cannot submit a candidate in the same round unless separated by an approved firewall.
- Material conflicts are published.
- Paid experts are compensated consistently to reduce sponsor influence.

## 44. Open rulemaking

Changes to:

- tracks;
- scoring;
- divisions;
- conformance requirements;
- submission rules;
- retirement thresholds; and
- governance

should use public requests for comment.

Hidden item content remains confidential, but the measurement model must be public.

## 45. Benchmark proposal process

A new benchmark track requires:

1. written proposal;
2. user and decision relevance;
3. sampling frame;
4. task-source plan;
5. grader plan;
6. human-baseline plan;
7. contamination plan;
8. cost and operational plan;
9. advisory board;
10. preview round;
11. public report;
12. formal acceptance vote.

Coverage should grow slowly enough that each active track remains maintainable.
