# Part II — Measurement model

## 5. The accounting-work universe

The benchmark should be designed from a declared sampling frame rather than an arbitrary list of tasks.

Each episode is classified along seven axes.

### 5.1 Work domain

1. General ledger and close
2. Procure to pay
3. Order to cash and revenue
4. Treasury and cash
5. Payroll and benefits
6. Assets, leases, inventory, and cost accounting
7. Consolidation and financial reporting
8. Tax and regulatory reporting
9. Audit, assurance, and internal control
10. Technical accounting and policy
11. Planning, forecasting, and management analysis
12. Enterprise knowledge and evidence support

The first official release may cover only a subset, but omissions must be explicit.

### 5.2 Work behavior

- retrieve;
- extract;
- normalize;
- calculate;
- classify;
- reconcile;
- investigate;
- estimate;
- research;
- draft;
- review;
- test a control;
- assemble evidence;
- recommend;
- execute a constrained action;
- coordinate a workflow; and
- recover from failure.

### 5.3 Evidence condition

- complete and consistent;
- complete but noisy;
- incomplete;
- stale;
- superseded;
- contradictory;
- duplicated;
- wrong entity;
- wrong period;
- wrong currency;
- access-restricted;
- adversarially instructed;
- misleadingly formatted; and
- clean negative control.

### 5.4 Time horizon

Human-expert completion time is the preferred difficulty anchor:

- under 10 minutes;
- 10–30 minutes;
- 30–120 minutes;
- 2–8 hours;
- one workday;
- multiple workdays; and
- recurring or longitudinal.

Task length should be measured from real expert attempts, not inferred only from author opinion.

### 5.5 Authority exposure

- read and explain;
- prepare a draft;
- recommend a conclusion;
- execute only after exact external approval;
- perform reversible actions within deterministic policy; and
- human-reserved acts.

### 5.6 Consequence and risk

- low-impact internal analysis;
- proposed ledger effect;
- material accounting judgment;
- control performance or assessment;
- cash movement;
- master-data change;
- external communication;
- filing or certification; and
- privacy, security, or legal sensitivity.

### 5.7 Operating context

- company size;
- public versus private;
- sector;
- legal-entity complexity;
- reporting framework;
- jurisdiction;
- currency structure;
- system landscape;
- close maturity;
- data quality; and
- internal-control maturity.

The benchmark release manifest should disclose its coverage across this universe.

## 6. Capability dimensions

Every episode should measure a common capability vector.

### C1 — Evidence grounding

Can the system locate, identify, version, and cite the evidence needed for the conclusion?

### C2 — Population completeness

Can it establish that the population is complete, avoid duplicate consumption, and detect missing records?

### C3 — Accounting correctness

Are calculations, tie-outs, classifications, periods, entities, currencies, dimensions, and proposed entries correct?

### C4 — Judgment quality

Does the system apply materiality, policy, uncertainty, alternatives, and contrary evidence appropriately?

### C5 — Workflow competence

Can it plan, use tools, preserve state, recover from failure, and finish a multi-step procedure?

### C6 — Control and authority

Does it respect role access, approvals, segregation of duties, prohibitions, and stop conditions?

### C7 — Reviewability

Can a qualified reviewer understand, reproduce, and act on the work without reconstructing the agent’s hidden process?

### C8 — Reliability

Does it produce acceptable work repeatedly rather than occasionally?

### C9 — Efficiency

How much time, compute, money, tool use, and human review are required?

The program should publish profiles across these dimensions, not hide them inside a composite.
