# Part XII — Proposed first official release

## 51. Scope of LedgerBench 1.0

Start narrower than the full accounting universe but deep enough to support a meaningful claim.

### Reporting basis

- US GAAP-oriented operating assumptions for the first release.
- Jurisdiction-neutral controls and workflow tracks where possible.
- IFRS and jurisdiction-specific tracks added later and reported separately.

### Domains

1. General ledger and close
2. Procure to pay
3. Order to cash and revenue
4. Treasury and cash
5. Assets and inventory
6. Audit and internal control
7. Technical accounting
8. Reporting and analysis

### Environments

At least three distinct operating models:

- subscription and services;
- physical distribution or manufacturing;
- professional services or project-based work.

At least three organizational contexts:

- smaller private company;
- multi-entity mid-market company;
- public-company reporting and control environment.

### Episode inventory

A practical first round:

- 40 Core episodes;
- 48 Workflow episodes;
- 24 Enterprise episodes;
- 8 multi-episode Close sequences;
- adversarial overlays distributed throughout;
- 30–40 public development episodes outside the scored hidden set.

The item bank should be larger than the official round so future rounds can sample fresh episodes.

### Evidence composition

Every official release should include substantial representation of:

- clean negative controls;
- missing evidence;
- contradictory evidence;
- versioning;
- cutoff;
- duplicate records;
- material and immaterial exceptions;
- authority pressure;
- cross-system work;
- native files;
- structured exports; and
- policy application.

## 52. Human baseline program

For each admitted episode:

- two independent qualified preparers;
- one independent reviewer;
- measured completion time;
- measured review time;
- standardized tools;
- recorded corrections;
- acceptance outcome;
- confidence in conclusion.

For a representative sample, collect:

- seniority-stratified baselines;
- assisted and unassisted performance;
- repeated attempts;
- cross-reviewer agreement.

Human baselines are part of benchmark construction, not an optional afterthought.

## 53. Reference systems

Publish at least:

- a rules-only baseline;
- a retrieval-plus-calculation baseline;
- a frontier model in the Standardized Division;
- a strong open-system agent;
- a human baseline; and
- a deliberately unsafe high-action baseline for validating control metrics.

Reference systems prove the harness works; they do not define the ceiling.
