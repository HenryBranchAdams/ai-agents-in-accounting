# LedgerBench governance

LedgerBench should be governed by an independent nonprofit, standards-oriented consortium, or fiscally sponsored public-interest project.

No model provider, accounting-software vendor, audit firm, sponsor, or active submitter should control official scoring, hidden items, or appeals.

## Bodies

### Program Council

Owns the charter, budget, appointments, strategy, and public-interest accountability.

### Accounting Practice Board

Owns task relevance, professional realism, reviewer qualifications, domain coverage, and the meaning of acceptable work.

### Measurement Science Committee

Owns sampling, statistical plans, human baselines, uncertainty, validity, ranking discipline, and benchmark-health analysis.

### Evaluation Integrity Board

Owns hidden data, contamination controls, red teaming, sandboxing, audits, benchmark-detection testing, and incident response.

### Technical Working Groups

Build environments, tracks, schemas, graders, adapters, and reference implementations.

### Submitter Forum

Provides operational feedback but does not control hidden content, scoring, or disputes involving its members.

### Independent Appeals Panel

Reviews task, environment, grader, scoring, rights, and compliance challenges without conflicts.

## Composition

A majority of decision-makers should be independent of active submitters.

The program should include:

- preparers and controllers;
- audit and internal-control practitioners;
- accounting academics;
- evaluation and statistics experts;
- security and privacy experts;
- enterprise users;
- technology developers;
- a public-interest representative.

## Conflicts

- Authors disclose affiliations and material interests.
- Reviewers recuse from results involving their employer or product.
- Submitters do not adjudicate their own compliance dispute.
- Hidden-item access is role-limited and logged.
- Individuals with hidden access cannot submit in the same round unless an approved firewall exists.
- Material conflicts and recusals are published.

## Rulemaking

Changes to products, tracks, divisions, scoring, submission rules, conformance profiles, lifecycle thresholds, or governance use a public request-for-comment process.

The measurement model is public. Hidden item content remains confidential.

## Appeals and corrections

A submitter may challenge ambiguity, unavailable evidence, environment failure, rights, grader validity, classification, scoring, or compliance disposition.

Possible remedies:

- no change;
- annotation;
- rescoring;
- task withdrawal;
- correction in a future release;
- benchmark version update;
- round pause or invalidation.

Sanitized decisions and rule changes are published.

## Funding

Sponsors receive no privileged item access, result treatment, or scoring control. Expert compensation should be consistent and disclosed.
