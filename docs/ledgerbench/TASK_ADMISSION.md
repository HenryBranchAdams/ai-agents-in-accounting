# LedgerBench task admission

An official LedgerBench episode must be realistic, solvable, rights-cleared, representative, and gradable without privileging one narrow narrative.

## Required admission record

The record must identify:

- user and business problem;
- acting role and accountable reviewer;
- domain and work behavior;
- initial enterprise state;
- evidence universe and as-of time;
- authority envelope;
- tools and resource budget;
- required deliverables;
- consequence and risk;
- human workflow and expected duration;
- acceptance model;
- task source and rights;
- related-item grouping keys;
- proposed primary split.

## Admission stages

1. **Nomination.** State the problem, role, evidence, authority, work product, risk, rights, and representativeness.
2. **Episode specification.** Define initial state, objective, tools, budget, terminal states, and acceptance model.
3. **Independent solve.** At least two qualified practitioners solve the episode without hidden expected outputs.
4. **Reviewer validation.** A separate qualified reviewer determines whether each solution would be accepted in practice.
5. **Ambiguity review.** Three independent reviewers assess instructions, evidence sufficiency, hidden requirements, alternative methods, and grader breadth.
6. **Grader proof.** Correct and alternative outputs pass. Plausible wrong, deceptive, incomplete, and formatting-confounded outputs fail.
7. **Adversarial review.** A red team attempts answer recovery, grader exploitation, future-evidence use, access bypass, input mutation, and unsafe action.
8. **Human baseline.** Record success, completion time, review time, corrections, evidence use, and disagreement.
9. **Multi-system pilot.** Diverse systems establish useful difficulty, separability, stable scoring, and operational feasibility.
10. **Admission.** Accounting-practice, measurement-science, and evaluation-integrity governance approve the episode.

## Acceptance model

A task does not have to have one golden narrative.

The acceptance model may include:

- deterministic invariants;
- allowed values and tolerances;
- permitted alternative methods;
- required evidence IDs and versions;
- hierarchical reviewer criteria;
- materiality;
- appropriate uncertainty;
- required completion, escalation, or stop behavior;
- hard exclusions.

Hard exclusions include fabricated evidence or approval, unauthorized execution, inaccessible evidence, hidden material exceptions, and mutation of source records.

## Maintenance

Episodes are monitored for:

- ambiguity challenges;
- grader false positives and false negatives;
- contamination;
- saturation;
- policy or standard changes;
- environment fragility;
- declining separability;
- unrepresentative failure harvesting.

Episodes move through `proposed`, `preview`, `active`, `saturated`, `retired`, and `archived`.
