# Controller areas 1 and 10: governance, controls, systems, and continuity

Research snapshot: 2026-09-24. This is US-first, source-backed design context for the family-office accounting guide. It is a discovery handoff, not a professional conclusion, control test, or canonical corpus addition.

## What the existing family-office records already cover

The existing [family-office guide](../../../../../data/corpus/guide.json) and [ownership, allocation, and payment control](../../../../../data/corpus/control.json) preserve separate office-company, individual, trust, investment-entity, and operating-entity records; separate account title from login or administrative access; reconcile banks, custodians, and ledgers; match reciprocal entries; document supported allocations; use dual approval and callback for changed payment instructions; restrict access; and keep material exceptions open. The [entity-close workflow](../../../../../data/corpus/workflow.json) is read-only. All six guide assessments remain partial. These records do not establish that the proposed controls operate effectively, and their supplemental family view is not a GAAP consolidation or an individual net-worth statement.

This research deepens the office-level structure around those activities: what services the office provides, who has authority for which decision, how controls are owned and reviewed, how systems and providers are inventoried, and how the office operates through an outage or leadership change.

## Area 1: office structure and governance

**Choose an operating model around complexity, capabilities, control needs, and cost.** A family may use a dedicated single-family office, a multi-family platform, outsourced specialists, or a hybrid arrangement. These models trade internal control and tailoring against cost, shared infrastructure, confidentiality, and coordination. The July 2026 Morgan Lewis practitioner article recommends assigning an internal integration owner when the office relies on multiple outside providers and periodically reassessing the model as family circumstances change. Treat its survey-derived cost figures and asset thresholds as one firm's reported market data and advice, not a universal benchmark or decision rule. The September 2024 EY/Wharton analysis also describes specialist outsourcing and reports capability and succession concerns, but its figures describe survey respondents rather than all family offices.

**Keep family-level governance connected to, but distinct from, office and entity authority.** EY's practitioner recommendation is to maintain parallel family and office governance, with communication between them: family governance supports owner purpose and capability; office governance oversees its own strategy, operations, and performance. The IIA's 2026 Three Lines Model supplies a general organizational role map: the governing body oversees and delegates; management owns and runs processes and controls; risk or compliance specialists support and challenge where the organization has them; and independent assurance checks the system without taking over management decisions. This is a flexible professional model, not a legal requirement to create a family council, office board, compliance department, or internal audit team.

For an actual family, the family council, family constitution, office executive, company board or manager, trustee, investment committee, custodian, and adviser may have different powers. Record each body's mandate, delegation, limits, escalation route, and reporting line. A family preference should be routed through the person or entity that has legal authority to act; a family forum's name or a staff member's access to a portal does not establish authority over company assets, trust property, or an investment account. Review the governing instruments and applicable law before assigning authority. Treat this boundary as a control-design recommendation, not a legal conclusion for an unidentified entity.

**SEC adviser status is a conditional legal question, not a synonym for “family office.”** The official 2025 annual CFR text of 17 CFR § 275.202(a)(11)(G)-1 describes an exclusion from the federal Investment Advisers Act definition when the family office meets the rule's client, ownership/control, and public-holding-out conditions. The rule's defined terms and limited statutory purpose matter. A multi-family or hybrid arrangement cannot assume the exclusion applies; serving another person after a death or involuntary transfer has a specific one-year transition provision. This worker read the 2025 annual CFR. The prior family-office [FO-R3 research](../../../2026-09-24-family-office-expansion/reporting-investments/brief.md#fo-r3-sec-family-office-exclusion-and-its-separate-perimeter) separately inspected the eCFR text current through September 22, 2026; that more recent point-in-time read is reused here. State adviser law, later amendments, other regulatory regimes, and any real office's clients, ownership, control, advice or public representations remain unverified. The existing source record `src_family_office_sec_2011_rule` is the historical 2011 adopting release.

**A usable governance record set** can include an entity and ownership map; office service catalogue and client/entity list; office charter and approved budget; governing-body and committee mandates; decision and signing-authority matrix; service agreements and cost-allocation basis; conflict/related-party register; minutes or decision log; exception and risk registers; and recurring performance, cash, close, and provider reports. Keep legal authority, operational delegation, accounting ownership, and system access as separate fields. Connect family-level or supplemental reporting to entity books through a documented adjustment and approval trail. The existing [office-philanthropy brief](../../../../../docs/research/2026-09-24-family-office-expansion/office-philanthropy/brief.md) already handles service-company versus household costs, nonprofit-vehicle independence, and household-employer responsibility; reuse those boundaries rather than folding them into a generic “family” owner. Its prior observations `FO-S030`, `FO-S031`, `FO-S035`, and `FO-S046` through `FO-S050` remain in the linked expansion [source inventory](../../../../../docs/research/2026-09-24-family-office-expansion/source-inventory.json), with existing source-record links including `src_irs_pub15_2026`, `src_professional_irs_pub15a_2026`, `src_aa_i115_irs_household_employer`, `src_nonprofit_fasb_2016_14`, `src_irs_form990_2025`, and `src_nonprofit_irs_990_2025`. They are not copied into this package.

## Area 10: controls, systems, and continuity

**Use a risk-based control map across operations, reporting, and compliance.** COSO's 2013 Internal Control—Integrated Framework, summarized in the 2019 Crowe/COSO implementation guide, organizes control around environment, risk assessment, control activities, information and communication, and monitoring. Its stated objectives extend beyond financial reporting to operations and compliance. The guide is healthcare-focused and is not the full licensed framework; it supports a general design lens, not a family-office rule. For each material office or entity process, record the objective and risk, responsible owner, preparer, reviewer/approver, evidence, frequency or trigger, source system, exception owner, and resolution.

The IIA model helps separate execution from challenge and assurance. The manager doing a payment, reconciliation, valuation input, or system-access change owns the first-line activity. A separate specialist may set policy or monitor higher-risk areas. An independent reviewer can provide assurance when scale, complexity, or risk warrants it. In a small office, one person may hold several roles; record the overlap and use a compensating independent review. Do not describe such review as independent assurance if the reviewer designed or operated the process. Internal audit is not required for every organization according to the IIA, and the right assurance design depends on the office's risks and obligations.

**Preserve transaction evidence through the full financial process.** The existing family-office control already calls for owner verification, dual authorization and independent callback for new payment instructions or payee changes, bank and custody reconciliations, supported cost drivers, matched intercompany IDs, access limits, and unresolved ownership or valuation exceptions. Those activities remain editorial proposals without operating-effectiveness evidence. Extend the workflow by mapping, for each entity, who initiates, approves, releases, records, reconciles, and reviews a transaction. Separate incompatible steps where practical; where staffing prevents that, document a compensating review by someone outside preparation and retain its evidence. Apply the same ownership and approval discipline to payroll, investment instructions, trust distributions, loans, grants, and related-party services, with specialist review where the activity has a separate legal or tax regime.

**Inventory systems and data in terms of the entity and service they support.** NIST SP 1300, its 2024 small-business quick-start guide to CSF 2.0, recommends an IT asset inventory with owner/administrator, official use, sensitive data, MFA status, and business impact if access is lost. It recommends limiting staff access, MFA on important accounts including banking, accounting/tax, email, and password managers, patching, encryption, backed-up data with restore tests, event monitoring, response authority, and a recovery playbook that identifies people, systems, services, responsibilities, and communications. These are voluntary cybersecurity recommendations for small organizations, not accounting-system specifications or proof that a family office is compliant.

For controller use, join that technology inventory to an entity/service map: legal owner; office or provider role; bank, custodian, general ledger, tax, payroll, and document systems; data categories; administrator and accountable business owner; authorized users; criticality; contract/renewal; backup and recovery owner; and termination/data-return path. Keep system access consistent with the entity's mandate and each person's duties. Preserve named users and audit trails rather than shared accounts; review access when roles change or end. Define which record is authoritative for each balance or instruction, retain dated source evidence, and keep reviewable backups outside the primary system. These are recommended controls; this research did not inspect a specific family office's systems, contracts, records, or security posture.

**Make continuity an owned and tested routine.** NIST's cyber guidance supports incident authority and communication lists, prioritizing recovery, checking backup integrity, documenting after-action findings, and identifying internal and external recovery responsibilities. EY's 2024 survey summary reports that about 40% of its respondents had formal succession plans for family-office leadership and about 20% had plans for the family and professional management; it also reports that about one quarter of respondents said family-office or personnel data had been compromised. These data are a bounded signal from the reported survey, not an audited prevalence estimate. They support treating succession, cyber response, and access continuity as governance agenda items.

A proportionate continuity plan can name a primary and alternate decision-maker for key processes; cover temporary or permanent loss of a principal, controller, trustee, authorized signer, essential employee, provider, facility, or system; identify manual workarounds and recovery priorities; maintain out-of-band contact and escalation details; and set an exercise schedule. Test restoration and data integrity, rehearse who may freeze or reinstate access and payment authority, and review provider dependencies, contact data, and delegated limits after a significant change. A cybersecurity incident plan and IT disaster recovery plan address only parts of broader business continuity. The sources read here do not establish an all-hazards, family-office-specific continuity standard or show that any family's plan has been tested.

## Recurring controller process and evidence

At onboarding or a material change, refresh the entity/owner/authority/service/provider/system map, governing documents, conflicts, access list, and approved delegations. At each close, complete entity-level cash, custody, and ledger reconciliations; match reciprocal entries and fees; document review; and carry forward unresolved items with an owner and due date. Periodically report office financial performance, service delivery, cash and commitments, material risk/control exceptions, provider performance, access/incident changes, and succession or continuity actions to the appropriate office oversight body. At least annually, review service scope, budget and allocations, authority limits, key roles, provider contracts, insurance and contact information, system inventory, backup/restore evidence, and continuity exercises. These cadences are a controller design proposal; the source set does not prescribe one frequency for every office.

## Handoffs and limits

Use legal counsel and the applicable governing instruments to settle entity ownership, office authority, trustee/fiduciary powers, state law, adviser status, and family succession documents. Use investment-regulatory counsel when investment advice, multiple families, public holding-out, or custody facts may affect registration or exemptions. Ask an accounting professional to decide current GAAP, consolidation, fiduciary, tax, or reporting treatment for actual facts. Bring in cybersecurity/privacy counsel and incident-response specialists for breach obligations and recovery after an event; NIST does not determine statutory, contractual, or insurer notification duties. Consider independent control assurance when risk and scale justify it, while keeping the reviewer apart from the work reviewed.

The work is US-first and does not survey state trust, privacy, employment, adviser, or breach-notification law; foreign entities and data; insurance, private trust companies, and complex investment operations; current FASB Codification; family-office actual records; or control effectiveness. The references in [sources.json](sources.json) carry their verification status and limits individually. No publisher full text is stored, no permission to reuse external content is inferred, no corpus record or assessment was changed, and no application or remote work was performed.
