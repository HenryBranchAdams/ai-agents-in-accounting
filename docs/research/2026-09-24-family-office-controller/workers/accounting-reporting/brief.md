# Family-office controller accounting, close, reporting and planning

Research date: September 24, 2026. This is a research-only handoff for controller area 2 (accounting and close) and area 11 (family reporting and planning). It proposes a clearer process around the current family-office package; it does not revise canonical records or establish an actual family's reporting policy.

## Existing coverage and main conclusion

The existing `guide-family-office-us-accounting`, `workflow-family-office-entity-close`, and `example-family-office-four-entity-close` already establish a useful foundation: keep the office company, investment partnership, trust, individual, and underlying businesses in separate ledgers; prove activity to statements and source documents; retain cost allocation support and reciprocal inter-entity balances; keep tax basis separate from carrying value and cash; and prepare any family view in a separate adjustment column. The four-entity fixture is expressly provisional and its supplemental view is a carrying-value illustration, not a personal statement at estimated current value or a GAAP consolidated balance sheet. Six associated answers remain partial. The neighboring `guide-holding-management-us-close` adds entity-level trial-balance matching, evidence-linked elimination entries, allocation and interest recomputation, and a separate tax handoff. Its evidence remains limited by the dated/historical sources described in that guide.

The strongest handoff is a **reporting map with separate books and clearly named views**, not a single undifferentiated “family consolidated” report. The controller should know for each entity who legally owns it, who controls it, who may benefit from it, who keeps its books, its tax classification, and which statements are prepared. Then prepare standalone books first. Only afterward build a GAAP consolidation, a personal financial statement, or a management aggregation when the purpose and rules for that view are known. A common owner, common family name, administrative access, or trust-beneficiary relationship alone does not answer those questions.

The public evidence is uneven by design. IRS Publication 583 gives practical, official federal tax recordkeeping advice for businesses. Deloitte, the Association for Financial Professionals (AFP), the AICPA Journal of Accountancy, KPMG, and the family-office articles give professional or vendor practice guidance; they are not accounting standards. The KPMG February 2026 consolidation handbook reproduces current Codification excerpts and interprets them, but remains a professional firm's handbook. The personal-statement source text is old AICPA SOP 82-1, later incorporated into Topic 274. FASB's March 2026 meeting handout says Topic 274 remains current, but the handout is discussion material, not standard-setting action. I could read Deloitte's Topic 274 overview but not the current detailed Topic 274 paragraphs. Keep a current-authority check open before presenting any personal statement as GAAP-compliant.

The report-design proposal rests first on those substantive nonvendor sources and the existing family-office records. Sage and Family Office Exchange are labeled vendor/service-provider practice sources; they contribute example data fields and possible audience/cadence patterns only. They do not establish accounting measurement, mandatory reports, or legal obligations.

## Findings by controller question

### FO-C2.1 — What belongs in the entity and ledger map?

The existing guide's separated-entity pattern is sound. Maintain one master entity/account register with stable entity ID, legal name and form, jurisdiction, tax year/classification, owners and percentages, governance/control rights, beneficial-interest holders, bank and custodian account titles, responsible bookkeeper, outside administrator, reporting framework, reporting purpose, close calendar, and open legal/accounting status. Link each account to exactly one legal owner even if an office can log in or initiate transactions. Keep trusts, foundations, investment vehicles, management companies, individuals, and operating/property companies distinct. Ownership, control, beneficial enjoyment, tax ownership, and custody are related fields, not substitutes for each other.

Design the chart of accounts (COA) and reporting dimensions from the reports the office needs. Deloitte's chart-design guide treats the COA as a governed data model that should support statutory, management, and consolidation reporting; it discusses common definitions, rollups, account hierarchies, budget dimensions, access security, and mapping when different entity charts are retained. For a multi-entity family office, a practical design is a stable natural-account list plus controlled dimensions for legal entity, counterparty, investment/asset, property or activity, family cost center, currency, and restriction or ownership class. Use mapping tables when entity-level charts differ; do not encode every owner, property, investment, or period into an ever-growing natural-account list. These are design proposals, not mandatory standards.

For each posted event, retain its source document, event/effective date and book date, amount and currency, payer, legal obligor, legal owner/beneficiary, entity, account, counterparty, allocation driver if any, reporting basis, and preparer/reviewer. IRS Publication 583 describes business books as journals and ledgers supported by transaction documents, says to keep business and personal bank accounts separate, and explains monthly statement reconciliation and system/account documentation. Its advice is federal tax recordkeeping for businesses, not a complete group-accounting design or a substitute for state law.

### FO-C2.2 — What should the period close prove?

Turn the close into a dated calendar and a checklist by entity and account, with owner, source, due date, reviewer, materiality/escalation threshold, status, and exception link. Include statement collection and population completeness; bank and custodian reconciliations; investment quantity/activity and valuation-date checks; payables, receivables, payroll, debt, and accrued-expense cut-off; journal-entry support; allocation and reciprocal-balance matching; tax-basis and K-1 intake; review; reporting-view preparation; final approval and period lock; and a controlled post-close adjustment route. Run bank, credit-card, or custody reconciliations before relying on totals. Reconcile opening to ending balances, list outstanding items with age and owner, explain differences, and clear or escalate them. A balanced trial balance only proves debits equal credits; it does not prove the underlying account population or valuation is complete.

The archived AICPA Journal of Accountancy article by Kevin Kelso (2011) is explicit about close calendars, exception hierarchies, journal-entry metadata and independent review, comparative reporting, large-dollar/percentage variance review, SOP ownership and freshness dates, escalation to subject-matter experts, and post-close learning. Its own page warns the article is an archive that may be out of date, so use it as durable practice material rather than current authority. The newer family-office article from Sage similarly recommends a repeatable close, reconciliations, documented owners/approvals, and an exception log, but is vendor education. The existing workflow already includes separate bank/custodian reconciliations and unresolved exceptions; this handoff deepens it with a close calendar, cutoff, reviewer and escalation fields.

### FO-C2.3 — How should allocations, inter-entity activity, and basis be controlled?

For shared services, identify the service recipient and the benefit received, eligible cost pool, agreement, allocation driver, period, calculation, approver, and the corresponding entries on both entities. Keep a service fee and markup distinct from a reimbursement of costs. Reconcile all due-to/due-from and loan balances from both ledgers by invoice or transaction ID, confirm terms, accrue interest where supported, and retain unresolved differences instead of plugging them. A tax allocation or deductible-expense conclusion needs its own tax facts and current authority. The current family guide already identifies the beneficiary, allocation basis, agreement, reciprocal entries, and unpaid variance; the holding-company guide adds an independent recomputation and related-party evidence. Reuse `src_aa_i123_fas57` for the limited historical related-party research and keep current Topic 850 authority open.

Keep these measures in separate fields and schedules: entity-book carrying amount, applicable GAAP measurement basis, tax basis (including partnership outside basis), K-1 tax-capital account, funded cost, unfunded commitment, investee-reported NAV and its date, appraisal or market evidence, and any personal-statement estimate. The existing canonical source `src_family_office_irs_k1_1065_2025` says outside basis is separate from a partnership K-1 capital account and that partner tax reporting may arise without cash. It is tax-year-2025 instruction evidence, not GAAP measurement authority. Reuse it as the tax handoff, not as a valuation source.

For private-fund interests, determine the investor entity's own accounting before using the fund's reported NAV. The 2026 KPMG investment-companies handbook distinguishes an investee's ASC 946 accounting from the investor's assessment. Deloitte's ASC 820 roadmap reproduces the current NAV-expedient conditions: the interest must qualify, the NAV must be consistent with ASC 946 measurement principles, and the calculation should be as of the investor's measurement date; if the supplied NAV is late or inconsistent, consider whether an adjustment is necessary. A dated bridge can organize subsequent calls, distributions, underlying-value changes, expenses, liabilities and allocation/waterfall terms, but is not an automatic formula or valuation conclusion. Preserve the existing example's stale-NAV and unposted valuation amounts as unresolved.

### FO-C2.4 — When is an output a consolidation, combined statement, or aggregation?

Choose the reporting purpose before preparing a rollup:

| Output | Question answered | Required boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Standalone entity statements | What did this legal entity own, owe, earn and spend on its reporting basis? | One legal entity and its own ledger, policies and source support. |
| GAAP consolidated statements | Which entities are controlled by this reporting entity under the applicable model? | Perform ASC 810 scope and voting-interest/variable-interest control analyses; retain the reporting entity's conclusion and required presentation/disclosure work. |
| Combined statements | Would a combined presentation of commonly controlled or managed entities be more meaningful, when no parent-to-subsidiary consolidation perimeter supplies the answer? | Explain why entities are combined, define which entities, and apply appropriate elimination procedures. Do not label this as GAAP consolidation by default. |
| Personal financial statement | What are the individual's, spouses', or family's assets, liabilities and net worth for the stated use? | Check current ASC 274, statement scope, beneficial interests, estimated current measures, tax effects and disclosures. |
| Supplemental family aggregation / net-worth schedule | What selected family-owned or beneficial interests and obligations are relevant to internal planning? | State included people/entities, economic-interest convention, measurement bases and dates, exclusions, restrictions, inter-entity treatment, tax assumptions, and unresolved exceptions. It is not automatically GAAP or a personal financial statement. |

The KPMG February 2026 handbook describes the two ASC 810 models, the VIE and voting-interest analyses, and consolidation procedures. It also reproduces the combined-statement distinction: check whether consolidation is required first; commonly controlled entities without a reporting-entity controlling interest may sometimes have a combined presentation, but it is not simply the same label as consolidation. Reconcile member-level records to group totals, prepare elimination entries in a separate column, retain third-party balances, and keep a trail from each consolidated number to entity trial balances and approved adjustments. Ownership percentage by itself does not settle VIE control or scope exceptions.

For personal financial statements, the reprinted SOP 82-1 explains that the intended users and purpose matter; it includes an individual, spouses, or family as possible reporting entities. It describes estimated current values of assets, estimated current amounts of liabilities, an estimated tax provision on differences from tax bases, and net worth; it limits jointly held property to the person's beneficial interest under applicable law and says to present a substantial separate going-concern business as one investment rather than combine its underlying balances with personal items. FASB's March 2026 handout says Topic 274 originated in SOP 82-1 and that a family may be the reporting entity, but notes stakeholder questions about the current estimated-current-value guidance. The exact current ASC 274 paragraphs and any later amendments were not directly verified. The SOP's mechanics should be reported as historical source material pending that current-text check.

For any net-worth schedule, include liabilities, and show unfunded or other commitments in a separate liquidity/obligation schedule with their status and due date. Do not imply an uncalled commitment is a recognized liability or add it to net worth without support. The family-office reporting article from Sage lists cash by account/entity and amounts reserved for tax, payroll, property costs and commitments; private investment calls, unfunded commitments, distributions, fees and NAV date; property values/appraisal dates, operating costs and debt; and other loans and intercompany balances. That is useful vendor practice guidance, not a rule that all items must be included or measured alike. Include an item only under the schedule's declared ownership/economic-interest policy. If both a family's interest in an entity and that entity's underlying assets are counted, reconcile the duplication explicitly. If a trust is included, do not automatically attribute its full corpus to a settlor or beneficiary; use the trust instrument, law and supported beneficial rights. The existing example keeps the trust corpus and individual beneficial interest separate and does not assign a value to the latter.

### FO-C11.1 — What belongs in a family reporting and planning pack?

Treat planning reports and accounting statements as separate outputs built from reconciled data. A management package can include a short principal view of liquidity, net worth by entity/asset class/liquidity bucket, outstanding debt, upcoming cash needs and capital commitments, period change, and major risks; an office-operations view of actual spending, vendor/payroll, accounts payable/receivable and entity reconciliations; an entity manager/controller view with ledger detail, allocations, debt schedules and exceptions; a tax-preparer view with taxpayer/year identifiers, book-to-tax schedules and basis; and separate trust/foundation schedules for the responsible fiduciary or governing body. Share only each audience's required detail and restrict underlying personal, trust, tax, custody and payment data by role.

Label every amount with entity or person, report date, currency, source, valuation date, basis, restrictions and status (reported, estimated, provisional, stale or unresolved). Label the report purpose on the cover. Use a short change narrative: cash in/out; earnings versus transfers; realized/unrealized movement; newly received or stale valuations; capital activity; debt and commitments; family/office spending variance; and exceptions with owners and expected resolution dates. Distinguish principal, office, trust, family-philanthropy and investment schedules so payments among them do not appear as unexplained income or spending. The value of this approach is clarity: a high-level net-worth figure alone does not explain ownership, liquidity, tax, restrictions or time lag.

The family-specific practice sources recommend different views for principals, operating staff, tax directors and investment analysts and show a cadence matched to decisions. Sage describes monthly operating reporting, quarterly portfolio views and annual tax/audit packages as a possible pattern; these frequencies are not universal. Family Office Exchange recommends standardizing and integrating data, dashboards and drill-downs, and consulting tax/legal specialists. Both sources are practical/vendor or service-provider claims rather than standards. Set cadence and audience from the family mandate, legal duties, decision cycle, service agreements and recipient needs.

### FO-C11.2 — How should budget, forecast and spending views work?

Make the budget population explicit: office-company operating expenses and payroll; property or operating-entity plans; family or principal personal spending; planned giving; and investment capital calls are distinct schedules even if they feed a liquidity forecast. State the approving person/body and limit, period, account or cost-center mapping, allocation policy, committed versus discretionary item, and source of the plan assumption. Present authorized budget, actual book activity, open commitments, and latest forecast separately. Reconcile actuals to the entity books. Explain material variance using a cause (timing, price/rate, volume/activity, change in assumption, unplanned event), an owner and a forecast or proposed decision. Do not use a spending budget as proof of legal authority, tax deductibility, trust permission, investment approval or payment instruction.

AFP's August 2026 budgeting guidance defines budgets as plans that allocate resources and communicate expectations; it distinguishes budget from forecast, describes variance analysis, and discusses assumptions, controls and thresholds, operating and capital budget differences, rolling forecasts and scenario updates. Apply those management-accounting concepts only to entity/family decisions the authorized principals actually want. The sources reviewed here establish no generic mandatory spending percentage, safe withdrawal rate, or universally correct annual family budget. The family-specific reporting articles suggest tracking planned purchases, philanthropy, staffing, vendor costs, cash reserves and expected taxes; they do not supply an authoritative family-distribution or investment policy.

### FO-C11.3 — When should the controller escalate to a specialist?

Escalation is a proposed workflow, not a statement of mandatory professional referral rules. Ask for a US GAAP technical accountant when the consolidation perimeter, VIE/VOE model, investment-company scope, equity method, related-party treatment, or combined-statement conclusion is material or unclear. Ask for a valuation professional when private holdings, closely held businesses, restricted securities, real estate, art/jewelry, a stale fund value or a proposed personal statement depends on material current value. The SOP explicitly identifies some such assets as cases where a specialist may be needed, but that statement belongs to the 1982 source and must be checked against current requirements. Ask a tax professional about tax-basis/K-1 differences, elections, tax provision assumptions, entity classification, withholding and cross-year reporting. Ask trust/estate counsel or a qualified fiduciary accountant before treating trust assets, principal/income, beneficiary rights, trustee powers, estate ownership or distributions as family property or obligations. Ask securities counsel/compliance specialists if the family office serves multiple families, holds itself out publicly, or the adviser-law exclusion's ownership/control/client conditions may not be met; that perimeter is separate from accounting consolidation. Escalate whenever account ownership, payment authority, source completeness, valuation date, recipient confidentiality or a material unresolved difference cannot be supported.

Preserve assessments as partial where current standards, actual family instruments/facts, or professional review are missing.
