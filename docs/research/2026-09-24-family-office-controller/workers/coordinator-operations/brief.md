# Properties, people and philanthropy: controller operations

Research snapshot: 2026-09-24. This supplements the [prior family-office package](../../../2026-09-24-family-office-expansion/INDEX.md). It is an original controller operating map, not an entity-specific accounting conclusion. The source observations for new reads are in `sources.json`; prior source identities retain their metadata in the earlier inventory.

## 7. Properties and personal assets

**Understand and route.** Keep an owner, legal entity, use, financing, insurance and reporting-basis identifier for each property and significant asset. An investment rental, personally used residence and office-owned asset may require different ledgers and tax workpapers. The existing `guide-real-estate-us-roles` and `wf-real-estate-property-to-ledger` cover property-role routing; R102/R106 in the specialty follow-up cover lease/deposit distinctions. [IRS Publication 527 (2025)](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p527), “Rental Income,” “Repairs and Improvements,” and “Personal Use of Dwelling Unit,” supports a tax-side distinction among rent, potentially refundable security deposits, repairs, improvements and mixed personal/rental use. These tax classifications do not establish a financial-statement basis or a universal journal entry.

**Recurring work.** Reconcile rent roll, lease terms, bank receipts, deposits and owner/manager statements by property. Preserve closing statements and title, debt, insurance, capital-project, work-order and disposition files. Maintain a separate asset/basis rollforward with acquisition costs, later improvements, depreciation and disposition support; [Publication 551 (12/2025)](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p551), Introduction and “Adjusted Basis,” explains why tax basis history must be retained. Record personal use and family-paid costs apart from rental activity. For significant personal possessions, an inventory with photos, descriptions and coverage data helps claims and insurance review; [NAIC *A Consumer’s Guide to Home Insurance* (2022)](https://content.naic.org/sites/default/files/publication-hoi-pp-consumer-homeowners.pdf) supports the claim-documentation purpose, not an accounting valuation. A property manager’s settlement must be reconciled to owner books and deposits, rather than copied as a single net receipt. The controller should monitor insurance renewal dates, limits and named insureds and refer coverage judgment to broker/counsel.

**Conditional and handoff.** Ownership vehicles, lease terms, local tenant-deposit rules, property tax, rental-use days, development activity, debt and environmental exposures change treatment. Bring tax advisers into mixed-use, basis, depreciation and sale questions; counsel into title, leases and deposits; valuation and insurance specialists into material fair-value or coverage questions. The current package does not resolve a particular property's financial-statement basis, state deposit law or insurance adequacy. Source intake: reuse the specialty package's `cp_irs_527` and the family-office package's `fo24_loans_p551`; propose the NAIC home-insurance guide as a new practical source. Lower-priority specialty details such as a full REIT/development guide remain deferred with the 45 specialty questions.

## 8. People and service operations

**Understand and route.** Build a worker and vendor register identifying service recipient, hiring/control facts, legal employer or contracting party, payor, reimbursement party, agreement, authorization and sensitive records. The prior [office/philanthropy brief](../../../2026-09-24-family-office-expansion/office-philanthropy/brief.md) and `src_aa_i115_irs_household_employer` already establish the household-company boundary. [IRS Publication 926 (2026)](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p926), “Who Are Household Employees?” and “What Records Must You Keep?”, and [Publication 15-A (2026)](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p15a), “Common-Law Employees” and section 2, support a facts-based employer/worker inquiry. The location of work or entity funding the payroll alone does not settle employer identity.

**Recurring work.** Reconcile approved time/service records to payroll registers, tax deposits, W-2/1099 outputs, bank cash and general-ledger labor and benefit costs. Attribute shared staff using documented time or another supportable driver, preserving both interentity sides and any personal benefit. Retain vendor onboarding, tax information, contracts, service levels, invoices, change orders, access grants, payments and termination records. [Publication 15 (2026)](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p15), section 16, explains that payroll outsourcing generally leaves the employer responsible for federal tax duties, subject to defined exceptions. The controller should independently reconcile provider filings/deposits to payroll and assign a reviewer for vendor and bank-detail changes. These are proposed operating controls; the IRS publications do not prescribe the whole control design.

**Conditional and handoff.** State employment law, household setting, benefit-plan status, worker classification, cross-entity allocation and particular outsourcing contracts change the answer. Employment counsel/HR should review classification and household arrangements; benefits and payroll specialists should review plan and filing rules; tax advisers should review fringe benefits and reimbursements. Reuse existing payroll source identities `fo24_irs_pub926_2026`, `fo24_irs_pub15a_2026`, `fo24_irs_pub15_2026` and their canonical IDs. Remaining gap: benefits plan administration and state household rules are signposts, not researched treatments here.

## 9. Philanthropy

**Understand and route.** Separate a private foundation's books and legal authority from a donor's advisory position over a donor-advised fund. The [IRS DAF page](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/charitable-organizations/donor-advised-funds) says the sponsor has legal control after contribution. A donor recommendation is a proposed action, not a family-office asset or disbursement. A private foundation is a separate charitable organization with its own grants, investments, costs, policies, filing calendar and insiders. The prior `FO-O2` brief contains substantive federal self-dealing, Form 990-PF and DAF findings. Reuse its source identities, especially `fo24_irs_daf`, `fo24_irc4941`, `fo24_irc4946` and `fo24_irs_i990pf_2025`.

**Recurring work.** For a foundation, reconcile donor receipts, custody/investments, restricted or designated resources, grant approval, payments and monitoring, compensation/administration, and year-end financial books to return workpapers. The [2025 Form 990-PF instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i990pf), Part I column (d), require a cash disbursement bridge even if books use another method and forbid duplicate amounts in investment/charitable columns. Maintain an insider/related-party register and route proposed payments or transactions for tax/legal review before execution. For a DAF, retain contribution acknowledgement, sponsor statements, recommendations and sponsor decisions; do not book sponsor assets as family-controlled investments after contribution. Existing `guide-us-nonprofit-cross-industry-routing` and nonprofit sources help with financial-accounting distinctions, but historical ASUs are not current consolidated GAAP.

**Conditional and handoff.** Foundation governing documents, grant recipient, donor-adviser relationships, restricted gifts, compensation, cross-border grants and state charity rules need specialist analysis. Foundation counsel and tax advisers should handle self-dealing, taxable expenditures, distribution and return positions; investment advisers/valuation specialists handle material contributed assets and investment measurement. The present research supports the role and workflow map. It does not establish a specific grant's permissibility or a current GAAP treatment for every contribution.

## Cross-area disposition

The three areas now have an operating route, specific records and specialist triggers. The strongest new evidence is Pub. 527 for rental versus personal use and repair/capitalization records, Pub. 551 for tax basis continuity, and NAIC for insurance inventory. Payroll and philanthropy rely mostly on earlier substantive reads, now organized as controller processes. Material remaining dependencies are actual ownership and contracts, chosen reporting bases, state employment/property rules, charity instruments and tax-year refresh. These are explicit scope limits, not a claim of complete specialty coverage.
