# Reading-room profession and regulator expansion

**Research cutoff:** 2026-08-23  
**Scope:** first-party publications from regulators, standard setters, professional accountancy bodies, tax authorities, supreme audit institutions, governments, and international organizations concerning AI or advanced automation in accounting, audit, tax, controllership, finance operations, internal control, governance, and workforce change.  
**Deduplication basis:** `app/resources-data.ts` and `app/reading-room-data.ts` were inspected before selection. No application source was changed.

## Recommendation at a glance

- Add the **18 P0 records** below to the catalog. Feature a smaller **12-reading route** in the public reading room; the rest should live on their subject shelves and in related-reading links.
- The most material gaps in the present catalog are **tax-practitioner duties**, **preparer-side corporate reporting**, **regulator field observations outside the US/UK**, **public financial management**, and **management-accounting/workforce evidence**.
- Do not present every first-party publication as “guidance.” An internally binding government policy, a professional regulator's interpretation, an audit inspector's observations, a self-report survey, and a profession-sponsored thought piece have very different evidentiary weight.
- Add an explicit `authority_class` or `evidence_role` field. The current `ResourceKind` values are useful editorial formats, but `Official guidance` by itself does not say whether a document is binding, interpretive, consultative, or merely good practice.
- Keep country and language visible. Global coverage is a strength only when readers can see the jurisdictional boundary rather than mistaking one regulator's expectations for universal requirements.

### Priority key

- **P0 — add and feature selectively:** distinctive primary source that closes a material coverage gap.
- **P1 — add to catalog:** credible depth source; feature only on the relevant shelf or in related reading.
- **Watch — do not add yet:** relevant source whose publication page, final status, access, or distinctiveness is not strong enough for the public collection.

### Authority and evidence key

- **A — binding authority in its stated scope:** legislation, a mandatory standard, or an internally binding government policy. It is not automatically binding on readers outside that scope.
- **G — official, nonauthoritative guidance:** regulator, professional-body, or government interpretation, alert, good-practice guide, or implementation aid. The underlying law, code, or standard remains controlling.
- **O — regulator observation or independent government audit:** inspection findings, thematic review, or public audit. Describes observed practice; it does not itself create requirements.
- **S — survey or field research:** empirical evidence with a disclosed sample or field method. It measures the sampled population, not universal adoption, accuracy, or return on investment.
- **T — profession or institutional thought leadership:** synthesis, proposed framework, technical note, or policy argument. Useful reasoning, but neither authority nor outcome evidence.
- **P — disclosed practice example or strategy:** an institution's own use, roadmap, or operating position. Treat as implementation evidence with self-report limitations.

The catalog should show this classification beside, not instead of, its existing `topic` and `kind` fields.

## Recommended 12-reading public route

1. [The use of Artificial Intelligence and the Code of Professional Conduct](https://www.tpb.gov.au/tpbgs-552026-use-artificial-intelligence-and-code-professional-conduct) — the clearest current mapping from AI and autonomous functionality to tax-practitioner duties.
2. [The Responsible Use of AI in Professional Practice](https://www.cpaontario.ca/members/regulations-guidance/regulatory-publications/the-responsible-use-of-ai) — concise professional-regulator guidance on competence, verification, confidentiality, and disclosure.
3. [Guidelines for using generative artificial intelligence if you’re a software developer](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/guidelines-for-using-generative-artificial-intelligence-if-youre-a-software-developer) — unusually specific government expectations for AI-enabled tax software.
4. [Delivering Quality Audits in a Technology-Driven Environment](https://www.acra.gov.sg/news-events/news-announcements/906/) — Singapore audit-regulator guidance connecting technology use to firm quality management.
5. [12 building blocks for safer use of advanced (GenAI) audit tools](https://www.afm.nl/nl-nl/sector/actueel/2025/dec/pb-12-bouwstenen-beheerst-gebruik-audit-tooling) — a compact audit-tool control model from the Dutch financial regulator.
6. [The use of Artificial Intelligence Technologies in Corporate Reporting](https://www.frc.org.uk/library/research-and-insights/the-use-of-artificial-intelligence-technologies-in-corporate-reporting/) — current preparer-side evidence rather than another audit-only view.
7. [Thematic Review: Certification of Automated Tools and Techniques](https://www.frc.org.uk/documents/8383/Thematic_Review_on_the_Certification_of_Automated_Tools_and_Techniques.pdf) — regulator observations on tool approval, testing, training, vendors, and monitoring at six large audit firms.
8. [Structured Digital Reporting: Insights 2025/26](https://www.frc.org.uk/library/digital-reporting/structured-digital-reporting-insights-202526/) — shows why machine-readable reporting quality and accessibility matter to downstream AI use.
9. [Artificial Intelligence: IRS Actions Needed to Address Skills Gaps, Information Quality, and Strategic Management](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-26-107522) — independent government audit of a tax authority's AI inventory, skills, information quality, and governance.
10. [Understanding Artificial Intelligence in Tax and Customs Administration](https://www.imf.org/en/publications/tnm/issues/2024/11/21/understanding-artificial-intelligence-in-tax-and-customs-administration-555097) — the strongest international public-administration primer and policy template.
11. [Shaping the Profession: Generative AI and professional judgement in accounting](https://www.icas.com/news-insights-events/documents/shaping-the-profession-generative-ai-and-professional-judgement-in-accounting-2026) — year-long, firm-level evidence with unusually clear limits on productivity claims.
12. [Enabling finance insight: Bridging skills and data gaps for AI-enabled finance](https://www.accaglobal.com/gb/en/professional-insights/technology/enabling-finance-insight.html) — broad management-accounting coverage from a 1,600-person global study.

This route deliberately mixes duties, oversight, preparer evidence, public tax administration, and finance work. Each card should carry the authority/evidence label above so the sequence does not imply equal weight.

## Deduplication and editorial decisions

1. **No exact proposed title above appears in the current catalog or reading-room data.** The candidate search also checked distinctive owner/title strings, not only URLs.
2. **Do not add another record for the FRC's 2026 “AI in audit” guidance.** It is already indexed. The new FRC records above are different: preparer-side corporate reporting, a six-firm ATT thematic review, and structured-digital-filing observations.
3. **Do not duplicate current IAASB primers, projects, or standards.** The roundtable outcomes remain P1 because they are consultation evidence about technology and quality management, not another authoritative baseline.
4. **Keep IFIAR's cross-jurisdiction technology report, already indexed, as the global overview.** ACRA, AFM, WPK, and IRBA add local operating detail and should link back to it.
5. **Keep current IESBA, APESB, AICPA & CIMA, COSO, IIA framework, IMA, OECD tax-administration, CAW/Ipsos, ACCA/CA ANZ audit-playbook, and GAO baseline records.** The additions above were selected only where they add a distinct jurisdiction, professional duty, empirical method, preparer function, or public-finance use case.
6. **Do not count the base PCRT and its AI topical guide twice on the reading-room route.** The catalog may preserve both documents, but the AI guide card should explain that mandatory force comes from the participating bodies' underlying PCRT obligations.
7. **Do not label regulator-origin material automatically as authoritative.** IRBA's own page expressly disclaims authority; AFM and ACRA publish good practice; FRC thematic observations do not amend standards; IRS IRM is binding only inside the IRS.
8. **Do not turn survey percentages into product or productivity claims.** The FRC, ICAS, ACCA/CA ANZ, CPA Australia, and IIA studies primarily report adoption, experience, or perception in their samples.
9. **Prefer landing pages over loose PDFs**, except where the regulator exposes only a stable first-party PDF or index. Preserve a separate `download_url` later if the data model supports it.

## Metadata and agent-access recommendations

Each record should expose the following fields in the human page and in the site's JSON/NDJSON interface:

- `authority_class`: `binding`, `official_nonauthoritative`, `regulator_observation`, `survey_or_field_research`, `thought_leadership`, or `practice_example`;
- `issuing_body_type`: regulator, standard setter, professional body, tax authority, supreme audit institution, government audit office, or international organization;
- `jurisdiction`, `language`, `published_on`, `status`, and `reviewed_on`;
- `method` and `sample`, when empirical;
- `evidence_role`: duty interpretation, oversight observation, adoption evidence, governance design, workforce evidence, or implementation example;
- `transfer_limit`: a one-sentence statement of what the source does **not** establish;
- `access_status`: open HTML, open PDF, registration, member access, or paid;
- canonical `source_url`, optional `download_url`, and a stable local `source_id`.

This makes queries such as “open regulator observations about audit tools outside the UK,” “final tax-practitioner guidance,” or “global surveys about finance-team data readiness” possible without asking an agent to infer authority from prose.

## Selection method

The search prioritized first-party publication pages and first-party PDFs from the issuing institution. Candidates were selected when they added at least one of: a distinct professional duty, a regulator's observed practice, a disclosed empirical method, a missing accounting function, a materially underrepresented jurisdiction, or a public-sector/tax implementation boundary. Publication status, sample or method, access, and jurisdiction were checked on the issuing body's page. Broad AI governance documents, announcements without stable publications, duplicate versions, vendor studies, and sources already represented in the application data were excluded or held.
