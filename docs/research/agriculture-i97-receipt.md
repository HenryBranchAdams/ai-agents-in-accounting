## Selected numerical evidence

The producer's $44,000 cash-paid growing inputs plus the contractor's $6,000 invoice create the assumed $50,000 eligible lot. Gross scale-equivalent output of 10,200 less a separately documented synthetic conversion of 200 equals 10,000 standardized bushels. This is not a real moisture conversion formula. The base sale of 6,000 at $8 gives $48,000 revenue and $30,000 cost, retaining 4,000 units at $20,000. December cash/AR/inventory of $16,000/$48,000/$20,000 less $6,000 AP equals $78,000 equity. After January collection and service payment, cash is $58,000, inventory $20,000 and AR/AP zero.

The contractor separately records 200 completed acres at $30, $4,800 service costs, $6,000 receivable and $1,200 margin. Opening cash $10,000 less costs plus January collection yields $11,200. Customer crop inventory is zero in the contractor ledger. The shared invoice and bank IDs connect the two entities without merging their assets.

An independent extension reconciles 6,000 regular-sale units, 800 quality-discounted sale units, 200 spoiled units and 3,000 December ending units to the 10,000 harvest. Cost reconciles $30,000 + $4,000 + $1,000 + $15,000 = $50,000. The quality deduction is $1,200 of price, not lost units. January shipment of 1,500 units remains in December inventory. A separate incomplete-service branch challenges a full invoice when only 160 of 200 acres are complete. Its $4,800 earned amount depends on a separately stated per-acre performance/right-to-payment assumption.

Original transition dates were later deferred; actual 2026 adoption/current text is not certified.
