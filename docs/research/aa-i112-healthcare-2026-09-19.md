# US health-care and social-assistance source package

This source-only proposal addresses issue 112 against main 3f415d8. It preserves six existing NAICS 62 associations and inventories 93 selected-family linked questions as reusable context. Four provider roles remain distinct: nongovernmental hospitals, ambulatory providers, residential-care services and nonprofit social-assistance operators. Governmental providers, insurers and state-contracted plans remain explicit counterroles.

Six named questions cover consideration and credit loss, claim/remittance/cash matching, institutional cost reporting and settlements, member-month and resident service fees, charity-cost disclosure, and conditional social-assistance awards. Three new source records describe CMS remittance guidance, CMS T-MSIS v4 sub-capitation reporting, and FASB ASU 2010-23. The existing CMS HCRIS record and accepted revenue, credit-loss and nonprofit foundations retain stable IDs and primary metadata. Exact source locators, dates, rights and access limits accompany each answer.

CMS claim codes and cost-report data do not determine GAAP accounts or reimbursement entitlement. T-MSIS encounter/provider reporting has a July 1, 2023 compliance date; the cited v4 page states that the sub-capitation payment reporting compliance date is undetermined. Neither that page nor a Medicaid label decides insurance classification. The charity amendment is dated August 2010 and effective for fiscal years beginning after December 15, 2010; current consolidated Topic 954 remains unverified.

The original integer-cent example has five separate balanced worksheets. The hospital batch bridges $100,000 memorandum charges to $55,000 initial consideration, a $3,000 supported entitlement reduction and a separate $2,000 credit-loss allowance. Its $45,000 claim payment less a $2,000 prior-overpayment offset matches the $43,000 bank deposit. Closing net receivables are $5,000. A separate charity memorandum of $10,000 maps to $4,000 direct/indirect cost already included in the service-cost ledger; no charity revenue or associated funding is assumed.

An ambulatory provider earns one $30,000 member-month period from a three-month advance and records utilization separately. A residential operator earns $1,500 from ten delivered care days and retains its $1,000 refundable deposit. A nonprofit earns $6,000 from 300 verified meal units under explicitly incremental award conditions. A separate FY2025 hospital settlement worksheet revises its opening $5,000 receivable to a supported $4,400 notice and collects it. Cost-center reconciliation does not compute that entitlement.

Synthetic contract, policy, valuation and legal predicates are explicit. These examples are not actual claims, patient data, a revenue-cycle execution system or professional conclusions. All six assessments remain partial with unknown current authority and unestablished empirical support. No state survey or whole-industry sufficiency is claimed.

The helper is additive and stages conflicts before writing. Tests use a pinned disposable corpus, resolved assessment schemas, actual corpus validation and bundled agent search/context/get. Current integration, full checks, generated downloads and archive, independent acceptance, CI and issue closure remain separate steps. No deployment is included.
