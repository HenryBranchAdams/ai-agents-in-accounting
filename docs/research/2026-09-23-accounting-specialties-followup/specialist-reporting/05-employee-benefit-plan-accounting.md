# Area 5: Employee benefit plan accounting

## Baseline and boundary

The baseline is adjacent/partial. It includes employer benefit and fringe-tax guidance, IAS 19, historical FAS 87 and DOL reporting/disclosure material (`guide-q-benefits`, `src_roadmap_ias19_overview`, `src_1os761s`, `src_fasb_asu201707_employee_benefits`, `src_i120_fas87_dc_contributions`, `src_dol_ebsa_reporting_disclosure_guide`, `src_irs_pub15b_2026`). These sources do not themselves establish accounting for a plan as a reporting entity. Employer benefit accounting, plan financial statements, Form 5500 reporting and ERISA audit requirements are distinct.

## Findings by question

### Employer accounting and plan accounting are different reporting roles

An employer accounts for its benefits and obligations as sponsor under applicable employer GAAP and tax rules. A pension or welfare plan reports its own assets, obligations and activity under the requirements applicable to that plan as reporting entity, and files regulatory returns as required. A plan's annual Form 5500 is not a substitute for plan financial statements, and sponsor expense under ASC 715 or IAS 19 is not plan GAAP.

The 2015 FASB plan-accounting amendments (`specialist_reporting_fasb_asu2015_12`, prior package) and actual plan statements illustrate separate plan-level presentations. A 2025 church-related DB plan statement includes plan-related assets, liabilities and actuarial benefit information and refers to ASC 960 (`f2_specialist_reporting_pcusa_db_statements`). A public-company 401(k) Form 11-K illustrates a DC plan statement of net assets and changes, with investments, contribution receivables, participant loans, accrued expenses, employer and participant contributions, investment changes, benefits and expenses (`f2_specialist_reporting_monro_11k`). These are examples of actual reporting, not universal requirements for every plan type or structure.

### Populations and Form 5500 reporting

The direct 2025 Form 5500 instructions distinguish plan sizes and schedules, including Schedule H for large plans and Schedule I for certain smaller plans not filing Form 5500-SF. They describe reporting populations and the plan-year-specific schedules (`f2_specialist_reporting_5500_2025_instructions`, 2025 instructions, Schedule H/I and general filing sections). The Form 5500 is a federal annual return/report; the form's assets, liabilities, income and expense lines are regulatory reporting and should not be treated as a complete current-GAAP disclosure checklist.

For defined-contribution pension plans, the DOL's 2023 final rule changed the small/large Form 5500 reporting count to participants and beneficiaries with account balances at the beginning of the plan year, effective for plan years beginning on or after January 1, 2023 (`specialist_reporting_dol_2023_dc_count`, 88 FR 11794, pp. 11795-96, 11801-04). Other plan types use their own count rules. The 80-120 continuity rule can allow a plan at the boundary to retain prior small-plan status; check the instructions for the exact plan year and type. The count used for a Form 5500 schedule decision should not be copied as a universal participant definition for every ERISA question.

### Contributions, participant balances, investments and distributions

The actual DC plan filing gives a useful reconciliation map: payroll elections and employer match to trust receipts and participant allocations; trustee/custodian holdings and valuation reports to investment totals; participant loan activity and distributions to individual records; and administrative invoices to plan expenses (`f2_specialist_reporting_monro_11k`, statements of net assets and changes and related notes). The actual DB statement illustrates that actuary-supported accumulated benefit information and funded plan assets/liabilities may also be presented (`f2_specialist_reporting_pcusa_db_statements`). This source-backed evidence map guides a close, but actual recognition, measurement, valuation date, master-trust presentation and participant allocation must follow applicable current GAAP and plan documents.

The previously read ASU 2015-12 is a historical amendment, not current consolidated ASC 960/962/965 and not a substitute for current plan accounting. The current Codification text was not directly read in this review. The official ASC open returned no readable text; this is a tool/access limit, not evidence that the text is necessarily paywalled or unavailable. FASB taxonomy examples are illustrative and cannot establish authoritative recognition or measurement.

### Plan audit requirement, waiver and section 103(a)(3)(C) election

The DOL's 2025 instructions describe the general independent qualified public accountant report requirement and distinguish plans based on filing size and plan type (`f2_specialist_reporting_5500_2025_instructions`, audit and filing instructions). A pension plan under the small-plan threshold may qualify for the separate waiver in 29 CFR 2520.104-46 only if additional conditions are met. The regulation includes the qualifying-asset threshold (at least 95% qualifying assets) or bonding alternative for nonqualifying assets, plus participant disclosures and recordkeeping conditions (`f2_specialist_reporting_dol_small_pension_waiver`, §2520.104-46). The filing remains due; headcount alone does not establish waiver eligibility. Welfare-plan filing exemptions, one-participant plans and other plan types have different branches.

An ERISA section 103(a)(3)(C) audit election is a distinct audit branch, not an audit waiver. The current instructions describe the election, but this review did not inspect the full applicable AICPA audit standard or every prerequisite. Avoid the older “limited-scope audit” label when describing current rules. The audit approach and report depend on the election, certification eligibility, plan type and current audit standards; verify the current-year instructions and AU-C 703 requirements with the live facts.

### Defined-benefit plans

For a single-employer defined-benefit plan, the 2025 Form 5500 instructions use Schedule SB and require enrolled-actuary certification; Schedule MB applies to multiemployer defined-benefit plans (`f2_specialist_reporting_5500_2025_instructions`, Schedule SB and MB). The IRS overview explains that benefits are set by a formula rather than directly tracking investment returns and that the employer funds the benefit obligation (`specialist_reporting_benefits_db`, prior package). Those are tax/funding and filing distinctions; an actuary's funding valuation, ERISA minimum-funding rules, PBGC coverage and employer ASC 715 accounting answer different questions from plan-level ASC 960 reporting. The PCUSA 2025 statements are an example of actual DB plan-related statements, not an authoritative accounting guide.

## Research and authoring recommendations

Create separate plan-administrator and plan-auditor workflows. Keep the sponsor/employer general ledger separate from trust/custodian records and participant subledgers. Reconcile payroll remittances, employer contributions, participant elections, investment statements, benefit payments, fees, receivables and allocations; tie the year-end trial balance to the plan statements and Form 5500 schedules without treating the regulatory return as the GAAP statements. Route first by plan type, plan year, size/count basis and filing exception, then determine the audit, waiver or section 103(a)(3)(C) election branch. Require the plan document, trust/custody evidence, participant population and actuarial schedules before applying the general examples.

## Unresolved gaps

Current consolidated ASC 960/962/965 was not directly read, so current plan-level recognition, measurement, master-trust, fair-value and disclosure requirements remain open. The 2025 form instructions establish filing branches but are not GAAP. Current AICPA audit-standard text and all section 103(a)(3)(C) execution details were not inspected. Plan type, ERISA coverage, start-of-year count, asset mix, plan documents, trust arrangement, actuarial assumptions, funding status, PBGC status and amendments change the result. No general conclusion from the audited DB or SEC 11-K examples should be transferred to a different plan.
