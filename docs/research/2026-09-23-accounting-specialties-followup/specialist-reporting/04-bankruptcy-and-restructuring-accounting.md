## Findings by question

### Chapter 11 operating report and reporting period

The U.S. Trustee Program routes non-small-business and non-Subchapter V debtors to Forms 11-MOR/11-PCR, while a small-business or Subchapter V debtor generally uses Official Form 425C before plan confirmation; the district UST supplies the post-confirmation route (`specialist_reporting_ust_ch11`, applicability and forms/instructions sections; `f2_specialist_reporting_ust_mor_workflow`, general instructions and FAQs). The UST instructions state a usual monthly filing deadline of the 21st of the following month, subject to local court directions, and monthly reports continue until the plan becomes effective, the case converts or is dismissed. These are general routing rules from instructions dated December 7, 2022. For an actual debtor, confirm chapter, small-business/Subchapter V status, confirmation status, current form, local rule, district UST instruction and court order.

### Tie the operating report to books and evidence

The UST 11-MOR instructions separate the statutory report from GAAP financial statements and identify reporting responsibility, attachments and schedules. Their accounting instructions call for cash balances across the debtor's bank and investment accounts, a bank reconciliation that ties statements to debtor records and explains differences, separate prepetition and postpetition obligations, a Statement of Cash Receipts and Disbursements, a Statement of Operations, and debtor-only reporting in centralized cash management situations (`f2_specialist_reporting_ust_mor_workflow`, relevant form basis, bank reconciliation, statements and liabilities sections; PDF refs turn228view0, turn234view8-15, turn266view3-5). The schedules may mix accrual-based books with cash reporting for specific items. A case close process should keep the submitted form, supporting ledger, bank/investment statements, reconciliation, cutoff and classification decisions together. The instructions are dated 2022 and allow district/local directions to matter, so the exact current form and line instructions must be verified for the case.

### Reconcile debtor schedules and creditor claims

The official bankruptcy forms keep debtor schedules and creditor proofs of claim as distinct records. Under the 2025 Rules of Bankruptcy Procedure, Rule 3001 addresses proof-of-claim form, supporting material and signature; Rule 3003 governs Chapter 9/11 claims filing, including treatment of scheduled liabilities, the case-specific bar date and supersession by a proof filed for a scheduled claim; and Rule 3007 governs objections (`f2_specialist_reporting_bankruptcy_rules_2025`). Rule 3002 is chapter-specific and its filing deadlines should not be generalized to an ordinary Chapter 11 case. Under Rule 3003, a claim scheduled as undisputed, noncontingent and liquidated may be treated as filed unless the court orders otherwise, while a disputed, contingent, unliquidated or unscheduled claim generally requires a proof. A properly filed proof can carry prima facie effect under Rule 3001, subject to its requirements and objection.

Direct reading of the 2024 U.S. Code clarifies the statutory consequence. Under 11 U.S.C. §502(a), a proof filed under §501 is deemed allowed unless a party in interest objects; following an objection, the court determines the claim amount after notice and hearing as of the petition date and applies statutory allowance/disallowance grounds (`f2_specialist_reporting_bankruptcy_code_502_506`, §§501-502, PDF pp. 103-106). Under §506(a)(1), only an allowed claim secured by a lien on estate property or setoff is secured to the extent of the value of the creditor's interest in that property or the amount subject to setoff, with an unsecured balance for any deficiency. The statute says valuation depends on its purpose and proposed use or disposition of the property, together with the relevant hearing; a valuation made for one purpose need not settle another purpose (`f2_specialist_reporting_bankruptcy_code_502_506`, §506(a), PDF pp. 116-117).

For a reconciliation, preserve the debtor's scheduled amount/status and the creditor's asserted amount/status separately; match underlying contracts, invoices, payments, collateral and amendments; record differences, objections and court outcomes; and do not mark a claim allowed or secured from matching arithmetic alone. This resolves the general statutory path, not whether an actual claim is allowed, what priority applies, which lien is valid or what collateral value the court will adopt. Case documents, plan, bar-date order, local rules and the docket remain decisive.

### Reorganization and fresh-start accounting

EY's April 2026 technical guide states the two fresh-start eligibility criteria it attributes to ASC 852: reorganization value is below postpetition liabilities plus allowed claims, and the preconfirmation voting-interest holders lose substantive control, with existing holders receiving less than 50% of postconfirmation votes. If both criteria are met, the guide describes fresh-start accounting and assignment of reorganization value to assets and liabilities using ASC 805-20 procedures; it contrasts the nonqualifying route, where historical asset basis continues and compromised liabilities are present-valued (`specialist_reporting_ey_asc852`, April 2026 guide, PDF pp. 49-55 and 61-62). This is technical-firm interpretation, not the authoritative consolidated Codification. It provides a bounded general eligibility and allocation overview, not the proper measurement date, fair values, tax effects, disclosures or court-approved plan analysis for a specific debtor.

The Codification access attempt during this follow-up returned no readable page text. That is an observed access/tool limit only, not a finding that ASC is necessarily paywalled or unavailable. ASU 2025-12 remains only a prior search/discovery observation; no claim here relies on an unread excerpt. Current consolidated ASC 852 and related Topics remain to be directly checked before an authoritative current-GAAP conclusion.

### Liquidation basis

Directly read paragraphs in FASB ASU 2013-07 describe when liquidation is imminent, prospective transition to the liquidation basis, estimates of the cash or other consideration expected from asset disposition, separate disposal-cost presentation, continued recognition of obligations unless legally released, and statements of net assets in liquidation and changes in net assets in liquidation (`f2_specialist_reporting_asu2013_07_read`, ASC 205-30-25-1 to -3, 30-1 to -3, 35-1 and 45-1 to -2 as printed in the ASU). This is direct reading of the 2013 amendment text, not current consolidated ASC 205-30 or a full disclosure list. Apply the trigger and measurement using current Codification text and entity liquidation plans; do not equate expected disposal proceeds automatically with fair value or write off a liability without legal release.

## Unresolved gaps

The direct UST report instructions read are dated December 2022, and local UST/court practices can alter details. The current consolidated ASC 852 and ASC 205-30 were not directly read; the ASC site open returned no text, which does not establish subscription or license status. The allowance and secured-value framework in 11 U.S.C. §§501-502 and 506 was directly read in the official 2024 U.S. Code edition; verify enactments after that edition and the current Rules/order before live reliance. Priority, lien validity, claim allowance, valuation purpose, plan treatment and distribution still require the actual docket, evidence, applicable law and court determinations. Fresh-start eligibility/value and liquidation trigger also require current accounting authority and entity facts.
