# Area 3: Forensic accounting and litigation support

## Baseline and boundary

The baseline is adjacent/partial. It includes fraud and internal-control material (`guide-q-controls-fraud`, `src_1wj6irw`, `src_075usnq`, `src_0rs2kpd`, `src_05n7atb`, `src_0vf7hhg`, `src_gaogb25`, `src_cfr200grants`, `src_aa_i115_irs_household_employer`) but does not supply a complete forensic engagement or litigation-damages method. The follow-up preserves the existing practical and professional sources, and adds digital-evidence and economic-damages references. Findings below describe general methods and rules. They do not establish what happened, what is recoverable, or what a court will admit in a particular dispute.

## Findings by question

### Forensic work across an investigation, trial, and sentencing

The DOJ practice article describes forensic accountants analyzing records and databases, tracing funds and assets, applying accounting concepts to transactions, and estimating loss, restitution or forfeiture in federal prosecutions (`specialist_reporting_doj_forensic`, January 2017, pp. 45-46, section II.A). It is a government-practice article, not a generally binding engagement standard. SSFS No. 1 supplies a separate professional boundary for specified forensic services by AICPA members accepted on or after January 1, 2020 (`specialist_reporting_ssfs1`, applicability/effective-date discussion). It does not make those services a universal legal requirement.

### Evidence, records, and a reproducible trace

A useful source-to-conclusion workpaper should let another reviewer identify the data used, the transformations and matching rules applied, the excluded or unresolved items, and the arithmetic that reaches each reported amount. That is a design recommendation for reproducibility, not a universal private-practice protocol established by the sources.

For digital evidence handling, NIST IR 8387 discusses evidence acquisition and preservation, provenance and chain-of-custody documentation, hashes as integrity checks, retaining original and working copies, access control, and careful storage and transfer (`f2_specialist_reporting_nist_8387`, §§1.1, 2 and 3.2, pp. 1, 7-10). This federal technical guide supports an evidence-handling process; it is not an accounting standard, a civil discovery rule, or a substitute for a preservation order or counsel's hold instructions.

For transaction tracing, the existing IRS Criminal Investigation bank-deposits method instructs agents to distinguish taxable deposits from documented non-income receipts such as loans, gifts, transfers and return of capital, and to investigate duplicate deposits and alternative explanations (`specialist_reporting_irs_irm_methods_of_proof`, IRM 9.5.9.7.4.7 and .7.4.9-.13; .7.5-.6). The IRS method addresses criminal tax investigations and contains subsections with older revision dates. It is an example of testing alternatives, not a universal private fraud-tracing procedure. A forensic work product should label source facts, derived results, assumptions and unresolved explanations separately; the sources do not prescribe one mandatory labeling taxonomy.

### Loss, damages, and asset dissipation

The Federal Judicial Center's Fourth Edition Guide to Economic Damages describes a general comparison between the actual outcome and a supported but-for scenario, with the legally relevant measure and valuation date depending on the claim theory. For lost profits, the guide discusses support for counterfactual prices, units or market share, and costs avoided (`f2_specialist_reporting_fjc_damages`, “Standard General Approach to Quantification of Economic Damages,” printed pp. 754-763, and lost-profits discussion beginning p. 821). This is an economic-analysis framework for evaluating expert evidence, not an accounting-standard measurement or universal damages formula. Causation, mitigation, recoverability, the proper date, and the allowed loss categories require the governing law, pleadings, evidence and case orders. A trace of cash movements alone does not establish that the movements equal legally recoverable damages.

### Federal expert evidence and disclosures

The official U.S. Courts Rules of Evidence compilation dated December 1, 2024 includes Rule 702, as amended effective December 1, 2023. It requires the proponent to show, more likely than not, that specialized knowledge will help the factfinder, that the opinion rests on sufficient facts or data and reliable principles and methods, and that those methods were reliably applied (`f2_specialist_reporting_rule702_verified`, Rule 702(a)-(d), pp. 15-16). This is a federal admissibility rule, not a damages measure.

The prior specialist package's directly read December 1, 2025 Federal Rules of Civil Procedure compilation states the retained-expert disclosure and report requirements (`specialist_reporting_frcp26`, Rule 26(a)(2)(A)-(E), printed pp. 40-41). A report generally identifies opinions and their bases, facts/data considered, exhibits, qualifications/publications, prior testimony and compensation, subject to the rule's details and case-specific orders. Rules for other tribunals, privilege, discovery disputes and state evidence law remain separate. The previously listed OLRC Rule 702 page was not directly retrievable in this review; the new observation comes from the U.S. Courts PDF compilation, not from the search excerpt.

### AICPA-member forensic standard

The previously read public SSFS No. 1 is a professional standard for covered services performed by AICPA members, with the stated effective date of January 1, 2020 for engagements accepted on or after that date (`specialist_reporting_ssfs1`, opening scope and effective-date text). Confirm current text, the member's status and the service type before applying it. It is not a universal statutory standard for every accountant, expert or investigation.

## Research and authoring recommendations

Add a focused guide to evidence intake, preserved originals, transaction-to-source lineage, reconciliation, alternative explanations, assumptions and expert-report handoff. Keep the authority labels visible: technical digital-evidence guidance, IRS criminal-investigation method, professional standards, economic reference material and procedural admissibility rules answer different questions. A separate case worksheet should capture governing legal theory, measure of damages, valuation date, mitigation and recoverability only after counsel identifies the relevant legal questions.

## Unresolved gaps

No general private forensic-accounting protocol or universal fraud-tracing standard was established. No state-court, arbitration or criminal-procedure survey was completed. The FJC guide is nonbinding and cannot determine the measure in a specific case. Actual evidence preservation depends on systems, custodians, applicable hold/discovery duties and court directions; actual damages depend on facts, causation, legal theory and governing law. The 2017 DOJ article and Rule 702 compilation should be rechecked against the tribunal and current procedural materials for a live matter.
