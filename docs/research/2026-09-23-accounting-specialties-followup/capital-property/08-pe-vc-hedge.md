# 8. PE, VC and hedge funds: security valuation and equalization

Research date: September 23, 2026 (America/Chicago). US private-investment and hedge-fund reporting focus. This supplement extends the prior brief at `../../2026-09-22-accounting-specialties/capital-property/08-pe-vc-hedge.md`. ## Baseline and valuation evidence

The inclusive baseline contains shared investment, custody, consolidation and valuation materials rather than a dedicated PE/VC/hedge guide. Stable IDs and the prior source-key inventory are in `areas.json` and the prior package. Existing baseline work supports the broad facts that investment-company funds generally report investments at fair value and that registered-fund Rule 2a-5 obligations are a distinct branch. It did not resolve the hard question of calibrating an illiquid security after a financing transaction.

A BDO article summarizing a 2018 AICPA Financial Reporting Executive Committee working draft supplies a useful, explicitly nonauthoritative decision framework. Determine whether market participants would transact in the debt and equity together or as separate units of account; compare the observed transaction to the unit actually measured. Calibrate valuation techniques to the initial transaction price and later orderly company/security transactions, then update for facts known or knowable at the measurement date, including operating performance, milestones, current comparable-company inputs, and changes in financing or exit prospects. A transaction price is an anchor for analysis, not an automatic current fair value if rights, security class, market-participant assumptions, or circumstances differ.

For a complex capital structure, the article identifies scenario-based methods, option-pricing models, current-value methods and hybrids. Method selection depends on the preference stack, conversion and participation rights, anticipated exit timing, volatility/uncertainty and control assumptions. A model should allocate total equity value among the actual classes under market-participant assumptions, and the valuation file should show why the selected method best captures their payoff differences and how calibration was updated. The article reports working-draft practice, not authoritative fair-value guidance; the AICPA working draft itself was not consulted here. [BDO, “Valuation of Portfolio Company Investments – Important Reminders,” `f2_capital_property_bdo_valuation`](https://www.bdo.com/insights/assurance/valuation-of-portfolio-company-investments-important-reminders), “Unit of account and assumed transactions,” “Calibration,” and “Complex capital structure and allocation methods.”

This closes the general-method gap enough to guide a future worked example, but not the valuation conclusion. A guide should show (a) a new-round price with a different seniority or conversion feature, (b) a bridge from total enterprise/equity value to each class, (c) calibration to entry price and later measurement-date facts, and (d) sensitivity to exit scenarios. A recent round with rights unlike the measured security cannot be copied as its fair value without the unit-of-account and payoff analysis. IPEV materials remain excluded under the prior access/rights boundary; this supplement does not reproduce or rely on them. Current ASC 820 text, actual cap table, transaction rights and measurement-date evidence remain necessary.

## Hedge equalization: cohort history matters

K&L Gates’ practitioner presentation distinguishes three approaches. Investor capital accounts track each investor’s own history; series accounting issues separate share series at different NAVs/HWMs; equalization keeps one share class/series but requires investor-specific credits or deficits to prevent newcomers from paying incentive fees on appreciation that preceded their entry. The presentation also discusses fee reversals when NAV falls, reinforcing that accrued incentive-fee balances need to track subsequent losses and crystallization terms. It is practical commentary, not a mandatory accounting policy. [K&L Gates, “Understanding Hedge Fund Fees: Implications for Hedge Fund Managers,” `f2_capital_property_klgates_equalization`](https://www.klgates.com/Understanding-Hedge-Fund-Fees-Implications-for-Hedge-Fund-Managers-03-09-2020), slides 16–18, 19–26, 28–29.

The Irish Funds historical 2009 Irish administrator guide, which is not US authority or a current U.S. standard, lays out equalization-credit/depreciation-deposit, contingent-liquidation and share-series methods. Its example has the fund’s HWM at $100, a new subscription at GAV $105, 1,000 shares and a 20% fee. The subscriber’s $5/share pre-entry appreciation implies a $1,000 equalization credit. To show why the credit exists, take a separate simple arithmetic step from those inputs: if GAV later rises to $107, that investor’s $2 post-entry gain produces $400 at 20%, while pooled HWM accrual on $7 would be $1,400; the $1,000 credit captures the pre-entry portion. This $107 comparison is our arithmetic illustration, not another printed guide scenario, and it ignores other subscriptions, redemptions, expenses, valuation changes, crystallization, and method-specific adjustments. Equalization credits may settle through additional shares or cash; deposits may be segregated and refunded/applied as specified; contingent methods may settle at redemption. Series methods keep the investor’s cohort history distinct. The investor agreement and administrator method decide the actual operation. [Irish Funds, `f2_capital_property_irishfunds_equalization`](https://www.irishfunds.ie/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Guidance-on-Sound-Practices-for-Hedge-Fund-Administrators.pdf), Appendix 3, pp. 45–49.

These approaches explain HWM and new-subscriber fee mechanics, but they do not establish a universal method for PE/VC funds or closed-end partnerships. Nor does a hedge administrator’s share-based method itself answer ASC 946’s capital-transaction or class-allocation entries. A hedge close needs a daily or dealing-date bridge from beginning NAV through investment P&L, fee accrual/reversal, subscriptions/redemptions, class/series changes, side-pocket participation, and ending investor NAV. The prior ASC 946 KPMG sources provide illustrative financial-statement presentation; actual PPM/LPA terms and the administrator’s data are needed to select the method and calculate amounts. [Prior sources `cp_kpmg_hedge_illustrative_2025` and `cp_kpmg_investment_companies_2026`](../../2026-09-22-accounting-specialties/capital-property/sources.json).

## Other baseline questions and guide additions

Prior evidence remains useful for capital calls, unfunded commitments, investor notices, waterfall/preferred-return/catch-up/clawback models, custody and the 2023 private-fund-rule vacatur, ASC 946 investor-class reporting, and joint-venture formation.
