# 2. Fund accounting: portfolio, class capital, and manager carry

Research date: September 23, 2026 (America/Chicago). US fund and manager reporting focus. This supplement extends the prior area brief at `../../2026-09-22-accounting-specialties/capital-property/02-fund-accounting.md`; it does not establish treatment for any fund, adviser, GP, or portfolio company.

## Baseline and entity boundary

The screening baseline includes investment, valuation, custody, family-office, and holding-company materials, but no adequacy conclusion. Stable record IDs and all prior source keys remain in this package’s `areas.json` and the prior capital-property package. The main control is to separate reporting entities: the ASC 946 fund reports portfolio investments and liabilities, net assets and investor-class capital activity; a GP reports its own interest and obligations; a management company reports contract revenue and costs; portfolio companies report their own assets, debt and operations. Consolidation, ownership, the investment-company exception and legal title must be tested for the actual structure. A fund-level carry allocation is not automatically manager service revenue, and manager carry classification does not itself determine what the fund allocates to each investor class. [Prior KPMG investment-company handbook, `cp_kpmg_investment_companies_2026`](../../2026-09-22-accounting-specialties/capital-property/sources.json); [Treasury §1.743-1, `f2_capital_property_743_reg`](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2025-title26-vol10/pdf/CFR-2025-title26-vol10-sec1-743-1.pdf), §1.743-1(j), illustrates the distinct basis and reporting entity boundary for partnership interests.

A close architecture should keep separate, reconcilable schedules for (1) portfolio investment and cash activity, including valuation support; (2) each class’s opening capital, subscriptions, allocations, distributions and ending NAV; (3) GP/manager receivables, fee income, carry investments or ownership interests under the selected accounting; and (4) each portfolio company’s books. This is a control design, not a prescribed GAAP form. The fund agreement, allocation method, management and advisory contracts, side letters, control rights, and consolidation analysis govern the actual ledger.

## Manager carry: service revenue and ownership models are different analyses

The material new finding is an authority-level distinction. The January 2020 FASB staff Q&A states staff’s view that incentive-based capital allocations such as carried interest fall within Topic 606 when they compensate asset-management services, and that delivering the performance compensation as cash or a capital allocation does not, by itself, change that conclusion. It explains this as consistent with the Board’s decision-maker fee model. This is staff implementation material, not a Board amendment or authoritative Codification text. [FASB, Revenue Recognition Implementation Q&As, `f2_capital_property_fasb_revenue_qa`](https://www.fasb.org/page/document?pdf=Revenue_Recognition_Implementation_QAs.pdf), Q3, pp. 15–16.

KPMG’s 2025 revenue handbook recounts the April 2016 TRG discussion: FASB members present said performance fees in carried-interest form were intended in Topic 606; the SEC Observer said 606 would be acceptable, while an ownership approach could be acceptable on specific facts if the entity fully applied consolidation, equity-method, or other relevant ownership guidance. KPMG’s own current interpretation is that private and public entities may consistently elect between those models, and that voluntarily moving away from Topic 606 after applying it is generally difficult to support. If the ownership model is used, KPMG says related equity income is presented separately from Topic 606 revenue. Those are KPMG’s interpretations of the 2016 staff comments, not an SEC rule or FASB Board conclusion. The newer handbook’s date must not be misread as a later Board reversal of the 2020 staff Q&A: its ownership alternative rests on the separate, earlier SEC Observer caveat. [KPMG, 2025 Handbook: Revenue recognition, `f2_capital_property_kpmg_revenue_2025`](https://kpmg.com/kpmg-us/content/dam/kpmg/frv/pdf/2025/revenue-recognition.pdf), Q2.3.80, PDF pp. 40–41.

Deloitte’s interpretive roadmap likewise says that no explicit Topic 606 scope guidance addresses carried interest that is an equity interest; it considers a Topic 323/ownership approach possible when facts support that the allocation is an attribute of equity, while a separate management fee remains a customer-service arrangement. Ares Management’s 2018 SEC filing is an example of a registrant treating carried-interest capital allocations and uncollected carry under ASC 323, separately from ASC 606 incentive fees. Neither source makes ASC 323 a default or settles the accounting for a new entity. [Deloitte DART, `f2_capital_property_deloitte_carry_323`](https://dart.deloitte.com/USDART/home/codification/broad-transactions/asc323-10/roadmap-equity-method-joint-ventures/chapter-5-other-considerations/5-1-equity-method-investments/5-1-2-capital-allocation-based-arrangements), §5.1.2.2; [Ares 2018 Form 10-Q, `f2_capital_property_ares_2018_10q`](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1176948/000117694818000035/ares-20180930x10q.htm), Note R10.

This evidence supports a bounded decision path, not one universal answer: first identify the reporting entity and whether carry is compensation for services or an attribute of an ownership interest; then analyze the contract’s performance condition, hurdle, clawback, vesting, fund and manager control, consolidation, and equity-method implications; document the accepted policy and consistent application; separate manager fee/carry accounting from fund-level class allocation. A manager revenue or receivable amount cannot be computed without the contract, crystallization and reversal facts, and selected accounting policy. Current Codification text and reporting-entity-specific review remain necessary.

## NAV, fees, commitments and equalization

The prior KPMG ASC 946 interpretation addresses fund statements, class capital allocations, and presentation of unrealized appreciation/depreciation, management fees, carried interest and clawback. It should be applied only to the fund’s reporting entity; manager revenue is separate. Prior ILPA materials standardize capital-call/distribution notice elements and show one bracketed waterfall model, but do not create universal call terms, waterfall order, or clawback rights. Custody, audit, delivery and adviser duties also remain a separate regulatory branch: prior SEC custody-rule, vacatur and Form PF materials must be checked against adviser status, custody, pool type and current litigation or rulemaking. Do not infer a custody conclusion from NAV accounting. [Prior source keys `cp_kpmg_investment_companies_2026`, `cp_ilpa`, `cp_ilpa_model_lpa_deal_by_deal`, `cp_sec_custody`, `cp_sec_vacatur`, and `cp_sec_formpf`](../../2026-09-22-accounting-specialties/capital-property/sources.json).

The equalization evidence provides actual method alternatives for hedge structures, not a universal private-fund subscription formula. K&L Gates compares investor capital accounts, series accounting and equalization; equalization must retain issuance NAV/high-water-mark history and compensate for accrued performance fees to avoid charging a new investor for pre-entry gains. The Irish Funds source is a historical 2009 Irish administrator-practice guide, not US authority or a current U.S. standard; it describes share-series, equalization-credit/depreciation-deposit, and contingent-liquidation methods. Its worked example issues 1,000 shares at GAV $105 when HWM is $100 at a 20% incentive fee, producing a $1,000 equalization credit. As a simple arithmetic illustration derived from those inputs, if GAV later reaches $107 and no other changes occur, the new investor’s post-entry appreciation fee would be $400 (20% × 1,000 × $2), while pooled HWM accrual would be $1,400 (20% × 1,000 × $7); the prior $1,000 equalization credit offsets the pre-entry component. This calculation is derived, not an additional scenario printed by the guide. The method’s settlement, share creation, cash/deposit use, and fee crystallization differ by contract. It does not prove how a private partnership should book subscriptions or ASC 946 class transactions. [K&L Gates, `f2_capital_property_klgates_equalization`](https://www.klgates.com/Understanding-Hedge-Fund-Fees-Implications-for-Hedge-Fund-Managers-03-09-2020); [Irish Funds, `f2_capital_property_irishfunds_equalization`](https://www.irishfunds.ie/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Guidance-on-Sound-Practices-for-Hedge-Fund-Administrators.pdf), Appendix 3, pp. 45–49.

## Guide additions and remaining gaps

Add a reporting-entity map and a paired ledger illustration: fund-level class allocation versus manager-level carry accounting, showing a Topic 606 route and an ownership route without implying that both may be chosen freely in every case. Add a hedge equalization comparison with investor entry NAV/HWM, accrued fee, subsequent performance, redemption, and the governing method. Keep commitment notices, waterfall rights, ASC 946 close entries, SEC custody/audit duties, and manager revenue as separate workstreams.

No partnership or advisory agreements, fee schedules, allocation ledgers, investor subscription records, side letters, consolidation analyses or current Codification passages were examined. Accordingly, the sources support the general reporting-entity distinction, staff/interpretive carry routes, and equalization method mechanics, but not a specific policy election, revenue amount, fund entry, audit obligation, or private-fund equalization journal entry. The cited KPMG handbook and other practice sources are copyrighted interpretive material; use original summaries only. Research the exact current ASC and entity facts for application.
