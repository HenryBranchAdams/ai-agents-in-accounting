## Rent, lease incentives and tenant deposits

For a lessor operating lease, BDO’s January 2026 ASC 842 blueprint summarizes payments as generally recognized on a straight-line basis over the lease term when collection is probable. Variable lease payments are recognized when the relevant event/fact occurs, or when collected if collectibility is not probable under the guidance described. The lessor retains the underlying property and depreciation; its balance sheet may include rent receivable or deferred rent reflecting the difference between straight-line lease income and cash received. This is professional interpretation, not Codification text; classify the lease, collectibility, incentives, concessions and any separate service components from actual terms. [BDO, *Blueprint: Accounting for Leases Under ASC 842*, `f2_capital_property_bdo_leases_2026`](https://arch.bdo.com/getContentAsset/9f7428e4-09d3-4ee2-a530-b026eb0bc16e/bb620d56-5e9c-4774-8d17-fb9323eefdf4/Accounting-for-Leases-Under-ASC-842-BDO-Blueprint-01-2026.pdf?language=en), §6.6, p. 11; §8.2.4.1, p. 273.

Deloitte’s lease roadmap distinguishes refundable deposits from nonrefundable deposits. A refundable amount is not a lease payment while the tenant can recover it; applying it to rent arrears settles the unpaid lease amount. Retaining it for damage or breach is generally analyzed as a variable lease payment. A nonrefundable amount is treated as fixed consideration in the explanation. IRS Publication 527 adds a separate tax rule: a refundable security deposit generally is not rental income when received, but amounts kept become income; advance rent is included when received for tax even if it covers a later period. Thus a book liability and a tax inclusion can occur on different bases and dates. The actual lease, reason for retention, applicable state/local law and lessor accounting drive application. [Deloitte DART, `f2_capital_property_deloitte_security_deposits`](https://dart.deloitte.com/USDART/home/codification/broad-transactions/asc842-10/roadmap-leasing/chapter-6-lease-payments/6-1-general), §§6.1.2.1–.2; [IRS Publication 527 (2025), prior source `cp_irs_527`](../../2026-09-22-accounting-specialties/capital-property/sources.json), “Rental Income.”

This bounded evidence extends, but does not replace, prior CAM/recovery research. Common-area-maintenance and other reimbursements may be lease or nonlease components and need allocation/true-up under the applicable policy. Do not net collections into rent without examining the performance obligation, lease election, billing clause and expenditure reconciliation. The rent roll should separately carry contractual rent, straight-line adjustments, variable rent, tenant concessions/incentives, deposits and liabilities, recoveries/CAM, taxes, and billed/collected/receivable amounts.

## Property manager collections and owner settlement

The prior KPMG source addresses ASC 606 recognition for property-manager fees and variable fee consideration, but revenue guidance alone does not determine whether a manager controls tenant cash, acts as agent, must use a trust account, or when the owner is entitled to settlement. One concrete state example is California: the 2026 DRE compilation requires a licensed broker handling others’ funds to keep chronological receipt/disbursement records and a daily balance; a separate beneficiary/transaction record; and a monthly reconciliation of those separate records to the broker’s control record, including each beneficiary liability. It generally requires placing third-party funds with the owner, a neutral escrow depository, or a broker trust account within three business days. Before a disbursement would reduce the account below the aggregate trust liability, every fund owner’s written consent is required. These are California broker requirements, not a national rent-remittance formula or a rule for every property manager. As an editorial owner-settlement control, reconcile per property and period: opening operating and deposit cash; tenant rent and recoveries collected; refundable deposit liability and tenant-level balance; authorized property/repair/tax/utility disbursements; manager fees and approved reimbursements; owner remittance; ending cash, reserve and aged payables/receivables. Tie each movement to bank activity, rent roll, invoices, approval rights and lease/management terms; check whether separate statutory trust accounting applies. [California DRE, 2026 Real Estate Law, `f2_capital_property_ca_dre_trustregs_2026`](https://dre.ca.gov/files/pdf/relaw/regs.pdf), §§2831–2832.1; [prior KPMG, `cp_kpmg_real_estate_revenue_2026`](../../2026-09-22-accounting-specialties/capital-property/sources.json).

## Federal REIT qualification route

The Code sections inspected provide the high-level annual qualification map. Section 856 describes the required domestic entity and share/beneficial-interest characteristics, a 100-beneficial-owner test, the closely-held limitation, at least 95% and 75% gross-income tests, and quarterly asset-diversification tests including the broad 75%-of-assets real-estate/cash/government-securities category and limits on single-issuer exposure. Section 857(a) generally conditions the dividends-paid deduction on distributions equal to at least 90% of REIT taxable income computed with specified adjustments and excluding net capital gain. These are thresholds to screen and calendar, not a full return algorithm. Definitions, attribution and look-through, taxable REIT subsidiaries, hedges, foreclosure property, prohibited transactions, cure provisions and distributable amounts require detailed statutory/regulatory analysis and current-year instructions. [26 U.S.C. §§856–857, `f2_capital_property_uscode_reit`](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/26/856); [2025 IRS Form 1120-REIT Instructions, prior source `cp_irs_reit`](../../2026-09-22-accounting-specialties/capital-property/sources.json).

The inspected U.S. Code display did not state its consolidation-through date, while the IRS instructions are for tax year 2025. Verify amendments and regulations applicable to the REIT year, entity election, ownership and income/assets schedules before relying on any threshold. This research supports a general checklist but cannot conclude that a company qualifies.

The California regulation is a state-specific broker rule and the DRE 2026 compilation is stated as of January 1, 2026; it is not a nationwide property-manager duty. The deposit interpretation and BDO lease material are secondary accounting commentary; IRS Publication 527 is a tax source. The federal Code display date and complete REIT exceptions require verification. This package supports general accounting/tax routes but leaves property ownership, agency, cash custody, lease components, legal deposit handling, current ASC language and REIT qualification dependent on the entity, contract, jurisdiction, and year.
