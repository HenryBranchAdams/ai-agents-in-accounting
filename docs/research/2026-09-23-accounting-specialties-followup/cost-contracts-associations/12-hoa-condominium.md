# Area 12: HOAs and condominiums

This supplement adds current Florida condominium statute text and sharpens the distinction between state-law collection/reserve controls and financial-statement recognition. The direct statutes reviewed are for Florida condominiums under Chapter 718. They do not establish rules for other states or for every HOA under Florida Chapter 720. The group remains a national common-interest-real-estate-association research area, and legal facts vary by declaration, bylaws, statute and entity.

## Assessment revenue: an unresolved interpretive disagreement

FASB’s June 2024 CIRA agenda handout describes Topic 972’s role in fund presentation, common-property recognition and future major repair/replacement disclosures, and summarizes stakeholder questions about assessment recognition, liabilities and fund reporting. An agenda request or handout is not a Board decision or an amendment to GAAP (`cca_hoa-fasb`, pp. 6-7, issues A-D; [FASB handout](https://storage.fasb.org/BMHO20240612.pdf)).

Two practitioner positions point in different directions. DesRoches & Company argues that regular assessments should be recognized in the period assessed and that replacement assessments do not automatically become Topic 606 contract liabilities when the board can retain discretion to use funds for broader association purposes. The article describes a competing PPC Guide view under which replacement assessments remain contract liabilities until related future maintenance costs are incurred. The DesRoches page does not date its article, and the PPC Guide itself was not independently opened; treat the latter as a secondhand characterization, not primary evidence. Neither practitioner article settles the current Codification or the facts of a particular declaration (`cca_hoa_specialist_revenue`; [DesRoches practitioner discussion](https://www.desrochescpas.com/revenuerecognition)).

For a real association, the analysis should identify who has enforceable rights, what the assessment promises, whether funds can be redirected or refunded, any specific obligation to perform or maintain property, applicable owner approval, when the assessment becomes legally due, and whether payment precedes promised service. Keep Topic 972 fund presentation, Topic 606 customer-contract analysis, member equity/fund balance, cash restrictions, and legal authorization as distinct questions. The current ASC text was not extractable in the research browser; that is an access observation only, not evidence that the Codification is paywalled or unavailable by license.

## Operating and replacement funds, reserves, and repairs

A 2018 HOACPA article quotes ASC 972-205-45-8/-9 and 972-720-25 to explain replacement-fund assets, liabilities and fund balance, operating-fund activity, and how major repair/replacement costs may be charged to designated fund(s) when fund accounting is used. Its explanation is a dated practitioner interpretation, not a direct read of current ASC text; its broader characterization that GAAP is “fairly silent” is the author’s view. Florida law separately requires reserve amounts to be accounted for and permits reserve cash to be commingled with operating accounts only for investment, with separate accounting and a required minimum balance. These legal controls are not themselves a conclusion about GAAP fund-balance classification or bank-account presentation (`cca_hoa_specialist_reserve`; `f2_cost_florida_718111_2026`; [HOACPA reserve discussion](https://hoacpa.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/FAQ-Can-Reserve-Monies-be-used-for-Capital-Improvements-and-Other-Items-not-included-in-Reserve-Study.html); [Florida Statutes § 718.111](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/718.111)).

Florida’s 2026 condominium statute requires reserve calculations, including useful lives and replacement costs/deferred maintenance and the recommendations of a structural integrity reserve study for covered components and associations. It limits reserve waivers or use in defined circumstances and identifies special assessments, loans or credit as possible funding mechanisms for certain covered structural items. The statute also provides notice rules for nonemergency special-assessment meetings. In the provisions reviewed, the majority vote applies to selected structural-integrity funding decisions, not every special assessment. A reserve study, required contribution, vote, notice or project schedule supplies legal/planning evidence; none alone establishes a recognized liability for all future repairs or dictates whether work is capitalized (`f2_cost_florida_718112_2026`, § 718.112(2)(b), (f)-(g); [2026 Florida Statutes § 718.112](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/718.112)).

Section 718.111 also prescribes Florida condominium financial reporting tiers by annual revenues: compiled statements for $150,000-$299,999; reviewed statements for $300,000-$499,999; audited statements at $500,000 or more; and, below $150,000, a cash-receipts-and-expenditures report, subject to statutory alternatives. These thresholds and any member-vote alternatives are jurisdiction-specific statutory duties. They are not universal HOA thresholds or a substitute for identifying the GAAP framework and applicable reporting requirements (`f2_cost_florida_718111_2026`, § 718.111(13)).

## Special assessments and developer transition

For a special assessment funding a major project, distinguish (1) board or owner authority to levy and collect, (2) the date enforceable rights and payment terms arise, (3) any promise to provide services or carry out a specified project, (4) assessment cash received before performance, (5) restricted or designated reserve accounting, and (6) whether project costs create an asset, expense or liability. Florida § 718.112 supplies some approval, reserve-use and meeting-notice conditions for covered condominium situations. It does not settle recognition timing under Topic 972/606 or the capitalization and cutoff of project costs. The competing practitioner positions described above should stay visible until current authoritative paragraphs and the association’s actual documents/facts are reviewed.

For turnover, Florida § 718.301 requires delivery of financial records and source documents from incorporation through turnover, together with association property, funds and enumerated records. It requires an independent CPA audit of the incorporation-to-turnover period, or the period since the most recent annual audit where annual audits were performed; it specifies GAAP financial statements and GAAS audit. This establishes a source-data and statutory audit boundary, not that the carried balances are correct or that the incoming board has a prescribed opening-balance method. Reconcile cash and reserve ledgers, assessment receivables/aging, prepaid assessments, payables, debt, contracts, property title, capital assets, warranties, litigation/commitments and cutoff to underlying records before accepting opening balances (`f2_cost_florida_718301_2026`, § 718.301(4); [Florida Statutes § 718.301](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/718.301)).

The previously supported federal tax branch remains separate: a valid § 528 election and Form 1120-H tax computation do not determine GAAP assessment revenue or reserve-fund presentation (`cca_hoa-irs1120h`; `cca_hoa-irs557`; [IRS Instructions for Form 1120-H](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1120h); [IRS Publication 557](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p557)).

## Remaining boundaries

Current ASC 972/606 paragraphs and the PPC source have not been directly verified. The DesRoches/HOACPA pages are public practitioner interpretations with unknown reuse rights; no reuse permission is inferred. Florida condominium statutes were directly read as current 2026 sources, but no Chapter 720 HOA statutes, other-state laws, declaration/bylaw clauses, association ledgers, reserve studies or turnover records were examined. No universal answer is offered for recognition of special assessments, future replacement funding, opening balances or statutory reporting outside the Florida condominium scope.
