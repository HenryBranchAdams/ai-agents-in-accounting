# Area 7: Cost and managerial accounting

This supplement adds an October 2025 U.S. GAAP interpretation of normal-capacity allocation, a practitioner discussion of ABC alongside standard costing, and an original avoidable-cost example. These methods answer different questions: management costing describes how resources are consumed and decisions change; external inventory accounting determines what cost can remain in inventory; federal contract rules determine what cost can be claimed under a particular award.

## Cost objects and drivers

The prior OpenStax reading distinguishes job costing for separately identifiable work from process costing for homogeneous continuous output, with hybrid systems possible. It remains educational rather than reporting authority. The IMA article adds a useful driver-selection lens: a single broad volume base can misstate product, service, or customer profitability when different activities consume support resources unevenly. ABC groups resource costs into activity pools and assigns them with drivers that reflect consumption; standard rates can still support routine control and valuation when periodically reconciled. Use ABC where better causal attribution is worth its measurement cost, not as a requirement to allocate every common cost to every product. The IMA article was actually read in the earlier browser step, but its old URL now redirects to a general IMA page, so treat it as dated practitioner discussion rather than current standing guidance.

Sources: [cca_openstax_managerial](https://openstax.org/books/principles-managerial-accounting/pages/4-1-distinguish-between-job-order-costing-and-process-costing) in the prior package; new `f2_cost_ima_standard_abc_2020`, *Standard Costing and ABC: A Coexistence*, May 2020, [original article URL](https://www.sfmagazine.com/Articles/2020/May/Standard-Costing-and-ABC-A-Coexistence), sections on volume overhead, activity pools/drivers, and standard-cost coexistence (previous direct read, web ref `turn194view4`, lines 379-416).

## Production overhead, idle capacity, and waste

KPMG's October 2025 U.S. GAAP handbook reproduces and interprets ASC 330-10-30-3 through 30-7. Variable production overhead follows actual facility use. Fixed production overhead uses normal capacity, defined across multiple periods or seasons under normal circumstances and including expected variation such as planned maintenance. The entity must determine its own normal range and judge when output is abnormally low.

KPMG's example has $700,000 monthly fixed production overhead and a 1,000-ton normal level. At 600 tons during unplanned downtime, the unit allocation stays at $700: $420,000 enters inventory and $280,000 is expensed. At 2,000 tons, allocation falls to $350 per ton to prevent inventory from exceeding cost. Unallocated fixed overhead from abnormally low output or an idle plant, unanticipated spoilage, and abnormal freight/handling are period expense. Expected spoilage inherent in the process is part of production cost. This answers the general accounting interpretation; it does not set an entity's normal-capacity range or decide contract allowability.

Source: new `f2_cost_kpmg_inventory_2025`, *Handbook: Inventory*, October 2025, [KPMG PDF](https://kpmg.com/kpmg-us/content/dam/kpmg/frv/pdf/2025/inventory.pdf), pp. 29-30 and 37-38, Questions 3.2.110/120 and Example 3.2.05; web refs `turn250view0`, `turn252view2`. It is professional interpretation reproducing ASC excerpts, not the authoritative Codification. ## Standard-cost reconciliation

KPMG quotes ASC 330-10-30-12: standard costs are acceptable only when adjusted at reasonable intervals so they reasonably approximate a recognized cost basis at the balance-sheet date. Thus a standard-cost variance report is a management control, not automatically the inventory entry. At close, reconcile standards to actual conditions and adjust inventory and cost of sales under the entity's selected, consistently applied cost method. Separate ordinary expected production loss from abnormal waste and unallocated idle-capacity cost; the latter may be a current-period expense even when management tracks it as an overhead variance.

Contract-cost calculation adds another separate reconciliation. The prior [cca_far31](https://www.acquisition.gov/far/part-31) source (FAR 31.201-1 and 31.201-2) includes applicable variance adjustment when standard cost is used for contract total cost, alongside allowability, allocability, records, and award limits. Do not move a management variance into inventory or a government claim solely because it is labeled “overhead.” Actual contract rules still depend on the incorporated clause, CAS status, and award.

Sources: `f2_cost_kpmg_inventory_2025`, p. 47 (ASC 330-10-30-12 excerpt); prior [cca_far31](https://www.acquisition.gov/far/part-31), [FAR Part 31](https://www.acquisition.gov/far/part-31), §§31.201-1 to 31.203.

## Avoidable fixed costs and common allocations: a worked decision

The following is an original, illustrative service-line example, not an external reporting method or source-provided company result.

| Service line for the period | Amount |
| --- | ---: |
| Revenue | $1,000,000 |
| Variable costs | (620,000) |
| Contribution margin | $380,000 |
| Traceable fixed cost | (300,000) |
| Of that fixed cost, avoidable if closed now | $120,000 |
| Allocated common facility cost | (60,000) |
| Reported fully allocated profit | $20,000 |

The service line appears only modestly profitable after both fixed-cost layers, but closing it loses $380,000 of contribution and saves only $120,000 of traceable fixed cost. Assuming the remaining $180,000 of traceable fixed expense and $60,000 common cost continue, company operating income falls by $260,000. The allocation did not cause the common cost and its disappearance is not a saving. A decision can change if closure cost, demand transferred to other capacity, a capacity constraint, or a longer planning horizon makes additional costs avoidable. The practical question is which future resources actually change under each alternative; an ABC label by itself does not answer that question.

The IMA article supports examining resource consumption and incremental cost rather than treating broad allocated margin as decisive. The arithmetic and assumptions above are constructed for this supplement; actual customer or segment profitability requires entity data, policy horizon, capacity facts, and any external-reporting allocation requirements.

## Remaining boundary

The new handbook supports a dated general explanation of normal-capacity treatment and standard-cost approximation. Current Codification paragraphs were not independently extractable in this session. The IMA article is from 2020 and its current publisher URL redirects. Cost-object choice, drivers, normal capacity, unused capacity, and avoidability remain context-dependent; managerial costs, GAAP inventory cost, and FAR allowable cost must remain separate in any later guide.
