# Area 10: Government contractors

This supplement expands the direct labor and final indirect-cost evidence and gives a source-backed bridge from contract cost records to GAAP revenue and contract balances. Cost allowability, contract billing, and financial-reporting revenue are separate calculations; the same job-cost ledger can feed them without making them the same measurement.

## Labor timekeeping: requirements and DCAA practice guidance

The prior package correctly recorded the DCAA pamphlet as discovery-only based on the access available then; I now directly read the official source and its relevant sections. DCAA Manual No. 7641.90, dated June 26, 2012, is a 99-page PDF; this review inspected pp. 13-15. The manual describes system criteria for cost accumulation by contract/job, logical indirect allocations, general-ledger control and reconciliation, labor timekeeping by intermediate/final cost objective, and labor distribution. The timekeeping pages discuss employee daily entry to actual work objectives, correction trails that preserve original and corrected time with employee concurrence, recording all hours worked including uncompensated overtime, and employee certification. The manual identifies supervisor cosignature as recommended.

That last distinction matters. The manual expressly says its examples are not uniform guides and that it is not a substitute for applicable rules and regulations. Its recommendations and auditor expectations are useful controls but are not independently binding contract text. Binding obligations come from applicable FAR/DFARS clauses, CAS where applicable, and the exact executed award. For example, the prior [cca_dfars_2522427006](https://www.acquisition.gov/node/36757/printable/pdf) source covers the DoD accounting-system clause when incorporated; the coordinator directly read FAR 52.216-7 and records it as `f2_coordinator_far2167` in the follow-up `coordinator-sources.json`. The clause's exact award version and alternatives govern.

Source: new [f2_cost_dcaa_contractor_pamphlet_2012](https://www.dcaa.mil/Portals/88/Information%20for%20Contractor%27s%20Pamphlet%20%28DCAAM%20764190%29%20%28508%20C%29.pdf), *Information for Contractors*, [official DCAA PDF](https://www.dcaa.mil/Portals/88/Information%20for%20Contractor%27s%20Pamphlet%20%28DCAAM%20764190%29%20%28508%20C%29.pdf), DCAA Manual No. 7641.90, dated June 26, 2012, pp. 13-15. DCAA describes this manual as unlimited public release. It is guidance, not an amendment to FAR/DFARS.

## Cost-to-GAAP bridge

A government contractor can maintain one actual-cost subledger by contract, task, or other cost objective while preserving separate attributes for cost type, direct/indirect treatment, accounting pool/base, source record, award terms, allowable/unallowable status, provisional billing rate, and amount billed. The subledger supports FAR allowability and contract billing; it does not itself determine Topic 606 performance, revenue, or contract-balance presentation.

KPMG's December 2025 Topic 606 example shows why. For a $5.0 million refurbishment contract with $4.0 million expected cost, $1.5 million of procured elevators would overstate progress if included in the cost-to-cost measure. The contractor excludes those costs from its progress measure and transaction-price base, recognizes the elevator transfer at zero margin, and measures other progress using $0.5 million incurred against $2.5 million expected other cost. That is 20 percent progress, yielding $0.7 million revenue on the adjusted $3.5 million price plus $1.5 million elevator revenue, or $2.2 million total revenue. Costs recognized are $1.5 million for elevators plus $0.5 million of other cost, or $2.0 million. A job-cost system may capture the $2.0 million, but it still needs a Topic 606 performance-obligation and measure-of-progress judgment; a FAR allowability review is a separate step.

The contract terms, enforceability and conditionality of the contractor’s rights, and the performance transferred determine the balance-sheet classification; invoicing alone is not decisive. As a constructed overlay on the KPMG example, assume the contractor has an unconditional right to $1.9 million and the remaining $0.3 million right to consideration is conditional on a later performance certificate. The unconditional right is a receivable and the conditional right associated with performance already transferred is a contract asset. If only passage of time remains before payment on that $0.3 million, it is a receivable, including if unbilled. Amounts received or unconditionally due before the related performance create a contract liability. Contract assets and liabilities for a single contract are presented net; unconditional receivables remain separately identified. For an over-time performance obligation in which the customer controls the asset as it is created, KPMG says the contractor does not book a separate WIP asset as a substitute for the contract asset or receivable.

A separate rate-settlement example shows the calculation without deciding the Topic 606 result: on a $1,000,000 allocation base, $200,000 billed at a provisional 20% rate versus $180,000 at a final 18% rate produces a $20,000 billing overage before other contract adjustments. A final indirect-rate adjustment is not automatically the same journal entry as a Topic 606 revenue change. If settlement changes the consideration expected under the customer contract, the contractor separately assesses whether and how it changes the Topic 606 transaction price, including variable consideration and its constraint, and how any change is allocated to satisfied or partially satisfied performance obligations. That assessment depends on award/customer contract terms and facts not established by this illustration; where the adjustment is only a cost-reimbursement billing or settlement matter, its accounting is separately evaluated. FAR 52.216-7 duties and allowability limits remain controlled by the clause as incorporated in the award.

## Remaining boundary

The 2012 pamphlet answers the prior “unread PDF” gap for pp. 13-15, but it does not establish a universal timekeeping manual, independent compliance duty, or complete records recipe for every agency or award. The v2 ICE manual has real page-level reads; the current v1.08 Power Query user manual was only identified as part of a download package and was not read. The separate DCAA adequacy checklist was not used as evidence. The KPMG guide is 2025 interpretation rather than the authoritative Codification. Application still requires the actual award, contractor accounting practices, cost data, revenue terms, performance facts, and rate/billing schedules.
