# Area 11: Auto and equipment dealerships

This supplement adds an equipment-dealer filing and two equipment-dealer records on rental-fleet accounting evidence. The sources show why dealership close work should follow each VIN or serial-numbered unit through purchase, floorplan, trade-in, reconditioning, use as a rental or demo, sale and warranty/service activity. The filings are issuer examples; they do not create a universal policy for auto or equipment dealers.

## Inventory, trade-ins and incentives

Titan Machinery’s FY2026 Form 10-K reports equipment inventory at specific identification and lower of cost or net realizable value. Its used trade-in estimate starts with expected resale price less expected gross profit and reconditioning; reconditioning/preparation is tracked as work in process, and parts use average cost subject to net realizable value. This gives an equipment-specific measurement example, but the estimated resale inputs, reconditioning costs and sale cutoff remain Titan’s facts and estimates ([f2_cost_titan_10k_2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1409171/000162828026022376/titn-20260131.htm), Note 1, lines 1909-1943).

Auto policy can look different. Lithia’s FY2025 filing reports trade-ins at stand-alone selling price using third-party pricing information, with new and used vehicle costs identified by vehicle and lower of cost or net realizable value. It includes vehicle equipment, reconditioning and transportation in its inventory cost. The contrast is useful for identifying records to test, not evidence that all equipment trade-ins use Titan’s formula or all auto trade-ins use Lithia’s ([cca_dealer-lithia-2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1023128/000102312826000015/lad-20251231.htm), Notes 1 and Revenue Recognition, filed 2026).

Titan says equipment-purchase incentives reduce inventory and cost of revenue when the associated unit sells; qualifying expense reimbursements reduce the related expense when earned. Lithia reports manufacturer holdback, floorplan interest and advertising assistance, and vehicle-specific cash incentives as inventory/cost-of-sales reductions when the related vehicle sells. Deere’s 2024 filing describes the separate manufacturer-side estimation of dealer sales incentives. A dealer’s accounting depends on the actual program: whether it attaches to a unit or expense, qualifying purchases or volumes, cutoff, clawbacks, and whether it is consideration payable to a customer. An OEM policy is not the dealer’s accounting policy ([f2_cost_titan_10k_2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1409171/000162828026022376/titn-20260131.htm); [cca_dealer-lithia-2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1023128/000102312826000015/lad-20251231.htm); [cca_dealer-deere](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/315189/000155837025000116/de-20241027xars.pdf)).

## Floorplan, rental and demonstration equipment

Titan’s filing describes manufacturer and nonmanufacturer floorplan balances separately, including different lender terms, collateral, interest periods and repayment triggers. That is a useful debt-reconciliation example: tie each facility and unit to the lender statement, unit sale or rental status, payoff and cash-flow posting, rather than assuming that every floorplan program works alike ([f2_cost_titan_10k_2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1409171/000162828026022376/titn-20260131.htm), Note 8, lines 2357-2380).

Titan also reports dedicated rental fleet as depreciable property and equipment while some sale inventory may be rented; its rental income, unbilled amounts, customer billings and rent-to-purchase options require separate contract and unit facts. Alta Equipment’s August 2024 response to an SEC staff question distinguishes (a) immediate-sale inventory, (b) sale inventory transferred to rental fleet (“rent-to-sell”), and (c) equipment purchased directly for long-term rental (“rent-to-rent”). Alta described the transfer from inventory to fleet as noncash and classified it in property and equipment while rented. The original response also advanced a company-specific cash-flow view for some later sales. The SEC’s November 2024 follow-up records a promised reclassification of rent-to-rent sale proceeds from operating to investing, so the initial issuer response is not SEC approval or a usable universal rule. Read the later letter and subsequent filings alongside the earlier company explanation ([f2_cost_altg_sec_response_2024](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1759824/000095017024094579/filename1.htm); [f2_cost_altg_sec_followup_2024](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1759824/000000000024012417/filename1.pdf); [f2_cost_titan_10k_2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1409171/000162828026022376/titn-20260131.htm)).

Alta’s 2022 ABL agreement adds a different kind of evidence: the lender separately defines eligible inventory, used fleet, rental fleet and demonstration units, and limits demo eligibility under the borrowing base. Serial visibility, lien priority, payoff, location, condition, appraisal and age affect collateral eligibility. Those definitions govern the parties’ borrowing base, not GAAP classification or the capitalization date for a rental asset. The accounting record and lender certificate should therefore have explicit bridges, not one shared label assumed to answer both ([f2_cost_altg_abl_agreement_2022](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1759824/000095017024031600/altg-ex10_5.htm), Exhibit A, lines 1632-1697 and 2223-2237).

## Warranty, service and departmental margin

Titan reports service-and-consumed-parts revenue over time using its right-to-invoice policy, while standalone parts sales are point-in-time. It distinguishes OEM warranty repair reimbursement from third-party protection plans it may offer on used equipment; rental revenue and deferred/unbilled balances are separately described. Lithia reports manufacturer warranty claims in manufacturer receivables and acts as agent for certain third-party extended-service contracts, net of estimated chargebacks. These examples show why an OEM warranty claim receivable, a dealer’s repair-service revenue, an OEM warranty obligation, and a dealer-sold service contract cannot be collapsed into one “warranty” account. Contract terms, risk of denial or chargeback, promises to the retail customer, principal/agent facts and service periods control ([f2_cost_titan_10k_2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1409171/000162828026022376/titn-20260131.htm), Note 1 and Note 13, lines 1909-1943 and 2640-2653; [cca_dealer-lithia-2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1023128/000102312826000015/lad-20251231.htm), Note 1, lines 2351-2371).

The Titan filing supplies substantive parts/service timing but does not provide a department-level cost accounting or service-bay control manual. A management report can show service labor, parts, sublet work, warranty work, rental, equipment sales and financing separately, with traceable costs and clearly disclosed allocation bases. For short-run decisions, the Area 7 avoidable-cost example illustrates why allocated common costs should not be treated as avoidable merely because a department is closed; that example is a constructed managerial analysis, not an external-reporting rule. A public, rights-cleared dealer departmental accounting manual and evidence for standard labor rates, internal work orders, technician time, parts transfers, comeback/rework and departmental common-cost allocation remain open.

## What this evidence does and does not close

The new equipment filing directly answers the gap for a real dealer’s policies on parts, used equipment, rental income, OEM incentives, floorplan debt and warranty/service activity. Alta’s filed response and the later staff letter show a concrete rent-to-sell/rent-to-rent distinction and why the original issuer explanation must be read with subsequent correspondence. The ABL exhibit supplies lender collateral vocabulary for demos and fleet assets. Together with Lithia and Deere, the records permit useful auto/equipment comparisons without suggesting identical accounting.

Remaining work is entity-level: contracts and dealer/OEM programs, title/control and lien dates, demo-hour or rental use, trade-in payoff and valuation, floorplan payoff/interest, inventory and fleet unit ledgers, repair/warranty claim files, principal-agent facts and current GAAP application. Current detailed Codification paragraphs were not independently verified here. There is still no independently reviewed rights-cleared dealer operations manual or worked departmental profitability case based on a real dealer’s system.
