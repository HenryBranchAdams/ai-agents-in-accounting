# Area 15: Oil and gas operators and nonoperators

Research date: September 23, 2026 (America/Chicago). This supplement extends the September 22 package with actual public-filer accounting policies and transaction-date examples, current interpretive ARO guidance, and one state-specific division-order source. It is general U.S. financial-reporting research, not a title opinion, a JOA interpretation, or an entity accounting conclusion. No operator ledger, executed JOA, JIB, title opinion, purchaser deck, or actual production data was inspected.

## Baseline and selected gaps

The prior corpus already distinguishes operators, nonoperators, royalty owners, purchasers, ONRR reporting, tax depletion, SEC rules and historical ARO, and includes a synthetic production settlement. Its source IDs include `src_us_extractive_sec_410_2025`, `src_us_extractive_onrr_45`, `src_family_office_irs_k1_1065_2025`, and `src_us_extractive_fas143_historical`. The latter is a superseded FASB Statement 143, not current ASC 410. This follow-up adds two actual 2025 issuer accounting policies using different oil-and-gas cost methods, current firm interpretation of the ARO/remediation boundary, and real transaction disclosures where contract effective dates differ from completion dates. MFI-40 remains a July 2023 committee-approved draft; no final adoption was established.

## Findings

### Operator, nonoperator, cost recovery and settlement populations

Two 2025 filings show why working-interest ownership, operator role and the booked transaction population should be tracked separately. Northern Oil and Gas (NOG) describes itself as a nonoperator with interests across more than 100 operators and reports an average working interest of 10.2% in its gross wells. Its filing separately describes advances to operators expected to be applied to joint-interest billings within 90 days (`f2_operating-verticals_oilgas-nog-2025-10k`, Business and Note 3, SEC HTML lines 375-379 and 2916-2922). SM Energy describes operator activities and receivables from co-owners for joint-interest billings; its revenue policy separately discusses purchaser sales and the point at which control transfers (`f2_operating-verticals_oilgas-sm-2025-10k`, Notes on revenue and accounts receivable, lines 1071-1075 and 2573-2587). These are company policies and examples, not a universal allocation of title or a JIB procedure.

For an operator, tie well/facility authorization, property and cost class to vendor support, affiliate/equipment charges, overhead basis, co-owner interest, JIB invoice, credits, and the signed JOA/accounting procedure. For a nonoperator, reconcile its effective-dated WI or participation schedule to the operator statement and its own ledger, including cash calls/advances, JIB billing, net proceeds, suspense and audit exceptions. COPAS procedures can inform a contract but do not replace the signed agreement. The public Fall 2023 MFI-40 document is expressly titled “24-Month Adjustment Period for Joint Account Adjustments” and marked “Committee-Approved Draft - July 20, 2023”; a scheduled vote is not evidence of final adoption. Do not apply that draft's proposed timing as a general rule (`ov-oilgas-copas`).

### Cost methods, depletion and abandonment

Actual 2025 financial statements illustrate two oil-and-gas accounting methods within the SEC reporting framework. NOG discloses full-cost accounting: qualifying costs are accumulated in cost centers, depleted using proved reserves, and subject to an SEC full-cost ceiling test; it also describes its treatment of dry holes, exploration/geological costs, and ARO costs included in the full-cost pool (`f2_operating-verticals_oilgas-nog-2025-10k`, Note 2, lines 1960-1974 and Note 9, lines 3448-3475). SM Energy discloses successful-efforts accounting: geological/geophysical costs and unsuccessful exploratory wells are expensed, while exploratory well costs may remain capitalized as results are evaluated. Its depletion denominators differ by cost pool: capitalized drilling/completion costs, including lease and well equipment, development costs and field support facilities, use units of production based on estimated net proved developed reserves; proved leasehold costs use estimated total net proved reserves. Restoration, dismantlement and abandonment costs and expected salvage proceeds are also included in the DD&A computation (`f2_operating-verticals_oilgas-sm-2025-10k`, Note 2, lines 2522-2523 and 2531). These actual policies demonstrate distinct methods and disclosure patterns; a company’s method, scope, reserve data and impairment/ceiling inputs must be read from its own current filing and records.

The existing dated SEC Regulation S-X rule, 17 CFR 210.4-10, remains the research source for SEC oil-and-gas reporting and full-cost provisions (`ov-oilgas-sec`, 2025 annual CFR version). The live eCFR URL did not yield readable text in this browser run, so this supplement does not claim that the 2025 annual edition proves every current regulatory detail. Book depletion uses company accounting policy and proved-reserve/production evidence; federal income-tax cost or percentage depletion is a separate owner-level tax computation. Existing 2025 IRS partner instructions and prior tax research remain relevant, subject to taxpayer eligibility, basis and year-specific limits (`ov-oilgas-irs-k1`, `ov-oilgas-irs-pub535`). Neither book depletion nor a JIB amount determines a federal royalty allowance.

### ARO recognition and remediation boundary

EY’s August 2026 Financial Reporting Developments guide interprets ASC 410-20 as covering a legal obligation associated with retirement of a tangible long-lived asset when incurred through acquisition, construction, development or normal operation. It distinguishes obligations arising from improper operation or a catastrophic event, which it routes to environmental/remediation contingency guidance rather than treating as the ordinary retirement obligation (`f2_operating-verticals_oilgas-ey-aro-2026`, sections 2.1-2.3, PDF pp. 7-11). Deloitte’s publicly readable ASC 410-20 roadmap likewise separates retirement-associated legal obligations from environmental obligations outside that scope (`f2_operating-verticals_oilgas-deloitte-aro-2025`, sections 4.3.1-4.3.3). Both are interpretive publications, not authoritative FASB text.

NOG’s 2025 filing provides a current company example: ARO fair-value estimates are capitalized in the full-cost pool, accretion is recognized, and the filing provides a liability rollforward and estimation inputs (`f2_operating-verticals_oilgas-nog-2025-10k`, Note 9, lines 3448-3475). SM Energy’s 2025 filing describes initial ARO recognition when the relevant well or facility is placed in service/acquired, use of discounted expected cash flows, and capitalization of the related asset retirement cost (`f2_operating-verticals_oilgas-sm-2025-10k`, Note 1 ARO policy and Note 15 rollforward, lines 2557-2567 and 3658-3685). Company examples do not resolve whether a particular plugging, removal, contamination, bonding or remediation duty is a legal obligation, its obligor, settlement date, cost, credit-adjusted discount rate or subsequent change. FASB Statement 143 in the prior corpus is historical only; current guidance is ASC 410/450, not FAS 143 (`src_us_extractive_fas143_historical`).

Keep the financial-reporting liability and asset cost separate from state/federal financial assurance, tax deductions, ONRR royalty allowances and JIB recovery. They can use different obligors, scopes, measurement rules and dates. No state plugging-bond survey or actual permit/lease obligation was researched.

### Effective dates, closing dates, and ownership decks

NOG’s disclosures give direct examples of transaction completion dates differing from stated effective dates. Its 2026 second-quarter filing describes transactions completed in 2025 with effective dates of June 1, 2024 and July 1, 2025 (`f2_operating-verticals_oilgas-nog-2026-q2`, Note on acquisitions and divestitures, lines 892-894). A separate 2026 offering document describes a February 23, 2026 closing for an acquisition with a July 1, 2025 effective date and separately identifies NOG’s 40% nonoperating working interest and the 60% operator interest acquired by another party (`f2_operating-verticals_oilgas-nog-2026-424b5`, recent acquisitions, lines 186-197). Those disclosures demonstrate that a deck may distinguish contractual/economic effective date, closing/record date and operator role; they do not establish the legal or accounting consequences for another transaction.

The Texas Railroad Commission describes its royalties FAQ as an informal guide, says it has no authority over lease or royalty matters, and cautions that a lease or other contract may set different payment terms. Its account of Texas Natural Resources Code §91.402 says a payor may require a signed division order as a condition of payment; the form may identify effective date, property, payee decimal and interest type, title certification, and notice of an interest change. The page says division orders do not amend a lease or operating agreement (`f2_operating-verticals_oilgas-rrc-division-orders`, Texas Royalties FAQ, introductory disclaimer and “Division Orders”). This is a limited Texas statutory/payments example, not an RRC title determination or national rule. A robust ownership schedule should preserve separately: mineral/royalty title, working/participating interest, purchaser or division-order decimal, operator identity, NRI or revenue decimal, JOA cost share, ONRR payor and royalty/allowance fields. Store each source document and effective date, completion/recording date, suspense status and superseded deck. Do not overwrite one percentage with another simply because the numbers differ.

### Federal royalty rates, allowances and percentages

The existing ONRR Release 4.5 source distinguishes lease/agreement, report month, volume/value, payor, royalty rate and transportation/processing allowances. A gross transportation payment is not itself an ONRR deduction: the applicable federal lease and reporting rules plus documented qualifying costs determine any allowance (`ov-oilgas-onrr`). The ONRR filing decimal and royalty rate are not a working-interest percentage, a private division-order decimal or a GAAP revenue-recognition conclusion. The RRC source above adds one state-specific division-order distinction, but private/state royalties remain dependent on the governing lease and state authority. No state-law or state-tax survey was performed.

## Workpaper recommendation

Enrich the existing production-settlement example with linked but separate schedules for: (1) property and effective-dated ownership deck; (2) operator JIB and nonoperator advances, billed costs, support and exceptions under the executed procedure; (3) purchaser volume/price/transport settlement; (4) owner/royalty disbursement and suspense; (5) ONRR lease, payor, rate and allowance filing; and (6) book cost method, proved reserves, depletion, impairment and ARO rollforward. Each schedule should retain the source document, relevant date type and version instead of substituting an adjacent population’s percentage or date.

## Remaining gaps

- No signed JOA/accounting procedure, final current COPAS interpretation, JIB invoice or nonoperator audit file was available. MFI-40 remains unverified as adopted and must remain labeled draft.
- No executed assignment, title opinion, purchaser deck, private royalty instrument, division order or well-level ledger was inspected. The 2026 transaction filings and Texas FAQ demonstrate date/role distinctions only; they do not resolve legal ownership or accounting cutover for an entity.
- The two issuer filings demonstrate full-cost and successful-efforts accounting in practice but do not determine which method a particular registrant uses, the current SEC/CFR requirements, or an entity’s reserve/depletion/impairment result. The eCFR live page did not return readable content in this research pass; the dated 2025 CFR source is retained with its version limit.
- EY and Deloitte provide public interpretation of ASC 410 boundaries, and company filings provide policy examples, but current consolidated Codification text was not directly verified here. This is an observed web-retrieval limitation, not proof that the Codification is paywalled. the current rendering/access response still was not resolved. Specific legal duties, measurement inputs, state plugging assurance, environmental claims and subsequent changes remain entity-specific.
- No reserve engineering, actual acquisition deck, partnership tax basis, tax depletion eligibility, ONRR filing, state/private royalty settlement or professional review was performed. Historical FAS 143 is not current ASC.
