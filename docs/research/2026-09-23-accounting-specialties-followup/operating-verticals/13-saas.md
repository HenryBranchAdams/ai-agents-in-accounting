## Findings

### Advance billing, contract liabilities, credits, and refunds

The September 2026 EY Revenue FRD reproduces the relevant ASC 606 provisions and explains that advance billings and cash receipts do not by themselves establish that subscription revenue has been earned. For a stand-ready hosted service, identify the promised service and transfer period, then recognize consideration as that service is transferred. Consideration received in advance can remain a contract liability. If the entity expects to refund all or part of received or receivable consideration, the refund component is a refund liability, remeasured at each reporting date. EY's examples extend the refund-liability analysis beyond product returns to refunds for poor service and retrospective price reductions (`f2_operating-verticals_saas_ey-revenue-2026`, September 2026 FRD, section 5.3, PDF pp. 184-185).

Do not put every customer credit in the same bucket. A credit that obligates the provider to deliver future service may represent prepaid future service or an option. If a renewal, usage credit, or discount gives the customer an incremental material right, allocate consideration to that right and recognize it when the option is exercised and the related goods or services transfer, or when it expires. A credit that is an expected refund, a service-level price concession, a correction of an amount invoiced, or an unexercised balance has a different path. The current EY guide distinguishes material rights from ordinary marketing offers, but whether a particular credit has incremental value depends on its terms and comparable prices (`f2_operating-verticals_saas_ey-revenue-2026`, section 4.6, PDF pp. 140-156). The prior breakage source remains relevant for nonrefundable unused rights, with any state unclaimed-property duty assessed separately (`ov-saas-breakage`).

Close evidence should therefore join the signed contract and amendments to invoice and cash dates, period of access, usage detail, right-to-refund and service-level terms, credit issuance and use, customer balance, and any legal remittance obligation. The public interpretation narrows the classification choices; it does not establish a provider's credit terms, expected refunds, rollover rights, or unclaimed-property exposure.

### Contract modifications and blend-and-extend renewals

The September 2026 EY FRD provides a current interpretive explanation of the familiar modification framework. First determine whether the parties approved a change that creates or changes enforceable rights or obligations. A change in an estimate of variable consideration already promised is not automatically a modification. A modification is a separate contract when it adds distinct goods or services at their standalone selling prices as of the modification date. Otherwise, account for the remaining services prospectively when they are distinct from the services already transferred; if the remaining promise is part of a single partially satisfied, non-distinct performance obligation, account for the change as part of the existing obligation, including a cumulative catch-up where appropriate. For a service series, added distinct services are either a separate contract or are accounted for prospectively, not automatically as a catch-up (`f2_operating-verticals_saas_ey-revenue-2026`, section 3.4, PDF pp. 48-64).

This gives a current framework for blend-and-extend analysis, but it does not make a blended price alone decisive. Identify which service periods and remaining rights are added or repriced, test distinctness, compare the added service's consideration with its standalone selling price, and then determine whether the modification creates a new contract or adjusts the existing service series. The prior Deloitte SaaS example is still useful as an illustration of judgment around blended pricing, but it is a 2019 interpretive example and should be read with the current criteria rather than treated as a policy template (`ov-saas-blend`).

### Usage fees, minimums, and credits or rollovers

For a stand-ready service, usage-based fees are variable consideration even when the provider must be ready throughout the term. A usage amount may be allocated to the day or period in which usage occurs when it relates specifically to the service effort or outcome for that period and the allocation reflects the value delivered. EY's cloud-storage example describes a daily per-unit rate where each unit is not an optional purchase. If that allocation exception is not met, the amount is estimated at contract inception, recognized over the service period using an appropriate measure of progress, updated at each reporting date, and remains subject to the variable-consideration constraint. Volume tiers that decline over time may fail the exception unless the pricing is market-based or connected to cost or value (`f2_operating-verticals_saas_ey-revenue-2026`, section 6.3, PDF pp. 261-263).

That guidance does not settle every SaaS contract with a minimum, committed volume, capped usage, or rolled-over units. Determine whether a minimum is fixed consideration for standing ready, a minimum purchase, or a different promised service; determine whether unused units remain available, expire, refund, or carry forward; and assess if the balance is a material right or breakage. No actual rollover or minimum contract was inspected, so this package does not select an accounting outcome for those patterns. The old KPMG public page and prior Deloitte materials remain interpretive context, not substitutes for those terms (`ov-saas-kpmg25`, `ov-saas-usage`, `ov-saas-breakage`).

### Provider software costs and ASU 2025-06 adoption

The official ASU 2025-06 text confirms the amendment to ASC 350-40 removes sequential project-stage references. Under the amended model, capitalization begins when management authorizes and commits funding and it is probable the project will be completed and used as intended. It does not remove the need to determine whether software provided through a cloud arrangement is governed by ASC 350-40 or the external-use software guidance in ASC 985-20. The ASU states that its amendments do not change that scope determination. A hosted-only provider product and a customer's hosted implementation are therefore still different questions (`f2_operating-verticals_saas_asu2506-dates`, official ASU 2025-06, pp. 5-8, 46-49).

The effective date is future: annual reporting periods beginning after December 15, 2027, with interim periods in those annual periods. Early adoption is permitted at the beginning of an annual period, and the ASU permits prospective, modified transition, or retrospective approaches. Thus, for a calendar-year company in 2026, the old stage model remains applicable unless that entity has early-adopted. No entity accounting policy or adoption disclosure was inspected. The prior KPMG 2026 manual continues to provide interpretation on external-use and hosted-only scope, but it remains firm guidance rather than FASB authority (`ov-saas-kpmg-software26`).

The amended probable-to-complete-and-use threshold is not met while significant development uncertainty remains, and the uncertainty must be evaluated even for a project that otherwise appears likely to complete. The ASU identifies two factors: novel, unique or unproven software functions/features; or significant performance requirements that have not yet been identified or continue to be substantially revised. The probable threshold remains unmet until significant uncertainty is resolved; the ASU’s example resolves uncertainty about novel functionality through coding and testing. Authorization and funding alone therefore do not trigger capitalization while the threshold is unmet (`f2_operating-verticals_saas_asu2506-dates`, ASC 350-40-25-12A as amended and BC38-45, PDF pp. 13-14 and 53-56).

## Workpaper recommendation

Add a contract-level rollforward that separately tracks advance cash/billings, earned stand-ready service, variable usage, usage credits and rollover balances, refund or service-level credits, material rights, breakage and remittance liabilities. Require a contract-change assessment for renewal and blend-and-extend events. For software costs, record the legal entity, product delivery model, customer possession rights, management authorization date, probable-completion evidence, adoption status, and chosen transition approach.
