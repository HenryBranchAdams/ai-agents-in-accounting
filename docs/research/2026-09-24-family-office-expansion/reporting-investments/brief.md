# Family-office personal reporting and investments

Research date: September 24, 2026. Scope is FO-R1 through FO-R3 only. This report deepens the existing six-question family-office guide; it does not alter its carrying-value illustration or make a professional conclusion about any actual family entity.

## FO-R1: Personal financial statements, consolidation and family aggregation

Three different reporting purposes need separate labels and reconciliations:

| Purpose | Reporting perimeter | Core reporting basis |
|---|---|---|
| Personal financial statements | An individual, spouses, or a family, as defined for that statement | ASC 274 uses estimated current values for assets and estimated current amounts for liabilities; it includes estimated income taxes on relevant differences from tax bases. |
| GAAP consolidated statements | A defined parent reporting entity and any subsidiaries required under ASC 810 | Analyze each legal entity under the voting-interest or VIE model and applicable scope exceptions. |
| Supplemental family aggregation | The family’s expressly defined beneficial interests and obligations across separate ledgers | State the inclusion, ownership, measurement, tax, restriction, and elimination rules. It is not automatically GAAP consolidation or an ASC 274 statement. |

The current-topic overview displayed by Deloitte’s DART ASC 274 landing page describes the topic as guidance for applying estimated current values and current amounts in personal financial statements, and says its focus is a person’s assets and liabilities. The FASB’s March 2026 agenda-consultation handout also describes current Topic 274 as originating in AICPA SOP 82-1 and covering individuals, spouses, or families. Neither the handout nor the agenda discussion is an ASC amendment. The FASB Codification landing page returned no substantive text in this reader; that access result does not establish a paywall. Accordingly, the mechanics below are supported by the underlying SOP text and current-topic descriptions, while a direct read of current ASC 274 paragraphs remains open.

The reprinted SOP 82-1 supplies concrete mechanics: a personal statement of financial condition presents assets at estimated current values, liabilities at estimated current amounts, estimated taxes on differences between those measurements and tax bases, and net worth. It separately describes current-year income taxes accrued to the statement date. The tax provision on value-versus-tax-basis differences is computed as if assets were realized and liabilities liquidated on the statement date, using applicable tax laws, recapture provisions, and available carryovers; the methods and assumptions should be disclosed. For joint property, the SOP says to include only the person’s beneficial interest as determined under the applicable property law. It also says to show a substantial going-concern business interest as one investment amount, rather than combine the separate entity’s assets and liabilities with similar personal items. These are personal-statement mechanics, distinct from quarterly estimated-tax payments and from a corporation’s ASC 740 tax provision. The SOP is the source material later incorporated into ASC 274, not a substitute for checking current Codification wording.

ASC 810 asks whether a specified reporting entity controls a legal entity. Current KPMG guidance describes two models: the VIE model and the voting-interest model. A VIE is consolidated by its primary beneficiary when that reporting entity has power over the activities that most affect the VIE and potentially significant exposure to its losses or benefits. For a non-VIE, majority voting rights or majority LP kick-out rights are usual control indicators, subject to substantive rights and other facts. This entity-level analysis does not arise merely because family members share ownership, an office arranges payments, or a trust appears on a family report.

A family-level schedule can combine selected interests without meeting ASC 810’s parent-subsidiary perimeter. It must show how the legal entity’s assets and liabilities are replaced by, or reconciled to, the family member’s ownership interest so the same value is not counted twice. It should separately identify trusts, restricted interests, external claims, and reciprocal balances. The existing example’s $1,060,500 of provisional carrying-value assets, $3,000 of outside liabilities, and $1,057,500 net assets remain a supplemental carrying-value view. They are not a current-value ASC 274 statement or a GAAP consolidated balance sheet. If the intended product is an ASC 274 statement, supported current valuations, beneficial ownership evidence, the estimated-tax calculation, and required disclosures are still needed.

## FO-R2: Investment-company scope and a family investor’s fund interest

Do not extend a fund’s ASC 946 treatment automatically to the family office, service company, trust, or holding entity. Decide the reporting entity first, then assess whether that entity itself is within ASC 946. The prior specialty follow-up’s August 2026 KPMG investment-company handbook (R068) is the current substantive scope source already read: it covers ASC 946 scope, fund capital/NAV accounting, class allocations, and carry/clawback. It says in-scope investment-company investments are generally at fair value. Its fund accounting conclusions do not by themselves classify a family office or tell an investor how to measure its separately held interest.

For an entity outside ASC 946, first consider ASC 810 consolidation and other applicable models such as the equity method; only then select residual investment-accounting guidance for the specific instrument. The current KPMG consolidation handbook reproduces ASC 810-10-15-12(d): an in-scope investment company generally does not consolidate an investee that is not an investment company, subject to the ASC 946-810-45-3 exception. KPMG explains that an investment company must still assess control of an operating service company, and that an investment company itself remains subject to the VIE and voting-interest models when another reporting entity evaluates it. The 2026 KPMG investment handbook also distinguishes debt/equity security accounting from consolidation and equity-method accounting. An investor’s choice of model is separate from the fund’s own ASC 946 accounting and NAV calculation.

An investor’s NAV practical expedient is conditional. Deloitte’s August 2026 ASC 820 Roadmap reproduces ASC 820-10-15-4 and 35-59 through 35-62: the investment must lack a readily determinable fair value and be in an eligible investment company or qualifying real-estate fund; NAV must be calculated consistently with ASC 946 as of the investor’s measurement date. The choice is investment-by-investment but is applied consistently to the investor’s whole position, except for a portion whose sale at a different amount is probable under the stated criteria.

When reported NAV is not as of the investor’s measurement date, or is not calculated consistently with ASC 946, paragraph 35-60 requires the investor to consider whether an adjustment to the latest NAV is necessary. The target is an ASC 946-consistent NAV as of the investor’s own reporting date. A June fund NAV is therefore not automatically a supported September carrying value. Build a dated bridge from the fund NAV through subsequent calls and distributions, changes in underlying asset values, accrued costs and liabilities, share-class allocations, and contractual carry/waterfall amounts. These are evidence inputs to assess and, where necessary, adjust the NAV; the rule does not prescribe an automatic adjustment for every late NAV. If the expedient is unavailable or a sale at a different amount is probable, estimate fair value under ASC 820 using the relevant market-participant evidence and rights of the investor’s interest. A stale NAV difference should remain identified as unresolved until support exists.

This adds an investor-measurement branch to the existing guide’s capital-activity answer. Its synthetic $140,000 June NAV and $20,000 unposted September difference should stay provisional; do not present the fund statement NAV alone as September measurement. Reused tax sources R040, R049/R050 and R120/R121 help distinguish tax capital, K-1 reporting, outside basis and special tax-basis layers; they do not establish ASC 946 or fair-value measurements.

## FO-R3: SEC family-office exclusion and its separate perimeter

The current eCFR text of 17 CFR 275.202(a)(11)(G)-1, displayed as current through September 22, 2026, provides that a qualifying family office is not considered an investment adviser for purposes of the Advisers Act. The rule requires the office to have no clients other than defined “family clients,” to be wholly owned by family clients, to be exclusively controlled by family members and/or family entities, and not to hold itself out publicly as an investment adviser. “Family client” is a defined category, not a general label: it includes specified family members and former family members, key employees subject to limits, certain family-funded charities, estates, trusts, and wholly family-owned companies operated solely for family clients. The rule contains a one-year treatment for a non-family client received through death or another involuntary transfer and narrow grandfathering provisions. The eCFR displays the original 2011 rule and a 2016 amendment.

The rule’s defined “family clients” determine this adviser-exclusion test. They do not determine an accounting reporting entity, make the family office owner of a trust’s corpus, decide ASC 810 control, or exempt an investment fund from other Investment Company Act requirements. A multifamily arrangement cannot rely on its name alone: actual client identities, the family relationships, ownership, control, and any statutory or regulatory alternatives require a specific legal review. The existing guide’s 2011 adopting release is useful history; the current eCFR text is the evidence for the rule as displayed now.

Source read: [eCFR, 17 CFR 275.202(a)(11)(G)-1](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-275/section-275.202%28a%29%2811%29%28G%29-1), paragraphs (a)-(d), especially (b)(1)-(3), (c), and (d)(4)-(9), point-in-time page current through 2026-09-22. The eCFR identifies itself as authoritative but unofficial; this is a federal adviser-regulation source, not an accounting standard.
