# Family-office trust and estate accounting research

Research snapshot: 2026-09-24. This is a bounded, research-only deepening of the existing Texas trust-beneficiary and federal tax-coordination routes. The selected IRS reporting sources are for tax year 2025; the CFR sources are the 2025 annual edition (April 1, 2025). It is not a trust-specific, return-year-specific, probate, legal, or tax conclusion. Governing instruments, applicable local law, taxpayer/owner facts, accounting method, elections, valuations, and current-year instructions can change the result.

## FO-T1. Fiduciary principal/income, federal DNI, and capital gains

**Prior coverage.** `rq-family-office-trust-beneficiary` already routes to Texas Property Code Chapter 116 and the 2025 Form 1041 instructions. It says principal/income is governed first by instrument/local law and that those fiduciary labels do not determine federal DNI, a distribution deduction, or beneficiary tax character. This work adds the federal bridge and the capital-gain exceptions.

**Mechanics.** For Subchapter J, “income” generally means trust income determined under the governing instrument and applicable local law. Treasury Regulation §1.643(b)-1 says traditional principles matter: interest, dividends, and rent are generally income, sale proceeds generally principal; a reasonable local-law total-return apportionment can be respected. It also cautions that a state-law-authorized adjustment and a different, non-authorized method change can have different federal tax consequences. Locator: 26 CFR 1.643(b)-1, official 2025 CFR, printed pp. 57–58, [GovInfo PDF](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2025-title26-vol10/pdf/CFR-2025-title26-vol10-sec1-643b-1.pdf).

Federal distributable net income is a separate tax computation: taxable income with statutory modifications, including removal of the distribution deduction from the calculation. Under 26 CFR 1.643(a)-3, capital gains are ordinarily excluded from DNI and are not ordinarily treated as distributed. Each of the regulation’s noncharitable inclusion routes is subject to the common condition that the treatment follow the governing instrument and applicable local law, or a reasonable and impartial exercise of discretion authorized by the instrument or local law: gain is allocated to income; corpus gain is consistently treated on books, records, and returns as part of a beneficiary distribution; or corpus gain is actually distributed or used to determine a required or discretionary distribution. The regulation separately includes gains paid, permanently set aside, or used for charitable purposes where a §642(c) deduction is allowed. Losses generally net at trust level, subject to the regulation’s exception for gains used under its actual-distribution rule. Locator: 26 CFR 1.643(a)-1 and -3, official 2025 CFR, printed pp. 51–53, [GovInfo PDF](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2025-title26-vol10/pdf/CFR-2025-title26-vol10-sec1-643a-3.pdf). These are specific inclusion routes, not a rule that every principal receipt is outside DNI or every payment carries capital-gain character.

For the existing Texas example, Chapter 116 defaults to the governing instrument and allocates unresolved receipts/disbursements to principal; its selected rules generally allocate bond interest to income and sale/redemption proceeds to principal. A prudent trustee’s adjustment power can move some capital gain to income when statutory conditions are met, but is constrained by impartiality, tax ownership/inclusion limits, and other restrictions. At death or termination, §§116.101–.102 classify receipts by when due/accrued; §§116.051–.052 address final allocation, undistributed income, and beneficiary-specific records. Expense rules are category-specific: §§116.201–.202 do not make every trust bill a 50/50 split. Locators: Texas Property Code §§116.004–.005, .051–.052, .101–.102, .151–.163, .201–.202, [official Texas Legislature text](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/pr/htm/pr.116.htm). Texas is a selected jurisdictional branch, not a national default.

**Guide/intake consequence.** Maintain distinct fields/schedules for fiduciary accounting income/principal; taxable income and DNI; realized gain and its inclusion basis, if any; required/actual distribution; distribution deduction; and beneficiary tax character. Capture trust terms, applicable situs/governing law, distribution power, beneficiary shares, payment date, capital-gain allocation decision, tax-year instructions, and return/ledger treatment. Reconcile those schedules rather than deriving one from another. Existing source records `src_family_office_texas_principal_income` and `src_family_office_irs_1041_2025` are enrichment candidates; add the two Treasury regulations as authority records if the coordinator accepts the proposed intake. The 2025 IRS instructions explain Schedule B DNI and beneficiary/K-1 routing, but do not establish 2026 return rules.

## FO-T2. Grantor/nongrantor reporting and tax payment versus reimbursement

**Prior coverage.** Existing `rq-family-office-tax-coordination` separates taxpayer, entity, and tax year; the 2025 Form 1041 instructions already route grantor trust items to their tax owner. This adds partial-grantor reporting and distinguishes who owes the tax from whose cash paid it.

**Mechanics.** Under 26 CFR 1.671-3, a grantor or other person treated as owner reports the items attributable to the owned portion. Under §1.671-4(a), that portion’s items generally are not reported as trust Form 1041 amounts but appear on a statement attached to Form 1041; the owner reports them on the owner’s return. A wholly grantor-owned trust has optional reporting methods subject to detailed conditions (including payor/TIN and statement or information-return rules); they are not a blanket exemption from information reporting. Where only part is grantor-owned, the nongrantor portion is reported under ordinary trust rules and Schedule K-1 can report distributions from that portion. The IRS’s 2025 instructions give this split and a securities-sale example. Locators: 26 CFR 1.671-3 and -4(a), (b), official 2025 CFR, printed pp. 322–329 and 323–326, [GovInfo §1.671-3](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2025-title26-vol10/pdf/CFR-2025-title26-vol10-sec1-671-3.pdf) and [§1.671-4](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2025-title26-vol10/pdf/CFR-2025-title26-vol10-sec1-671-4.pdf); 2025 Instructions for Form 1041, “Grantor Type Trusts,” [IRS](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1041).

A tax owner’s §671 liability remains that person’s liability when paid from personal funds. Rev. Rul. 2004-64 concludes on its facts that a grantor’s payment of tax attributable to grantor-trust income is payment of the grantor’s own legal obligation, not a gift to trust beneficiaries. Mandatory reimbursement from trust assets, whether required by instrument or local law, can cause full trust estate inclusion under §2036(a)(1). Discretionary reimbursement authority alone does not cause estate inclusion, whether or not exercised, assuming no understanding between grantor and trustee. The ruling states that discretion combined with other facts, such as a pre-existing arrangement, grantor power to replace the trustee with the grantor, or creditor access, may cause inclusion. IRS will not apply the Situation 2 mandatory-reimbursement estate-tax holding adversely to a grantor’s estate for a trust created before October 4, 2004. This ruling addresses its stated facts and does not establish a universal bookkeeping entry. Locator: Rev. Rul. 2004-64, 2004-2 C.B. 7, [IRS Internal Revenue Bulletin 2004-27](https://www.irs.gov/irb/2004-27_IRB).

For comparison, in a nongrantor trust, an amount applied under the instrument to discharge a beneficiary/person’s legal obligation is treated under §1.662(a)-4 as though distributed to that person, and the applicable DNI allocation/character rules still matter. That rule is not a substitute for the grantor’s §671 attribution analysis. Locator: 26 CFR 1.662(a)-4 and §1.662(b)-1, official 2025 CFR, printed p. 139, [GovInfo PDF](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2025-title26-vol10/pdf/CFR-2025-title26-vol10-sec1-662a-4.pdf).

**Guide/intake consequence.** Keep (1) legal tax owner and grantor/non-grantor portion, (2) trust asset/cash ledger, (3) tax assessed and tax year, (4) legal taxpayer, (5) payment source, and (6) reimbursement authority/request/approval/amount/date as separate, linked fields. A grantor’s own payment of the grantor’s §671 tax is not automatically a trust tax expense or distribution. A trust payment of someone else’s legal obligation needs instrument/local-law and tax-distribution review before classification. Obtain the trust instrument and amendments, grantor powers/ownership analysis, filed reporting method, payment proof, reimbursement terms, and trustee decision evidence. Add/enrich the cited primary sources; rights to reuse source text remain unknown and no source text should be copied.

## FO-T3. Death-time opening, basis, IRD, and beneficiary distributions

**Prior coverage.** Existing `rq-family-office-tax-coordination` cites 2025 IRS Publication 559 for separate decedent, estate, and beneficiary reporting. This research adds the federal basis/IRD distinction and a Texas death-time fiduciary allocation example.

**Mechanics.** At death, first establish which property/right was owned by the decedent or passes to the estate/trust; an internal workpaper schedule is not itself a conclusion about probate ownership, inclusion in the federal gross estate, or a statutory probate inventory. Treasury Regulation §1.1014-1 states the general basis rule for qualifying property acquired from a decedent: fair market value at death, or the alternate valuation date where the executor makes the §2032 election. Limitations and special rules apply. For property within §1.1014-10(c)(1) (generally qualifying included property whose value increases estate tax payable after credits), initial basis generally cannot exceed the final federal estate-tax value or, while final value is unresolved, the reported value; the requirement continues while basis remains related to that value. Section 1.1014-10 applies to covered property if the decedent’s federal estate-tax return is filed after September 17, 2024. Detailed property categories and exceptions still require review. A right to income taxed as IRD is excluded from the §1014 basis adjustment by §1014(c); do not mark every date-of-death asset “stepped up.” Locators: 26 CFR 1.1014-1(a)-(b), official 2025 CFR, printed pp. 66–67, [GovInfo PDF](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2025-title26-vol13/pdf/CFR-2025-title26-vol13-sec1-1014-1.pdf); 26 CFR 1.1014-10(a)-(c), (f), printed pp. 67–73, [GovInfo PDF](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2025-title26-vol13/pdf/CFR-2025-title26-vol13-sec1-1014-10.pdf).

IRD is gross income the decedent was entitled to but that was not properly includible in the final or prior income-tax year under the decedent’s method of accounting. The 2025 regulation lists all accrued income for a cash-method decedent, income accrued solely by reason of death for an accrual-method decedent, and contingent claims existing at death; special partnership/installment-obligation rules need their own review. The recipient estate/person generally includes IRD when received, and §691(c) may permit a related estate-tax deduction when the right’s value was included in the gross estate. Locators: 26 CFR 1.691(a)-1 and -2, official 2025 CFR, printed pp. 408–409, [GovInfo PDF](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2025-title26-vol10/pdf/CFR-2025-title26-vol10-sec1-691a-1.pdf); Publication 559 (2025), “Income in Respect of a Decedent,” [IRS](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p559).

The estate’s income-tax return is distinct from the decedent’s final individual return and any estate-tax return. For estates/trusts, allowable distribution deductions and beneficiary gross-income inclusion are limited/structured through DNI; the return and Schedule K-1 capture tax character under federal rules. IRS Publication 559 (2025), “Personal Representatives,” “Final Income Tax Return,” and “Income Tax Return of an Estate,” and 2025 Form 1041 instructions provide routing only for those editions. If death ends grantor ownership of a continuing trust, §1.671-4(h) says the affected portion may no longer report under the grantor method; a continuing trust wholly owned by the decedent must obtain a new TIN. Confirm which portion, if any, remains grantor-owned and follow the applicable transition rules. On the selected Texas branch, §§116.101–.102 distinguish pre-death due/accrued receipts from post-death periodic or nonperiodic receipts; §116.051 allocates after-death administration items; §116.052 requires beneficiary-specific accounting of collected but undistributed income. These fiduciary entries do not by themselves determine federal basis, IRD, DNI, estate inclusion, or K-1 character.

**Proposed opening schedule and guide section.** For each asset or right, record account/legal title and beneficial owner; date-of-death quantity and valuation support; carrying/tax basis and valuation method; gross-estate/probate status as separately determined; accrued or contingent receivables and decedent tax accounting method; basis property versus IRD flag with rationale; cutoff receipts/disbursements and Texas fiduciary allocation where applicable; successor share/authority; transfers with value/date/consideration; and receiving taxpayer, EIN/TIN-controlled identifier, return year, 1041/K-1/1099 reporting link. Keep decedent final return, estate Form 1041, Form 706, trust records, and beneficiary return workpapers separate. Inputs needed include title and governing documents, date-of-death statements/appraisals, prior basis records, accounting method, claim/partnership/payor documents, executor elections, creditor/admin expense support, distribution authority, beneficiary shares, and actual post-death receipts/distributions. This is a control/workpaper recommendation, not a claim that one federal inventory form or one basis/IRD outcome applies in every estate.

**Source intake and limits.** Enrich existing `src_family_office_irs_p559_2025`, `src_family_office_irs_1041_2025`, and `src_family_office_texas_principal_income`; add 26 CFR 1.1014-1, 1.1014-10, 1.691(a)-1, 1.671-4, and 1.662(a)-4 as new primary source records. No broad state survey was conducted. Rights to external text are unknown; only links, locators, and short findings are retained. Tax-year 2025 IRS publications/instructions and the 2025 CFR edition do not establish later return-year or later-amended authority. Instrument/local law, decedent accounting method, asset title, elections, actual valuations, gross-estate inclusion, and distribution facts remain required.
