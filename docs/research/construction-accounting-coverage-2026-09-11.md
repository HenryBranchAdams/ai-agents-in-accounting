# Construction accounting coverage review

**Research cutoff:** September 11, 2026  
**Scope:** General US construction contractors; US GAAP and federal tax, with selected state comparisons  
**Corpus:** `2026-09-07.4` before review → `2026-09-11.1` after additions  
**Review basis:** AI-assisted source research and original editorial synthesis; no professional accounting or legal sign-off

## Finding

The corpus had broad accounting infrastructure but very little construction-specific depth. Its strongest dedicated accounting reference was a catalog entry for the AICPA construction contractors guide. Generic revenue, accounts payable, close, tax, treasury and asset workflows were relevant, but they did not explain how a construction business connects contract estimates, project costs, earned revenue, payment applications, retainage and cash. An industry AI survey did not fill that accounting gap.

WIP is therefore a central gap, but treating it as one isolated spreadsheet topic would leave most of the problem unresolved. The necessary coverage follows the project lifecycle: contract intake; estimating and cost commitments; work performed and cost cutoff; contract changes; earned revenue and billing; subcontractor and labor obligations; tax; collections and financing; final costs, claims and releases. Equipment, insurance and the reporting entity cut across that lifecycle.

This review adds **37 source records, 13 construction workflow maps, five reading collections and four research briefs**, and rechecks the existing AICPA guide’s publisher landing page. That brings the corpus from **779 to 838 records**, including **577 sources, 73 workflows, 35 collections and 17 guides**. Record counts describe the inventory. They do not establish complete or professionally verified coverage.

The result is a substantially broader research foundation. The remaining gaps are concrete: current consolidated GAAP validation, a complete jurisdiction footprint, current Florida general guidance, usable real job-cost evidence, tested construction-system extraction, and expert review. Those gaps are preserved instead of being hidden behind source counts.

## Why this business example is useful

Butler-Cohen’s public site describes general contracting, design-build and construction-management services, with education, industrial, government and commercial projects. This is enough to motivate a general contractor research profile with multiple project types, customers and delivery arrangements. It does **not** establish the company’s revenue, ownership, accounting basis, tax methods, financing, federal-contract exposure, entity structure or software. No conclusions in this report concern its actual accounting practices.[^profile]

The research uses a general US contractor as the target and includes other states because the requested scope is multistate. Texas is the strongest state anchor. California, Washington and New York illustrate materially different tax frameworks; Florida is included only through a qualified historical case because the general guide could not be retrieved. Public works, joint ventures, related-party leases and residential-contract tax rules are conditional branches, not assumed facts about the example company.

## How the gaps were assessed

The baseline is the preserved `2026-09-07.4` corpus: stable record IDs, descriptions, linked sources and detailed workflow content. Searches covered WIP, work in progress, construction, cost-to-cost, percentage of completion, retainage and related concepts. A reference counted as relevant only when its substance helped answer the accounting question. A generic framework link or incidental mention of construction did not count as a developed industry workflow.

The source hierarchy distinguishes enacted law and regulations, standard-setting amendments, official agency guidance, non-authoritative staff material, professional interpretation, contract-form instructions and commercial process documentation. FASB amendments are useful primary documents, but the current Codification is the authoritative consolidated GAAP source. The paid AICPA guide was not accessed beyond its public landing page. Older interpretations retain their dates; model rules are not treated as enacted state law.[^aicpa]

The research also retained adverse evidence: inconsistent tax-transition wording, a legacy SBA form with an expired printed OMB date, inaccessible guidance and historical model assumptions. Each canonical source states what was checked. No publisher full text was placed in the corpus, and unknown external reuse rights remain unknown.

## WIP: the central bridge

A WIP schedule should let a reviewer explain both the current project outlook and changes from the prior close. Contract value, forecast final cost, actual cost, earned revenue, billing and collections are separate quantities. The AICPA overview supplies an accessible introduction; the standards and industry interpretations are needed to test the assumptions underneath it.[^wip]

The starting questions concern the promised services and the basis for measuring performance. A design-build arrangement, a management service, and a contractor acting as principal can lead to different analyses. “Long-term contract” and “percentage of completion” are not sufficient accounting conclusions. The initial FASB revenue amendment addresses over-time criteria and progress measurement; the construction implementation discussion identifies practical questions that the generic revenue workflow did not surface.[^revenue][^issues]

For research and retrieval, the useful record is not simply “calculate percent complete.” It is a linked evidence package: executed scope and amendments; recognizable transaction price; current and prior estimates; cost-ledger reconciliation; excluded or adjusted progress inputs; cumulative and current-period revenue; billing status; contract balances; credit exposure; and exceptions requiring judgment. This is the proposed information design for the new workflow, not a claim that any particular system already supplies those fields reliably.

### A simple original illustration

Assume a single eligible over-time performance obligation, a faithful cost-based measure, no special material or inefficiency adjustments, a $10 million transaction price, estimated final costs of $8 million, and $4 million of qualifying cost incurred. The illustrative completion measure is 50%, cumulative revenue is $5 million and cumulative gross profit is $1 million. If cumulative billings are $4.5 million, the simple earned-versus-billed difference is $0.5 million. Financial-statement classification still requires the contract’s payment rights and balance presentation analysis.

If revised estimated final cost rises to $9 million while incurred cost stays at $4 million, the illustrative completion measure falls to 44.44%, cumulative revenue to about $4.444 million and cumulative gross profit to about $0.444 million. A roughly $0.556 million cumulative revenue adjustment can therefore arise from a forecast change with no new cash event. Current-period revenue also depends on what was recognized previously. The example is original and deliberately simplified; it is not an actual contract, a posting instruction or a universal GAAP formula.

This example explains why reliable estimate revisions and complete cost cutoff matter more than displaying a percent-complete field. It also explains why revenue, profit and available cash cannot be inferred from one another. A separate analysis is needed if a contract is expected to lose money, if unusual costs do not depict performance, or if material amounts remain subject to constraint or dispute. EY’s dated construction discussion is a useful route into those questions, with its current-applicability limitation kept explicit.[^ey]

## Upstream: estimating, costs and contract changes

The estimate-to-complete process is an accounting dependency. A research corpus should help distinguish original estimate, approved budget, incurred costs, open commitments and forecast uncommitted work. These are often different populations with different dates. A remaining subcontract commitment can already be included in the project manager’s remaining-cost forecast; adding both blindly can double count. Conversely, work performed but not invoiced can disappear from a ledger-only review.

The new job-cost workflow therefore asks for receiving and field evidence, time records, subcontract progress, purchase commitments, burden-rate policies, equipment usage and estimate versions. Its proposed controls reconcile costs and allocation pools to the ledger, retain cost-code mappings, and expose unexplained adjustments. DCAA training illustrates the need for direct/indirect segregation and reconciliation in covered procurement, while FAR 31.105 supplies a distinct federal cost context. Neither source imposes one government-contract accounting system on all private contractors.[^dcaa][^farcost]

Change orders require at least two tracks: commercial status and accounting status. A requested change can create expected costs before a customer has agreed to pay it. An authorized change may still lack a settled price. A billed claim may remain disputed. The proposed map retains requested, authorized, priced, executed, billed and collected states, with links between owner and subcontract changes. This avoids presenting the commercial log as a ready-made revenue journal.

Contract acquisition or fulfillment costs also deserve a separate research question. Winning a project, mobilizing for it and performing services already transferred are different events. The FASB staff compilation gives a qualified route into this boundary; it is not authoritative approval to capitalize any cost labeled “preconstruction.”[^staff]

## Downstream: billing, retainage, payments and cash

Construction billing introduces evidence that general invoice workflows do not fully describe: schedules of values, stored materials, architect or owner certification, prior applications, approved changes and retainage. AIA’s instructions explain the named payment-form structure. The corpus uses that as contract-administration context; it does not reproduce the licensed form or treat certification as earned revenue.[^aia]

The research model keeps requested, certified, invoiced and paid amounts separate. Retainage should be tracked by the condition that releases it, not just by a percentage. A retained amount can require continued performance or another condition beyond the passage of time. The accounting question then includes the nature of the right, presentation and credit risk. The new credit-loss amendment also requires attention to eligible current assets, entity type and elections; it is not a blanket exemption for retained balances.[^cecl]

On the payable side, the project evidence must connect accepted work and accrued liabilities to approved payments, withholding grounds and actual cash settlement. Generic vendor onboarding, independent bank-change verification, segregation of duties and payment approval remain valuable existing coverage. The construction extension adds project obligations and jurisdiction-specific legal evidence. It does not weaken those general payment controls.

Texas construction trust-fund law, private prompt-payment rules, public purchasing payment rules and lien law are separate sources with separate scopes. California’s conditional/unconditional release framework provides another concrete example of why a waiver cannot be interpreted without jurisdiction and payment status. This report intentionally does not supply a universal retainage rate, lien deadline or payment calendar.[^txtrust][^txprivate][^txpublic][^txlien][^cawaiver]

Cash forecasting should use expected receipts and disbursements, including delayed certification, retained amounts, payroll, tax, subcontract payments and debt service. A profitable job can still require financing while cash is tied up. Surety and lender information should reconcile to approved accounting records while preserving the specific definitions in their requests. SBA Form 994F illustrates work-in-process information requested for underwriting, but the checked copy has a printed OMB expiration of November 30, 2025. It is retained only as historical data-design context.[^sba]

Closeout is not merely a project status change. The proposed workflow continues tracking late invoices, estimate revisions, punch-list work, final releases, retained receipts and payments, warranties and claims. Backlog also needs its own definition: executed work, tentative awards, options and recognized revenue should remain distinguishable. Neither backlog nor bonding capacity was inferred for the example company.

## Public works and payroll

A government customer creates a coverage question; it does not automatically make a private contractor subject to GASB reporting or transform a procurement contract into a federal grant. The new reading path begins with actual clauses, funding and entity roles. FAR 52.232-5 and 52.232-27 describe payment evidence and downstream obligations for covered federal contracts. Texas Chapter 2253 provides a separate public-work bond framework.[^farpayment][^farprompt][^txbond]

Labor costing must reconcile person, day, project, classification, wages, fringes and payroll totals. Prevailing-wage duties are additional to payroll taxes and cost allocation. DOL’s indexed WH-347 instructions describe weekly certified information and a compliance statement for covered work. The direct page could not be fetched, so that source’s review is explicitly limited to publisher-indexed text. California’s public-works reporting page was separately inspected.[^dol][^capayroll]

The next depth layer should address the actual wage determinations, apprenticeship conditions, fringe credits, overtime, worker classification and subcontract reporting for the selected project population. State unemployment, withholding, local payroll taxes and employee travel remain footprint-specific gaps. A generic federal payroll source does not resolve those obligations, and this review did not certify or submit any payroll information.

## Federal construction tax and the source conflict

Financial-reporting WIP and tax accounting should remain separate but reconcilable. A contractor’s tax analysis needs contract category, tax year, entry date, expected duration, gross-receipts aggregation, tax-shelter status, accounting method history, cost capitalization and completion evidence. The 2021 IRS construction audit guide is useful as an issue map, but it explicitly limits its authority and predates the current statutory changes.[^atg]

Section 70430 of Public Law 119-21 changes the residential construction exception and applies by contracts entered into in taxable years beginning after July 4, 2025. That is different from applying a rule to every contract signed after July 4. For a calendar-year taxpayer, the distinction points to the 2026 contract cohort. The section’s three-year substitution concerns a specific residential capitalization provision; it does not justify a universal three-year contractor exception.[^law]

The December 2025 Form 8697 instructions contain notes using a broader contract-date formulation and saying that look-back does not apply to any contract completed within three years. That language appears inconsistent with the checked statutory section. Revenue Procedure 2026-32’s construction method-change provisions use the taxable-year cohort distinction. The new tax brief preserves this apparent conflict and routes readers to the statute and procedure instead of presenting the form note as a safe eligibility rule.[^lookback][^methods]

This review does not calculate a company’s tax liability or decide its permissible method. Current inflation-adjusted thresholds, aggregation, AMT interactions, method adoption versus change, consent procedures, later adjustments and state conformity need a fact-specific analysis. The unusually important corpus improvement is that the contradiction is discoverable alongside the sources, with dates and review scope attached.

## Multistate construction taxes

The sampled state sources demonstrate why a single contractor-tax rule would be unreliable. Texas differentiates work and contract categories. California distinguishes materials, fixtures and machinery. Washington distinguishes prime contracting, subcontracting and speculative building within a B&O and sales/use-tax framework. New York separates qualifying capital improvements from repair and maintenance and explains certificate consequences.[^txtax][^catax][^watax][^nytax]

These are organizing distinctions, not a complete comparison of liabilities. The new workflow requires project location, work type, purchaser, contract form, property classification, purchase and delivery facts, exemptions and period. It separates tax on purchases from tax collected from customers. Customer exemption status alone is insufficient to classify every contractor purchase.

Florida’s general guide was discoverable in search but returned 404 when fetched from the publisher. A retrieved 2022 Technical Assistance Advisement illustrates contractor consumption through specific window and door facts and a historical exemption. It is labeled accordingly; current Florida statewide coverage remains open. The MTC construction apportionment document is likewise a historical model that expressly anticipates state adaptation. Its three-factor formula cannot be imported as a current rule for all states.[^fltax][^mtc]

The next multistate pass should begin with the actual operating footprint, then verify enacted income/franchise/gross-receipts rules, sourcing, nexus, local taxes, exemptions, filing duties and conformity. No claim of all-50-state coverage is made here.

## Equipment, risk and reporting entities

Three different uses of “construction in progress” can otherwise become confused: work under customer contracts, a contractor building an asset for its own use, and developer-owned real estate. The new equipment workflow makes that distinction explicit. It connects owned and rented equipment, internal recovery rates, repairs, improvements, leases and in-service dates to the existing asset workflows. Tax repair/capitalization guidance answers a different question from book revenue or depreciation.[^tangible]

Related-party equipment or property leases require their own analysis. ASU 2023-01 addresses qualifying common-control lease arrangements and leasehold improvements; it does not eliminate lease accounting. Joint-venture formation accounting under ASU 2023-05 belongs to the venture’s own statements, while a participant must separately assess its investment and reporting boundary. ASU 2018-17’s qualifying private-company alternative also preserves other consolidation requirements.[^lease][^jv][^vie]

Insurance creates another link between operational evidence and accounting. Wrap-up enrollment, payroll, subcontract exposures, audit adjustments, deductibles and claims can change the evidence needed for premium and liability analyses. Travelers’ pages are used only as examples of insurer programs and requested documentation. They do not establish coverage, accounting conclusions or the effectiveness of any product.[^wrap][^premium]

Warranties, guarantees, litigation, expected contract losses and possible insurance recoveries should not collapse into a single reserve. The KPMG handbook provides a qualified research route across contingencies, commitments and guarantees; the reviewed portions do not decide any project claim. The new workflow keeps exposure, recovery, contractual commitment and accounting judgment visible as separate questions.[^contingency]

## What remains to reach deeper coverage

The high-priority continuation is professional review of the integrated contract-to-WIP-to-ledger model, using current consolidated standards and realistic contract evidence. The original illustration should eventually be supplemented by rights-cleared examples covering forecast changes, disputed modifications, uninstalled materials, loss contracts, retainage and reopened jobs. Those would be research examples within the corpus mission, not a revived execution lab or benchmark product.

The next source work is jurisdiction-specific. Establish the contractor’s actual footprint before adding states indiscriminately. Prioritize current Florida general guidance, applicable wage determinations and payroll mobility rules, state income apportionment and federal conformity, and legal payment/retainage/bond rules in the operating jurisdictions. Date every obligation and retain the source needed to refresh it.

The next data work is construction-system evidence. The corpus already has general ERP and extraction references, but it lacks validated links among project-management change logs, estimate versions, job-cost transactions, schedules of values, payroll, AP, the general ledger and banking. Useful research should establish population completeness, original identifiers, corrections, reversals, period cutoffs, mapping changes, access restrictions and reconciliation totals. No such live integration was tested in this review.

Conditional research should expand when the facts justify it: self-development and real-estate sales; business combinations; employee ownership and compensation; union and multiemployer plans; foreign operations and currency; current tax depreciation, interest limitations and R&D incentives; environmental obligations; financial distress; and specialized contracts. General accounting coverage remains useful, but these branches were not newly verified in depth.

Finally, independent deployment evidence and reusable real ledger/WIP datasets remain missing. A vendor feature page, a well-written workflow and a passing software test cannot establish that an accounting agent produces correct construction accounts. The present additions improve what readers can retrieve and what questions they can ask; they do not establish operational performance.
