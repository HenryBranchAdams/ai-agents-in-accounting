# Accounting Agents — full-scope source map

Research date: 2026-08-23
Scope: primary and official sources needed to expand the site from a first-deployment primer into a full accounting-lifecycle field guide. This note is research support for public educational copy, not accounting, audit, tax, legal, payments, or security advice.

## Executive finding

The existing 189-source catalog is strong on audit, AI governance, security, agent engineering, and evaluation. It is not yet balanced for the proposed workflow corpus. Of the 189 records, 133 (70%) sit in audit and assurance, controls and governance, security and identity, or evaluation and evidence. Only 10 sit in accounting and reporting, and those are mostly broad framework indexes, conceptual material, digital-reporting resources, or AI-related observations rather than workflow-specific authority.

The most important source additions are therefore not more general AI commentary. They are:

1. workflow-level accounting anchors for revenue, financial instruments and credit loss, consolidation, foreign currency, inventory, property and equipment, impairment, leases, income taxes, accounting policies and estimates, and presentation;
2. current official rules and operating material for certifications, SEC submissions, tax e-file authorization, payment rails, payment fraud, sanctions, vendor identity, and record retention;
3. current primary guidance for continuous monitoring, incident response, and media sanitization.

The site can then make complete coverage claims without implying that a source authorizes an agent to act. The A0–A4 model is a site-authored risk taxonomy, not a regulator-created classification. Its action boundaries should be presented as recommended control design derived from the cited accounting objective, applicable law or rail rule, organizational authority, reversibility, materiality, and segregation of duties.

## Method and claim standard

The audit reviewed every record in `app/resources-data.ts` and searched titles and editorial notes for the named workflows and cross-cutting subjects in the full-scope goal. Candidate additions were limited to standards setters, regulators, government sources, recognized technical specifications, and first-party official documentation. Peer-reviewed or preprint evidence was not needed for the legal and control claims in this note.

Use the following editorial rules throughout the guide:

- Reserve **must** for a requirement whose jurisdiction, entity, transaction, date, and facts are stated. Use **recommended control pattern** or **should be designed** for a cross-sector implementation recommendation.
- Pair every workflow page with at least one applicable accounting or reporting source, one authority/control source, and one evidence or record source. Add a rail, filing, tax, privacy, or security source when the action triggers it.
- Treat the FASB Codification—not an Accounting Standards Update—as authoritative US GAAP. Treat local law and endorsement status—not the IFRS Foundation index alone—as the determinant of IFRS applicability.
- Do not treat a prompt, trace, guardrail, OAuth token, MCP connection, benchmark score, or model evaluation as proof of authorization, accounting correctness, audit evidence, or ICFR effectiveness.
- Do not infer a redistribution license. Link to paid or registration-gated standards and summarize at a high level without reproducing protected text.

### Coverage conclusion

The catalog is already sufficient to explain what an agent is, why approval and evaluation matter, and how audit evidence and AI risk should be considered. It is not sufficient by itself to source the eight full workflow families. The recommended additions below close that gap without trying to duplicate an accounting research platform.

The defaults below are editorial/control recommendations for this site. They are not universal legal classifications. Entity policy may be stricter, and law, regulation, contracts, bank mandates, or professional responsibilities may make an action human-only.

## Cross-cutting implementation findings

### ICFR, audit, and evidence

- [AS 2201](https://pcaobus.org/oversight/standards/auditing-standards/details/AS2201) supports mapping agent-affected transaction flows, period-end processing, authorization, records, and unauthorized asset use. Its current page notes amendments to paragraph .09 and a new .99 effective 15 Dec. 2026; do not describe those amendments as currently effective before that date.
- [AS 1215](https://pcaobus.org/oversight/standards/auditing-standards/details/AS1215) supports documentation from which an experienced auditor can understand the work, evidence, conclusions, significant findings, preparer/reviewer, and timing. Preserve a reviewable workpaper packet; a raw trace alone will usually be incomplete.
- [Rule 13a-15](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-240/section-240.13a-15) and the SEC's [management ICFR rule release](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/33-8238.htm) support treating material agent, connector, and configuration changes as part of the reporting-control environment for in-scope issuers.
- The [2025 GAO Green Book](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-25-107721) was released 15 May 2025, supersedes the 2014 edition, is effective beginning in fiscal year 2026, and permits early implementation. It is the federal internal-control standard; elsewhere, present it as a useful open reference rather than binding private-sector law.
- Keep control performance support separate from management's deficiency assessment, management's ICFR conclusion, and the auditor's independent judgments.

### Security, identity, and segregation of duties

- [SP 800-53](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/53/r5/upd1/final) supports account management, access enforcement, separation of duties, least privilege, audit events, configuration control, and contingency/privacy controls. It is a federal control catalog and tailorable reference, not proof of implementation.
- [SP 800-63-4](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/) supports identity proofing, authentication, federation, and lifecycle management for the human principal. [RFC 9700](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9700/) supports secure, narrow, revocable OAuth delegation. Neither source grants business authority.
- The current [MCP authorization specification](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2026-07-28/basic/authorization/index) is a protocol contract, not an accounting control opinion. Pin the specification version and test the connector's real scopes, consent behavior, confused-deputy defenses, revocation, and logging.
- Give each agent and service a distinct identity. Record both the human principal and the executing workload. Do not share a user's password or use a broad standing token when scoped, short-lived delegation is available.

### Evaluation, production monitoring, and incident response

- Use the existing [NIST AI RMF 1.0](https://www.nist.gov/publications/artificial-intelligence-risk-management-framework-ai-rmf-10) and [Generative AI Profile](https://www.nist.gov/publications/artificial-intelligence-risk-management-framework-generative-artificial-intelligence) for governance, mapping, measurement, treatment, provenance, pre-deployment testing, and risk-specific evaluation. NIST indicates that AI RMF 1.0 is under revision; label it accurately and monitor the [AI Resource Center](https://airc.nist.gov/).
- Add [NIST SP 800-137](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/137/final) for an organization-wide continuous-monitoring strategy and [SP 800-137A](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/137/a/final) for assessing monitoring-program completeness and effectiveness. Both are federal guidance that can be adapted, not universal accounting requirements.
- Add [NIST SP 800-61 Rev. 3](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/61/r3/final), final April 2025, for preparation, detection, response, recovery, and improvement. The incident record should cover stop/contain, credential revocation, evidence preservation, transaction reconciliation, notification decision, controlled recovery, and lessons learned.
- Maintain four separate assurance layers: deterministic validation of data and accounting calculations; model/task evaluation on representative cases; control-design and operating-effectiveness assessment; and production health/risk monitoring. A passing result in one layer does not satisfy another.
- Protect traces as governed records because they may contain credentials, personal data, tax information, confidential contracts, or material nonpublic information. Trace completeness and trace retention should be policy-driven rather than assumed.

### Records retention and reproducibility

- [SEC Rule 2-06](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-210/section-210.2-06) requires specified issuer audit/review records to be retained for seven years by the covered accountants. It is not a seven-year rule for every corporate agent log.
- [IRS recordkeeping guidance](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/recordkeeping) says records should be kept as long as needed to prove return items and gives specific periods for some records, including at least four years for employment-tax records. The applicable period depends on the record and facts.
- [IRS electronic-accounting-record guidance](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/use-of-electronic-accounting-software-records-frequently-asked-questions-and-answers) supports retaining original electronic detail that can reveal changes, cutoff, and underlying transactions rather than only a cleaned export.
- [NIST SP 800-88 Rev. 2](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/88/r2/final), final September 2025 and superseding Rev. 1, supports a sanitization program and validation, including modern logical/cloud considerations. It does not resolve legal-hold, accounting, tax, audit, contract, or privacy authority to delete.
- [GDPR](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj/eng) requires storage limitation and provides an erasure right with exceptions, including some legal-obligation and legal-claims circumstances. State EU/EEA and extraterritorial scope, and require legal analysis for the facts.

## Sources to retain, but not overread

| Source class | Correct use | Do not claim |
| --- | --- | --- |
| FASB Codification / IFRS Standards | Recognition, measurement, presentation, disclosure, effective-date, and transition basis under the applicable framework | That accounting authority grants operational system access or approves an agent's judgment |
| PCAOB / IAASB / AICPA audit standards | Auditor responsibilities, evidence, documentation, risk assessment, supervision, estimates, and ICFR audit considerations within scope | That a management control is effective, an agent output is reliable, or a record is sufficient merely because it resembles audit documentation |
| COSO / GAO / NIST controls | Control objectives and implementation patterns for authority, risk, access, change, monitoring, and response | That adopting a control list is certification or proves design/operating effectiveness |
| NIST AI RMF / GenAI Profile | Risk governance, mapping, measurement, treatment, provenance, evaluation, and monitoring | That a risk profile is accounting assurance, legal compliance, or a model certification |
| Provider documentation | Concrete implementation patterns for approvals, guardrails, tools, evals, and traces | Neutral evidence that the provider feature satisfies accounting, audit, legal, privacy, or payment requirements |
| Benchmarks and empirical studies | Bounded evidence about a task, population, tool, setting, and date | General accounting competence, production reliability, or permission to automate a workflow |
| Practice examples | What a named vendor or organization says it built or observed | Independent proof of capability, control effectiveness, or representative outcomes |

## Recommended page-level citation bundles

To keep workflow pages useful without source sprawl, use a small claim-linked bundle rather than attaching every related source:

1. **Accounting basis:** current FASB Codification or the applicable IFRS/local standard.
2. **Authority and control:** AS 2201, COSO, the 2025 GAO Green Book, or the applicable filing/payment rule.
3. **Evidence and record:** AS 1215, AS 1105, tax/SEC retention, or an entity record schedule, as applicable.
4. **Identity and tool security:** SP 800-53, SP 800-63-4, RFC 9700, and the pinned connector specification.
5. **Evaluation and operations:** NIST AI RMF/GenAI Profile, SP 800-137, and SP 800-61 Rev. 3.
6. **Action-specific rule:** EDGAR, IRS, ACH/Fedwire, OFAC, GDPR, or another jurisdiction/rail rule only when that action is in scope.

Every consequential page should state framework, jurisdiction, period/effective date, access limitations, and the last date its source set was checked. Where the correct authority depends on facts not present in the workflow record, the agent should stop at research or recommendation and route the decision to the accountable person.
