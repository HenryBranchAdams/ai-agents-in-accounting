# Thought pieces and research papers: source audit

**Research cutoff:** 2026-08-23  
**Editorial purpose:** identify a small, balanced set of research papers, professional reports, practitioner essays, and disclosed commercial examples that add substance to the existing Accounting Agents catalog without repeating its standards and benchmark coverage.

## Recommendation at a glance

- Add or feature the **14 research papers** and **10 reports/essays** in the two shortlists below. They provide a usable arc from pre-LLM audit automation through current agent reliability, professional judgment, governance, tax administration, and early finance-agent deployments.
- Treat every firm or vendor item as a **practice example or perspective**, never as neutral evidence. Its limitation should be visible in catalog copy.
- Keep the **16 reserve items** out of the main path unless they fill a specific editorial need. They are useful, but less direct, more promotional, or partly duplicative.
- Prefer a publisher DOI over SSRN/arXiv when a peer-reviewed version exists. Preserve an open preprint link as an alternate only when it materially improves access.
- Proposed topics and kinds use the site's current taxonomy; no schema change is required.

### Priority key

- **T1 — add/feature now:** distinctive, credible, and directly useful.
- **T2 — add to catalog:** useful supporting context; not a homepage feature.
- **T3 — reserve/watch:** include only for a defined editorial gap or case-study collection.

## Editorial use and placement

### Feature as “start here”

1. Murphy et al., *Exploring accounting and AI using topic modelling* — field map.
2. Samiolo et al., *Auditor judgment in the fourth industrial revolution* — judgment is present even in routine work.
3. Munoko et al., *Ethical Implications* — responsibility and accountability.
4. Rabanser et al., *Science of AI Agent Reliability* — evaluation vocabulary, explicitly labeled a preprint and cross-domain.
5. CPAB or IFIAR report — independent regulator perspective.
6. OECD tax chapter — tax/public administration perspective.
7. Willison, *Lethal trifecta* — memorable security boundary.
8. PwC/OpenAI finance-function announcement — visibly labeled a first-party practice example, paired with independent evidence.

### Presentation rules

- Put **source type and limitation next to the title**, not behind a click. “Peer-reviewed study,” “regulator observations,” “independent essay,” and “vendor example” should be visually distinct.
- Separate **what the source observed** from **what its author predicts or recommends**.
- Never convert survey intent, product adoption, or a vendor's internal metric into a general performance claim.
- On evidence pages, order sources by independence and method: regulator/peer-reviewed evidence, professional reports, practitioner analysis, then commercial examples.
- Add `reviewed_on`, `publication_status`, and `access_status` metadata when the catalog schema next changes; these prevent stale living pages and preprints from looking settled.

## Scope and method

The audit used publisher DOI records, journal/publisher pages, regulator and professional-body publication hubs, and first-party author or company pages. Secondary news coverage was excluded when the original paper, report, or announcement was available. Cross-domain sources were admitted only when they supply a reusable concept—agent reliability, capability boundaries, or security—that directly improves accounting-agent design or evaluation.
