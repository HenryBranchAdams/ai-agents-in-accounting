# US family office accounting research

This source package answers six bounded questions for issue 163: ownership and reporting perimeter, investment capital activity, shared costs, Texas trust activity, taxpayer coordination, and supplemental close. It reuses the accepted finance, property, holding-company, nonprofit and tax foundations. A family-office label does not establish an industry classification or adviser, broker or registered-fund status.

The new source records link the published SEC family-office rule, the Texas Legislature's current Chapter 116 host, and selected 2025 IRS instructions. Original FASB source identities are reused. The SEC published rule became effective August 29, 2011; its conditions and multifamily boundary are distinct from a conclusion about the synthetic office. Historical FASB amendments are not current consolidated ASC. The selected IRS tax year is 2025 and is separate from the illustrative September 2026 close.

The Texas example selects a domestic irrevocable non-grantor complex trust whose instrument permits income distributions and accumulation. It assumes no contrary instrument allocation or adjustment election. A long-held bond sale is principal; specified interest is income. The shared accounting bill relates to both income and remainder interests, so the selected section 116.201(2) and 116.202(a)(1-a) split applies to that expense. This does not create a half split for all administration costs or determine DNI and beneficiary tax treatment.

## Connected example

The office allocates a $12,000 outside bill to itself, the investment entity, the trust and the individual; $1,000 remains due from the investment entity. A $6,000 internal management fee, office payroll, custodian transactions, a private-fund call and distribution, and a $4,000 beneficiary payment remain visible on the appropriate entity books.

Quoted equity moves from 3,000 shares at $100 to 3,500 shares at $103, giving a $10,500 mark. Private-fund commitment is $250,000; cumulative funding is $140,000 and uncalled commitment is $110,000. The provisional book carry is $120,000, while a separate assumed tax-basis workpaper yields $110,000. A $140,000 June NAV is stale at September close and its $20,000 difference is held unposted. No final fund measurement is asserted.

The supplemental administration view removes $650,000 of duplicate inception interests, $1,000 of reciprocal balances, the internal fee and the beneficiary flow. It reports $1,060,500 of provisional carrying-value assets, $3,000 of outside liabilities and $1,057,500 of net assets. The $7,500 change reconciles to $31,500 of outside income/gains and $24,000 of costs. Cash falls from $450,000 to $400,000. Trust resources remain separately identified and are not represented as the individual's unrestricted property. This supplemental view is not a GAAP consolidation or current-value wealth statement.
