# Reading-room scholarship expansion

**Research cutoff:** 2026-08-23  
**Scope:** peer-reviewed research and a small number of high-quality working papers on AI, algorithms, and agents in accounting, audit, tax, professional judgment, accounting education, work design, controls, and assurance.  
**Deduplication basis:** `app/resources-data.ts` and `app/reading-room-data.ts` were inspected before selection. No application source was changed.

## Recommendation at a glance

- Add the **17 T1 sources** to the catalog and use the **10-reading route** below to expand the public reading room without turning it into an undifferentiated bibliography.
- Add the **14 T2 sources** to the broader catalog. Surface them from shelf pages, filters, and “related reading,” not all on the first reading-room screen.
- Reorganize the scholarship path around seven evidence questions: **judgment and reliance; evidence and controls; work design; management accounting; tax; education; and AI assurance**. Keep “Start here” as a route across those shelves rather than as a miscellaneous topic.
- Preserve publication status, method, jurisdiction, access, and a one-sentence transfer limit on every card. A finding from an auditor experiment, a qualitative firm study, a legal analysis, and an AI-governance framework should never look interchangeable.
- Do not infer that a study of audit data analytics, RPA, or classical machine learning proves the behavior of a generative agent. These sources are useful because they reveal durable human, control, and organizational mechanisms; their technology transfer limits must remain visible.

### Priority key

- **T1 — add and feature:** distinctive evidence that closes a material gap in the current reading room.
- **T2 — add to catalog:** credible supporting scholarship; useful for depth, comparison, or a specific shelf.
- **Hold/exclude:** credible-looking but duplicative, methodologically weak, version-sensitive, or not yet ready for public curation.

## Proposed 10-reading “start here” route

1. [Control issues: How providing input affects auditors’ reliance on artificial intelligence](https://doi.org/10.1111/1911-3846.12974) — meaningful human input changes reliance, but not uniformly.
2. [Inheriting Versus Developing Data Analytic Tests and Auditors’ Professional Skepticism](https://doi.org/10.1111/1475-679X.70014) — inherited analytics can reduce skeptical action; a development memo can help.
3. [Does automation improve financial reporting? Evidence from internal controls](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11142-024-09822-y) — automation is associated with fewer material weaknesses, but weaker later monitoring can make failures more consequential.
4. [Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) in auditing](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.accinf.2022.100572) — connects model explanations to audit evidence and documentation requirements.
5. [Using algorithms to improve knowledge work](https://doi.org/10.1002/joom.1296) — task automation and process redesign produce different results; the surrounding knowledge regime matters.
6. [Management accounting and artificial intelligence](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bar.2025.101551) — the strongest current field map for controllership and management accounting.
7. [Tax, Technology, and Craftsmanship](https://doi.org/10.2308/TAR-2024-0499) — tax compliance and tax advisory work do not automate along the same path.
8. [Refining public policies with machine learning: The case of tax auditing](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jeconom.2024.105847) — high-quality administrative-data evidence on audit selection, with explicit selective-label and fairness issues.
9. [The effects of generative artificial intelligence on learning in an accounting data analytics course](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaccedu.2025.100987) — accounting-education evidence with observed course outcomes and clear causal limits.
10. [Auditing large language models: a three-layered approach](https://doi.org/10.1007/s43681-023-00289-2) — separates provider governance, model, and application assurance.

## Deduplication and upgrade decisions

1. **No duplicate current catalog records were proposed.** The existing app already contains the main baseline papers on “Man Versus Machine,” AI audit-process outcomes, professional judgment, ChatGPT accounting performance, AI-supported auditor decisions, fraud-risk assessment, auditability, and early field evidence from accounting platforms.
2. **Do not add working-paper and journal versions separately.** The peer-reviewed *Journal of Econometrics* version of Battaglini et al. supersedes NBER Working Paper 30777 for the canonical record. Keep only the DOI version.
3. **Do not duplicate the existing `Human + AI in Accounting: Early Evidence from the Field` record.** It remains an important field source and should be cross-linked from Work design and Management accounting, but counted once.
4. **Do not duplicate the existing `Man Versus Machine` record.** The new reliance papers extend it through perceived control, adaptability, uncertainty, skills threat, and inherited analytics; they are not replacement versions.
5. **Treat ADA, RPA, ML, GenAI, and agents as related but distinct.** Catalog notes should state the technology actually studied. “Transfer to agents” is an editorial interpretation, not the paper’s measured claim.
6. **Use canonical publisher/DOI pages only.** Repositories and preprints are useful alternate-access links, but should not replace the version of record when one exists.
7. **Avoid broad agent-systems duplication.** The current catalog already covers agent architecture, long-task reliability, benchmarks, and security. The three cross-domain assurance papers here remain only because they add distinct provider/model/application layers, lifecycle audit artifacts, and three-lines ownership—not another general account of how agents work.

## Editorial and data recommendations

Every scholarship record should expose, either in the catalog object or an adjacent machine-readable evidence note:

- `publication_status`: peer-reviewed, working paper, conference paper, review, or conceptual article;
- `method`: experiment, archival/quasi-experimental, field case, qualitative interviews, systematic review, legal analysis, or framework;
- `population_and_jurisdiction`;
- `technology_studied`: RPA, ADA, ML, GenAI/LLM, or agent;
- `evidence_role`: outcome, mechanism, field implementation, synthesis, or governance design;
- `transfer_limit`: one sentence written by the editor;
- `access_status` and `reviewed_on`;
- canonical DOI/publisher URL and, only if supported later, a separate alternate-open-access URL.

For agent access, expose those fields in the public JSON/NDJSON feed and include a stable `source_id`. This allows an agent to filter for “peer-reviewed experiments with auditors,” “open tax-administration studies,” or “conceptual assurance frameworks” without treating every reading as the same kind of evidence.

## Selection method

The search prioritized primary publisher, DOI, ACM, and NBER pages; top accounting, auditing, information-systems, public-administration, law, operations, and labor-economics venues; and explicit methods or publication status. Sources were selected for one or more of the following roles: direct accounting evidence, a reusable mechanism for supervised agent work, an underrepresented jurisdiction or accounting function, or a rigorous assurance/control framework. Weak convenience surveys, duplicate versions, unsupported product claims, and papers whose only contribution was another broad “AI will transform accounting” narrative were excluded.
