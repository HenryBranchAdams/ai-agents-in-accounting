## Research findings

**Identify owner, developer, property manager, broker, contractor, and REIT roles.** A property-holding entity recognizes its own property, lease, financing, and operating activity. A third-party property manager collecting rent for the owner generally must distinguish amounts collected on the principal’s behalf from its own management fee/reimbursable costs; principal-agent presentation depends on control and contract facts, not the use of a trust account alone. A developer constructing property for sale has inventory/project-cost and customer-contract questions; an owner constructing property to hold for rental has investment-property/PPE and leasing questions. A REIT is a federal tax election/status with statutory tests, not a GAAP asset category. NAICS is only classification, not accounting authority. Existing `rq-aa-i107-managed-property-settlement` addresses the manager/owner settlement boundary; expand it with separate entity ledgers and owner reporting.

**Development-for-sale or rental and an acquired operating asset follow different cost models.** EY’s July 2026 ASC 970 guide says project costs directly associated with developing, constructing, selling and initial rental operations are generally capitalized, while other costs are expensed, subject to specified rules. Specific-project preacquisition costs are capitalized only when identifiable to the property, otherwise capitalizable after acquisition, and acquisition/option probable; general and administrative preacquisition costs are expensed. ASC 970 excludes own-use properties and does not govern acquisition of an already-operating property/business, which uses ASC 805 analysis. Project ledgers should track land/building/components, direct/indirect project costs, interest when qualifying, amenities, initial rental activity, placed-in-service stages, abandonment and impairment as a workpaper design recommendation. The guide’s contents address those stage-specific branches. This is current public professional interpretation with excerpts, not a substitute for current ASC 970/835/360 text. Historical FAS 67/66 records in the corpus are not current consolidated GAAP. [EY Real estate project costs, `cp_ey_real_estate_project_costs_2026`](https://www.ey.com/content/dam/ey-unified-site/ey-com/en-us/technical/accountinglink/documents/ey-frdbb1883-07-31-2026.pdf), §§1-2 and contents; ASC 970/835/360/842 and Topic 606 applications need entity-specific review. For federal tax, repairs/improvements and capitalization use the tangible-property regulations; MACRS depreciation begins when property is ready and available for its specific use. Tax placed-in-service date and book depreciation commencement should each be supported independently. [IRS tangible property regulations, existing `src_construction_irs_tangible_property`](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/tangible-property-final-regulations); [IRS Publication 946, `cp_irs_946`](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p946), “When Does Depreciation Begin and End?”; historical corpus sources `src_aa_i107_fas67_real_estate_costs`, `src_aa_i107_fas66_real_estate_sales`.

**Rent rolls should bridge lease-level facts to ledger balances.** For each unit/tenant, retain executed amendments, commencement/expiration, possession, base rent, abatements/free rent, tenant improvements/incentives, escalations/indexation, options, percentage rent, nonlease services, recoveries, billed/collected amounts, receivables, concessions, deposits, and termination status. Under ASC 842, classify lessor/lessee roles, assess identified asset/control and lease/nonlease components, determine classification and commencement measurement. For lessor revenue, analyze contract performance obligations and variable consideration rather than assuming every tenant reimbursement is rent. Existing `rq-aa-i107-rental-incentives-deposits` points to lease and revenue sources but does not yet synthesize the full owner rent roll or Topic 842 lessor accounting.

**Refundable deposits are liabilities while held for return; rent labels can change the substance.** The IRS’s 2025 residential rental publication says a refundable security deposit is not included in income when received if it is intended to be returned; amounts retained for tenant nonperformance become income when retained, and an amount designated as final rent is advance rent. That is federal tax guidance, not a US GAAP journal-entry authority; financial reporting must assess the contract and applicable lease/customer-consideration guidance. Keep the tenant-by-tenant liability subledger, receipts, application against damage/arrears, refunds, forfeitures, interest requirements, and trust/custodial cash in separate fields. Landlord deposit laws differ by state and locality; do not generalize the federal tax publication to legal deadlines or interest rates. [IRS Publication 527 (2025), `cp_irs_527`](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p527), “Security deposits,” rental income, repairs and improvements.

**CAM and lessor reimbursements now have a bounded public technical route.** Deloitte’s ASC 842 Roadmap identifies CAM as a nonlease service component, contrasting that with real-estate property tax and insurance costs that protect the lessor’s ownership interest and are not transferred services. In a triple-net lease, tenant-paid/reimbursed CAM is variable consideration for a nonlease component and the lessor analyzes it under ASC 606, including transaction-price estimation/constraint; actual lease wording and applicable practical expedients matter. In a separate lessor repair/capital improvement example, routine repair reimbursement may be CAM/ASC 606, while a discretionary roof replacement can remain within the lease component. The example spends $200,000 on a roof and charges $10,000 annually in each remaining year; the technical interpretation recognizes the variable lease revenue in those years as the lessor continues to make the improved asset available rather than immediately recognizing all future recoveries. These are bounded professional interpretations, not universal outcomes. Keep CAM pools, estimates, invoices, lease allocation denominators, caps/exclusions, reconciliations, tenant credits, billings, and true-up as an editorial workpaper control design. [Deloitte ASC 842 CAM discussion, `cp_deloitte_asc842_cam`](https://dart.deloitte.com/USDART/home/codification/broad-transactions/asc842-10/roadmap-leasing/chapter-4-components-a-contract/4-4-determining-allocating-consideration-in); [Deloitte lessor repairs/capital improvements, `cp_deloitte_asc842_lessor_reimbursements`](https://dart.deloitte.com/USDART/home/codification/broad-transactions/asc842-10/roadmap-leasing/chapter-9-lessor-accounting/9-4-other-lessor-reporting-issues).

**Property manager revenue and owner property revenue are separate.** The manager’s contractual fee is an output of its management service, not the owner’s rental income. KPMG’s 2026 real-estate revenue handbook gives a direct example: a property manager with a single service series and variable fees based on operating results evaluates allocation to each distinct service period; monthly percent-of-operating-results or cost reimbursements may be recognized in the period earned when the ASC 606 criteria are met, with a possible right-to-invoice expedient on facts. A sale with three years of prepaid future management services separates the property disposition from management, allocates cash by relative standalone selling prices, and recognizes the management amount over service delivery (including a significant financing component in that example). For cash control, reconcile rent/recoveries billed and collected, refundable deposits, owner remittances, manager fees, vendor invoices and owner/manager balances. The gross-to-net reconciliation is an editorial workpaper recommendation, not a universal ASC presentation requirement. [KPMG Real estate revenue handbook, `cp_kpmg_real_estate_revenue_2026`](https://kpmg.com/kpmg-us/content/dam/kpmg/frv/pdf/2026/handbook-real-estate-revenue.pdf), Questions C30, D30 and F130.

**REIT tax tests are not ordinary property close rules.** IRS 2025 Form 1120-REIT instructions identify quarterly asset tests and annual gross-income tests (including 75%/95% categories), as well as rules for tenant services and taxable REIT subsidiaries. These are federal qualification tests with detailed statutory/regulatory definitions, timing and cure provisions. Record gross-income character, property ownership/value by quarter, securities, mortgages, rents, service activity, tenant relationships, TRS charges, and distributions separately; a GAAP rental-income caption does not establish tax test eligibility. This pass did not analyze statutory definition exceptions or the full cure regime. [IRS Instructions for Form 1120-REIT (2025), `cp_irs_reit`](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1120rei), asset-test failures and tenant-service/TRS references; IRS Revenue Ruling 2014-37 (existing search evidence), 75% quarterly asset and income test discussion.

## Additions and guide shape

Expand the current US real-estate guide with distinct owner/developer/property-manager/REIT routes. Include an acquisition and development cost ledger; lease-level rent roll; deposits and advance rent subledger; CAM reconciliation; manager-to-owner trust cash and gross-to-net settlement; building/land/component depreciation; property disposition; and REIT tax qualification inventory. Show book GAAP, federal income tax, state/local landlord law, and regulatory/REIT qualification as separate bases. Cite the exact reporting entity and period for each proposed treatment.

## Unresolved gaps

- Current consolidated ASC 842/970/606/835 and Topic 740 text was not independently accessed; the current EY/KPMG/Deloitte manuals provide professional interpretation and embedded excerpts, not official live Codification access. Historical Statement 66/67 do not settle current GAAP.
- Public examples answer common ASC 970 development-cost, property-manager fee and CAM/reimbursement fact patterns; actual leases, project facts, contract changes, variable estimates, tenant improvements and financing remain contract-specific.
- State/local security-deposit segregation, interest, return deadlines, escrow/trust, landlord-tenant, property tax, sales/use tax, rent control, and broker/property-manager rules vary and were not surveyed.
- Full REIT qualification requires current Code/Regulations, ownership and look-through facts, quarter-end valuation, TRS and rent tests, prohibited transaction and distribution analyses; IRS form instructions are not the entire rule set.
- The research did not inspect actual leases, rent rolls, closing statements, construction budgets, property manager agreements or investor statements. It cannot test a real ledger or advise on a transaction.
