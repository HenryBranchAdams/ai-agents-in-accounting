## Research findings

**Keep at least three capital records separate.** A partnership’s financial-reporting capital/equity is governed by its reporting framework and entity accounting; tax-basis capital on Schedule K-1 Item L is a tax information-reporting measure; outside basis is partner-specific tax basis in the partnership interest. IRS 2025 Schedule K-1 instructions explicitly warn that Item L tax-basis capital is not outside basis and that outside basis must be adjusted for items such as the partner’s share of liabilities. A K-1 allocation can change tax reporting without a cash movement, while distributions can affect outside basis without matching book equity. Neither a section 704(b) book capital account nor tax-basis capital alone proves a partner’s outside basis. [IRS instructions, `cp_irs_k1`](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1065sk1), headings “Basis Limitations,” “Worksheet for Adjusting the Basis of a Partner’s Interest,” and “Item L.”

**Contributions require property-level and partner-level lineage.** Cash contributions ordinarily increase partner interests/capital under the governing agreement and relevant book framework. Contributed noncash property brings at least two basis/value populations: partnership carryover tax basis and contribution-date fair market value. Section 704(c) rules aim to allocate pre-contribution built-in gain or loss to the contributing partner through later tax items; book revaluation, tax basis, and 704(c) layers should not be collapsed. Maintain contribution date, contributor, tax basis, fair value support, liabilities assumed, allocation method, later dispositions/distributions, and remaining built-in gain/loss by asset. IRS Form 1065 instructions discuss reasonable 704(c) methods and note that later distributions can trigger sections 704(c)(1)(B) and 737 within seven years. [IRS instructions, `cp_irs_1065`](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1065), “How Income Is Shared Among Partners,” “Dispositions of Contributed Property,” and Schedule K/K-1 line 19b.

**Allocations are contractual but not purely elective for tax.** The partnership agreement supplies the economic allocation provisions, but federal tax allocations must be analyzed under section 704(b) and regulations. The regulation’s substantial-economic-effect test requires economic effect tied to partner economic benefits/burdens, capital-account maintenance under the regulatory rules, liquidation in accordance with positive capital-account balances, and deficit-restoration or an applicable alternative; substantiality is a separate test. If the agreement does not provide for an item or an allocation lacks substantial economic effect, the partner’s distributive share is determined by the partner’s interest in the partnership. IRS instructions also require changes in interests during a year, including admission, exit, and changes in percentage interests, to route through section 706(d) rules, generally proration or closing of the books subject to exceptions. [Treasury regulation, `cp_reg704`](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2025-title26-vol10/pdf/CFR-2025-title26-vol10-sec1-704-1.pdf), 2025 CFR annual edition, paragraphs (b)(2) and (b)(4); [IRS instructions, `cp_irs_1065`](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1065), “How Income Is Shared Among Partners.”

**Calls, distributions, withdrawals, and service payments need distinct event classes.** A capital call notice is not itself evidence that cash settled or that a partner’s commitment is legally enforceable; tie notice, partnership agreement, member-level call allocation, bank receipt, and ledger posting. A distribution is not a deductible expense. Federal tax rules generally reduce outside basis by money/property distributions, can recognize gain when money exceeds outside basis, and impose separate property basis rules; liabilities and marketable securities have special treatment. Service compensation to a partner may be a section 707(a) transaction or a section 707(c) guaranteed payment rather than a profit allocation or distribution. IRS Form 1065 instructions distinguish those routes and identify the special K-1 reporting. [IRS Publication 541, `cp_irs_pub541`](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p541), “Distributions,” “Guaranteed Payments,” and “Partner’s Basis”; [IRS instructions, `cp_irs_1065`](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1065), guaranteed payments and distributions.

**Admissions, transfers, exits, and liquidation need agreement-first work.** Record admission date, contribution consideration, agreed percentage/profit rights, capital-account/book revaluation, tax-basis consequences, any section 743(b) election/adjustment, and the partner’s share of liabilities. For interest transfers, distinguish transfer of an interest from partnership asset sale, and preserve transferor/transferee tax-year allocation, section 706(d) varying-interest method, and any section 754 election. A retiring/deceased partner’s payments may divide between property distributions under section 736(b) and distributive/guaranteed payment treatment under section 736(a), with unrealized receivables and goodwill facts material. Liquidation accounting must reconcile agreement rights, book capital, tax capital, outside basis, liability relief, and final tax allocations. IRS sources explain components but do not replace the partnership agreement or resolve all fact patterns. [IRS Publication 541, `cp_irs_pub541`](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p541), “Sale or Exchange of a Partnership Interest,” “Payments for Retiring or Deceased Partner,” and “Distributions”; [IRS instructions, `cp_irs_k1`](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1065sk1).

## Additions and guide shape

Add a US partnership/LLC guide with parallel schedules for: agreement and member register; book capital by financial-reporting basis; section 704(b) capital where relevant; tax-basis capital; partner outside basis and liabilities; 704(c) layers; tax allocations/K-1; commitment, call, settlement, and distribution; and admission/transfer/exit/liquidation events. Add worked but synthetic reconciliations that show a noncash contribution with built-in gain, an allocation without cash, a distribution with debt relief, a midyear admission, and a liquidating distribution. Label assumptions and do not present the schedule as legal or tax advice.

## Unresolved gaps

- Current consolidated professional guidance on US GAAP partnership equity, allocation and liquidation presentation was not publicly available in an authoritative paragraph-level source during this pass; accessible FASB Codification scope is not a substitute.
- No state-specific LLC acts, operating agreements, partnership agreements, carried-interest terms, or state/federal tax election scenarios were reviewed.
- Built-in loss limitations, basis ordering, at-risk/passive loss limitations, disguised sales, debt allocations, technical termination changes, centralized partnership audit adjustments, and tiered partnership reporting need focused research.
- Current-year 2026 tax form changes and legislation after 2025 were not independently reviewed; this package uses 2025 IRS return instructions as the available source edition.
- Existing corpus coverage is adjacent and selective; no sampled user records demonstrate a complete partner rollforward workflow.
