# 8. Private equity, venture capital, and hedge funds

Research date: September 22, 2026 (America/Chicago). US first. Findings are bounded research, not professional conclusions.

## Baseline and existing records

The screening set returns 9 guides, 36 questions, and 19 assessments, but most are shared foundations: consolidation, investments, custody, institutional reporting, and family-office activity. Area-specific existing questions include `rq-aa-i106-funds-valuation-custody`, `rq-aa-i121-investment-classification`, `rq-aa-i121-investment-losses`, and `rq-family-office-capital-activity`. They cover investment ownership/fair value, credit-loss and K-1 source routes, but there is no dedicated PE/VC/hedge-fund guide, waterfall, carry, or hedge equalization question. Existing source records include `src_aa_i106_sec_2a5_2020`, `src_aa_i124_custody`, `src_fasb_asc820_fair_value`, `src_fasb_asu_201601_equity_investments`, and family-office tax K-1 references. Registered-fund Rule 2a-5 material is bounded to that regulated entity class.

## Research findings

**Separate the investment vehicle, GP, adviser/manager, and portfolio company.** Each may be a distinct reporting entity. A portfolio company’s operating results are not the fund’s revenue; the fund recognizes its ownership/investment under its applicable framework. The manager records contractual fee income and its own payroll/vendor costs. The GP’s commitment, carried interest/promote, and other participation are separate from manager fees and fund operating expense. Consolidation depends on the applicable US GAAP control/variable-interest analysis, not labels such as “sponsor,” “GP,” or “investment vehicle.” Existing `rq-aa-i106-funds-valuation-custody` frames this role split, but does not determine specific fund consolidation conclusions.

**Value private investments at the reporting date using supportable fair-value evidence.** For an entity that qualifies as an investment company under ASC 946, KPMG’s August 2026 handbook says investments are generally measured at fair value and NAV is a core investor measure; those statements do not by themselves prescribe security-level calibration for each PE/VC instrument. Rule 2a-5 applies only to registered investment companies and BDCs. This pass therefore supports the fund-level measurement perimeter and fair-valued NAV, while recent-round calibration, preference-stack modeling, complex instrument valuation and portfolio company reporting remain open. The IPEV 2025 PDF is only a restricted discovery lead: its front matter states that access indicates acceptance of terms, so its substantive content is excluded. [KPMG ASC 946 handbook, `cp_kpmg_investment_companies_2026`](https://kpmg.com/kpmg-us/content/dam/kpmg/frv/pdf/2026/handbook-investment-companies.pdf), executive summary and scope; [SEC Rule 2a-5 guide, `cp_sec_2a5`](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/small-business-compliance-guides/good-faith-determinations-fair-value-small-entity-compliance-guide); existing `src_fasb_asc820_fair_value`.

**A capital call is not investment cost until the relevant activity is evidenced.** Keep commitment, call notice, cash receipt, investment funding, broken-deal costs, organizational costs, and fund-level expenses in separate event classes. Link LP-level call allocations to agreement provisions, use of proceeds, cash activity, and capital rollforward. Distributions require classification into proceeds, income, return of capital, recallable distribution, or other agreement-defined category; “net contributed” and “unfunded commitment” can move differently. ILPA 2025 v2.0 gives a standardized notice layout including LP commitment, unfunded balance, cumulative/current activity, transaction type, and impact on unfunded commitments, but does not prescribe ledger entries. [ILPA template, `cp_ilpa`](https://ilpa.org/resources-tools/resource-library/ilpa-capital-call-distribution-template-v-2-0/).

**Waterfall contract math and the ASC 946 presentation are related but different questions.** ILPA’s public Delaware-based deal-by-deal model LPA gives a detailed contract example: qualifying contributions and expenses return first, then a bracketed 8% annual preferred return (compounded annually and calculated daily), then a bracketed GP catch-up until GP carry equals a bracketed 20% of cumulative profits, then a 20% GP/80% LP split. It includes interim/final clawback, escrow and LP giveback. Under a simplified one-year, $100 contribution example with no fees, tax, loss or additional cash flows, the modeled sequence returns $100 capital, pays $8 preferred return, then $2 to the GP to bring its profit share to 20% of $10 total profit; subsequent profit is split 20/80. This arithmetic is an editorial illustration of bracketed model terms, not a typical-market assertion, universal agreement term or accounting conclusion. A useful workpaper follows the actual signed agreement’s definitions, including funded amounts, fee expenses, reserves, interim/final clawback and escrow. The model has brackets and is not an industry standard. Separately, KPMG’s ASC 946 handbook reproduces AICPA TQA guidance for nonregistered investment partnerships reporting capital by class: calculate ending investor-class equity as if assets were realized and liabilities settled at reported fair values and distribute net assets under the governing documents; in the covered examples, GP carry may be an expense or a disproportionate allocation to GP capital. These are technical interpretations bounded to ASC 946 scope; they do not decide an actual fund’s 718/other participant classification. Federal tax allocations under §§704(b), 704(c), and 1061 remain distinct from US GAAP fund capital and from cash distributions. [ILPA model LPA, `cp_ilpa_model_lpa_deal_by_deal`](https://ilpa.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/ILPA-Model-Limited-Parnership-Agreement-Deal-By-Deal.pdf), art. 14; [KPMG ASC 946 handbook, `cp_kpmg_investment_companies_2026`](https://kpmg.com/kpmg-us/content/dam/kpmg/frv/pdf/2026/handbook-investment-companies.pdf), TQA 6910.29; [IRS instructions for Form 1065, `cp_irs_1065`](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1065).

**Venture structures need dilution, follow-on, and multiple-security tracking.** Track each financing instrument and share class, pre/post-money or negotiated valuation basis, options/warrants/convertibles, liquidation stack, ownership percentage, follow-on rights, impairment/credit features, and portfolio-company reporting date. A simple ownership percentage cannot substitute for security-specific valuation or a consolidation/equity-method analysis. For a joint venture newly formed on or after January 1, 2025, FASB ASU 2023-05 requires the JV’s own separate financial statements to recognize and initially measure contributed assets/liabilities at formation-date fair values under its new model; the investor’s accounting is a separate question. Existing `rq-aa-i123-jv` provides this formation boundary. [FASB ASU 2023-05, `cp_fasb_jv`](https://storage.fasb.org/ASU%202023-05.pdf), summary and effective-date paragraphs.

**A hedge-fund example illustrates class/series fees and investor-specific side pockets.** KPMG’s fictional ASC 946 hedge statements show contributions, withdrawal payables, fees and partner capital, with domestic LP profits/losses allocated monthly. Side-pocket returns follow only participating investors as of pocket creation; the pocket cannot be redeemed until the designated investment is sold or deemed freely transferable. The offshore share-class example reports issued/redeemed shares, capital and NAV per share by class and series, and performance fees on each series’ NAV exceeding its original issue price or previous high NAV. A separate domestic partnership sample makes incentive allocations subject to a loss carryforward. The examples provide useful bounded statement models, not universal terms. KPMG’s ASC 946 handbook states loss carryforwards reduce future performance fees without creating an asset, and describes share equalization accounting for share issuance/repurchases. It does not establish that a private PE fund’s equalization credit/debit or an LPA subscription adjustment must follow the same method. Record each contract’s dealing cut-off, hurdle/high-water definition, series formation, crystallization, equalization, gates, pocket investor population and liquidation release terms. These recommended fields are editorial workpaper controls. [KPMG hedge fund illustrative statements, `cp_kpmg_hedge_illustrative_2025`](https://kpmg.com/kpmg-us/content/dam/kpmg/pdf/2025/illustrative-financial-statement-hedge-funds-2025.pdf), partners’ capital and class/series notes; [KPMG ASC 946 handbook, `cp_kpmg_investment_companies_2026`](https://kpmg.com/kpmg-us/content/dam/kpmg/frv/pdf/2026/handbook-investment-companies.pdf), equalization and performance fees.

**Current regulatory boundary matters.** The SEC’s 2023 private fund quarterly-statement, mandatory-audit, restricted-activity and preferential-treatment package was vacated effective June 5, 2024; SEC technical amendments reflecting vacatur became effective November 19, 2024. Current books must not assume that vacated package is effective. Separately, custody rule audit pathways, Form ADV duties, other Advisers Act obligations, registered fund rules, CFTC requirements, ERISA, and offering documents may apply based on facts. SEC/CFTC Form PF amendments adopted in 2024 have been extended to July 1, 2027; Form PF applies to covered advisers, including relevant hedge-fund reporting, and is not a financial-statement standard. [SEC vacatur, `cp_sec_vacatur`](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/2024/11/s7-03-22); [SEC Form PF compliance-date action, `cp_sec_formpf`](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/2026/08/s7-22-22).

## Additions and guide shape

Add a guide with PE/VC and hedge-fund paths. For PE/VC: commitment-to-call-to-investment, investment valuation, fees/expenses, portfolio-company and fund perimeter, realizations, waterfall/carry, clawback, investor reporting, tax K-1. For hedge funds: positions/derivatives/collateral, NAV cut-off, subscriptions/redemptions, investor series/equalization, incentive fee/high-water mark, side pockets, and administrator/custodian tie-out. Maintain a separate register for current regulatory status and supersession; the vacated 2023 private-fund rules should be retained only as historical source material.

## Unresolved gaps

- KPMG’s public 2026 handbook supplies substantial ASC 946 interpretation, but current Codification text remains controlling; ASC 820 private security/recent-round calibration is still open and the restricted IPEV content is excluded.
- The public ILPA model LPA and KPMG hedge illustrations cannot represent actual fund terms. No actual LPA, side letter, PPM, administration agreement, valuation policy, audited financial statement or transaction-level record was reviewed.
- Publicly read material supports common hedge high-water/series and side-pocket illustrations; actual equalization variants, notice periods, gates, valuation fallbacks and incentive fee reversals still depend on contracts and administrator policy.
- Tax carried-interest §1061, partnership audit regime, blocker/international tax, withholding and state sourcing require separate current-year research; derivative/collateral and prime-broker accounting need a specialist source pass.
- SEC/CFTC registration, exemptions, custody, Form PF reporting thresholds and state requirements need current entity-specific review.
