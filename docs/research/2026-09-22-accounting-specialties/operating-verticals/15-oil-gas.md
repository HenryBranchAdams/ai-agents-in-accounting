Research date: September 22, 2026 (America/Chicago). Primary scope is U.S. oil and gas exploration/production. Separate U.S. GAAP/SEC reporting, contractual joint-account billing, federal royalty reporting, state royalty rules, and federal tax treatment. This is bounded research and not a legal, engineering, tax, or professional accounting conclusion.

## Findings by accounting question

### Roles and rights: operator, nonoperator, mineral owner, and service contractor

The operator conducts joint operations and may incur/pay costs and bill participating working-interest owners, but is not necessarily the sole owner of production or the mineral/royalty interest. A nonoperator's participating/working interest is a contractual share of costs/risks and production rights, which can differ from royalty burdens and tract/mineral ownership. A mineral/royalty owner may be paid a royalty and is not automatically a cost-bearing working-interest owner. A service contractor earns consideration for services, not a working interest merely because it receives production-linked payment. Contractual JOA/COPAS accounting procedures, lease/title instruments, division orders, purchaser statements, and state law establish different facts. COPAS guidance states that it does not supersede the written agreement. See `ov-oilgas-copas` and `src_us_extractive_onrr_45`.

### JIB chargeability and nonoperator audit

Joint-interest billing (JIB) is contract administration under the signed operating agreement and its attached accounting procedure, not a universal GAAP chart of accounts. COPAS's paid 2022 model-form product page lists charge classes such as labor, materials, transportation, services, operator/affiliate-furnished equipment, overhead, inventory and adjustments, but its detailed $220 text was not accessed. The publicly accessible Fall 2023 notice contains MFI-40, titled '24-Month Adjustment Period for Joint Account Adjustments,' marked 'Committee-Approved Draft - July 20, 2023'; its publication date remains a placeholder. The notice says a vote was scheduled, not that it was adopted. The draft proposes a 24-month model-form interpretation and gives first-time-charge examples on pp. 45-46; those examples are not the title or evidence of adoption. Do not represent its proposed rules as a final/current COPAS interpretation. A nonoperator audit still keys each billed share to the signed JOA/accounting procedure, invoice/property/authorization, participation interest, overhead basis, credits/transfers and contract objection deadlines. See `ov-oilgas-copas`.

### Operator revenue, purchaser settlements, and royalty distributions

Production statements, purchaser statements, operator cash, owner revenue distributions, royalty remittances, and ledger revenue are distinct populations. For principal-agent, determine who controls the specified oil/gas sale before transfer and identify the party's promise to the customer; cash-routing role alone does not justify net revenue. Use volume/quality/price/measurement and purchaser deductions to reconcile sale proceeds, then allocate ownership/royalty rights with effective-dated decimal interests and subsequent adjustments. A later division-order change may correct who receives proceeds or who bears a joint-account charge without changing what commodity was sold or the original gross sales event. Existing synthetic example and `rq-us-extractive-revenue-presentation` already support this boundary; current title/contract facts remain entity-specific. See `src_fasb_asu2016_08_principal_agent` and `src_us_extractive_onrr_45`.

### Ownership changes and mineral interests

Do not use one percentage for royalty title, federal mineral ownership, tract allocation, working interest, participating interest, purchaser decimal, and ONRR payor responsibility. Maintain effective dates, transfer instruments, title opinions, division-order revisions, suspense/held proceeds, and state/federal lease identifiers. COPAS's interpretation treats working-interest ownership adjustments as one of the model-form exceptions to the ordinary JIB adjustment window. ONRR's handbook example shows a federal agreement with multiple leases/payors and independently identifies tract allocation, federal/Indian mineral ownership interest, company working interest, price, royalty rate, and volume; that demonstrates why report decimal and ownership populations must be explicit. It is federal reporting methodology and not private title/ownership determination. See `ov-oilgas-copas` and `ov-oilgas-onrr`.

### SEC successful efforts/full cost, depletion, and abandoned property

The SEC's dated 2025 Regulation S-X 4-10 is registrant-specific. It routes successful-efforts filers to ASC 932 disclosure requirements, while the full-cost method applies across the reporting entity and subsidiaries with country-by-country cost centers. Full-cost acquisition, exploration, and development costs are capitalized within the cost center subject to scope and direct-cost limitations; production costs are expensed as incurred. Reserves-based amortization and ceiling-test rules depend on the rule-defined cost base; abandonment/sales treatment under full-cost differs from successful efforts. Separately, federal income-tax depletion is not the book depletion calculation: current 2025 IRS partner instructions describe owner-level partnership reporting, and IRS Publication 535 (2022 edition, cited by those instructions) describes percentage depletion eligibility and limits, including 15% for qualifying independent producers or royalty owners subject to production and retailer/refiner exclusions (`ov-oilgas-irs-k1`, `ov-oilgas-irs-pub535`). Tax eligibility must be checked for the taxpayer and year; do not reuse GAAP reserve depletion as a tax deduction. SEC rule needs current CFR/entity scope verification; engineering/reserve evidence is absent. See `ov-oilgas-sec`.

### ARO versus remediation; book/tax/royalty distinctions

An asset retirement obligation concerns a legal obligation associated with retirement of a tangible long-lived asset; environmental remediation from improper operation/contamination follows a different contingency/cleanup analysis. FASB Statement 143 in the corpus is a superseded original statement and establishes only historical model concepts, not current ASC 410 wording. Current ASC 410-20/450 and fact-specific laws, permits, plugging duties, timing, credit-adjusted risk-free measurement inputs and revisions need review. Do not confuse GAAP ARO measurement with state/federal plugging bond or financial-assurance amount, tax deduction/tax depletion, SEC disclosure, or ONRR royalty allowance. The federal 4-10 capitalization/depletion model and ONRR royalty calculation are their own regimes. See `src_us_extractive_fas143_historical`, `src_aa_i123_fas143`, `ov-oilgas-sec`, and `ov-oilgas-onrr`.

### Federal royalty reporting and transport/processing allowances

ONRR Release 4.5 (dated March 18, 2026) is current operational reporting guidance for its covered federal/Indian mineral revenues. Its ONRR-2014 examples separate sales volume and value, ONRR lease/agreement, report month, royalty value before and after transport/processing allowances, and payor; federal/Indian ownership and working-interest facts can coexist in the same fact pattern. Its transaction codes and allowances follow federal lease/regulatory rules. The handbook expressly does not establish private royalty ownership or GAAP revenue. An operator's gross transport payment is not itself an allowable royalty deduction: the lease/program rule, allowance qualification/limit, and actual transportation/processing cost evidence govern. State royalties and private leases require separate authority. See `ov-oilgas-onrr` and the existing `rq-us-extractive-royalty-reporting`.

- Current full-text COPAS 2022 procedures and detailed interpretations are restricted/paid. The public Fall 2023 MFI-40 is explicitly a draft circulated for comment with a proposed vote, and adoption/publication is unverified; it cannot substantiate final/current adjustment rules.
- No publicly inspected AAPL executed/model JOA, title opinion, division order, state pooling/forced-pooling order, or actual JIB audit package. State law and agreement controls remain unresolved.
- No evidence supports a current consolidated ASC 932/410 application beyond the date-limited SEC rule and superseded Statement 143; Codification access and entity method/elections are unverified.
- Proved reserves, book depletion/unit-of-production calculations, full-cost ceiling, successful-efforts capitalization, impairment, and taxpayer-specific tax depletion require engineering/entity/tax records. The IRS sources add the federal tax-versus-book framework but do not resolve taxpayer qualification, basis, annual limits, or current law.
- Federal ONRR handbook is operational federal/Indian royalty reporting only. Private/state royalties, state severance taxes, federal income-tax basis, and SEC registrant disclosure are separate and incomplete.
- No live production/purchaser/JIB/royalty data, professional review, or legal ownership determination was made. Rights remain unknown where no explicit permission statement was inspected.
