# Area 13: SaaS and subscription businesses

Research date: September 22, 2026 (America/Chicago). This is bounded source research, not professional advice. Primary scope is U.S. nongovernmental entities applying accrual-basis U.S. GAAP; IFRS examples and customer-side software costs are called out separately.

## Findings by accounting question

### Deferred subscription revenue, customer credits, and billing

Invoice dates, contract billing schedules, cash receipts, and performance are separate events. Advance billing and cash collection can create a contract liability before the service transfer; a provider's stand-ready access subscription is commonly satisfied over time, but the contract's distinct promises, period of access, cancellation/termination terms, and whether the customer controls a license or receives hosted access have to be established. Credits may be price concessions, refunds, service-level credits, or consideration payable to a customer; their accounting depends on the promised service and contract facts. The baseline's provider/cutoff examples already distinguish cash and billing from earned subscription/usage revenue (`rq-software-subscription-timing`, `rq-information-provider-usage-subscription`). KPMG's public current-edition page confirms its Dec. 2025 software/SaaS ASC 606 and 340-40 guide covers the five steps, modifications, and contract costs, but only its summary and contents were accessible here. See `ov-saas-kpmg25` and `ov-saas-revcore`.

### Contract modifications and renewals

For a modification, first identify whether scope or price changed; an additional distinct service priced at its standalone selling price is accounted for as a separate contract. Otherwise, remaining distinct services may be treated prospectively as termination/new contract, while a change to a partially satisfied non-distinct performance obligation produces a cumulative catch-up. Blend-and-extend SaaS renewals are fact-sensitive when new services are added while old remaining rates are repriced. Deloitte's public worked example describes three interpretive views for comparing blended price to standalone prices and says an elected approach should be applied consistently to similar contracts. It is interpretive firm guidance and dates to 2019, not an authority amendment; current Codification and later developments need review. See `ov-saas-blend` and baseline source `src_construction_fasb_2014_09`.

### Usage billing and credits

Do not default to straight-line revenue or invoice-date recognition just because the invoice is variable or the arrangement is SaaS. Deloitte notes a hosted SaaS arrangement often transfers a service rather than an IP license, so the sales/usage-based IP royalty exception often does not apply. ASC 606 still requires estimating variable consideration subject to constraint unless a specific expedient/exception applies. Deloitte describes the invoice practical expedient for over-time stand-ready service when the invoiced amount directly corresponds to value delivered, such as fixed-rate usage. Fixed fees, substantive minimums, changing rates, and upfront/back-end amounts can complicate that conclusion; check the contract and allocation facts (`ov-saas-usage`). Nonrefundable prepaid amounts remain a contract liability until service transfer. For actual unexercised rights, Deloitte summarizes proportional breakage recognition when entitlement is expected, otherwise recognition when further exercise is remote, subject to the constraint; remittances to government remain liabilities (`ov-saas-breakage`). A credit can instead be a refund, price concession, service-level credit, or material right, and must be classified by contract facts, refundability, purpose, transfer pattern, multiple promises, rollover/expiration, and unclaimed-property law. Existing blend-and-extend material remains useful for modifications but is not a universal SaaS usage rule (`ov-saas-blend`).

### Sales commissions and contract acquisition costs

ASC 340-40's basic screen is whether the cost would not have been incurred absent obtaining the contract and whether it is expected to be recovered. Qualifying incremental acquisition costs are recognized as an asset unless the one-year-or-less practical expedient is elected. The asset is amortized systematically with transfer of the related goods/services, potentially including expected renewals. Renewal commissions and initial commissions that differ materially make the benefit period judgmental; commission plans, clawbacks, compensation liability timing, and data supporting expected customer life are relevant. Deloitte's public technical discussion summarizes the distinction between truly incremental commissions and general selling costs and highlights commensurate renewals and amortization. This is interpretive guidance, not the FASB Codification. See `ov-saas-commission` and the existing KPMG guide record.

### Software development and hosted implementation costs

Keep three populations separate: provider software for sale/licensing (ASC 985-20); the provider's internal-use platform (ASC 350-40); and a customer's implementation work in a hosting arrangement (ASU 2018-15 points service-contract customer implementation to internal-use software guidance). KPMG's 2026 public manual interprets ASC 985-20 as expensing costs through technological feasibility, capitalizing later product-master production costs only to the extent recoverable until general release, and expensing maintenance/support after release (`ov-saas-kpmg-software26`). KPMG's scope analysis adds that hosted software used only to provide services, without a customer license or a substantive external marketing plan, is internal-use software under ASC 350-40; a substantive plan to market the software externally can put it in ASC 985-20. It is interpretive firm guidance, not authority, and project facts determine the scope. For internal-use software, ASU 2025-06 was issued September 2025 and is future effective for annual periods beginning after December 15, 2027 (early adoption allowed). It removes sequential stage references and requires capitalization when management authorizes and commits funding and it is probable the project will be completed and used as intended. It is not the default for a 2026 adopter that has not early adopted. See `ov-saas-asu2506`, `src_asu201815`, `src_roadmap_asu202506`; current consolidated ASC text and entity adoption remain to verify.

## Editorial workpaper recommendation

Enrich `guide-software-subscriptions` with a focused provider close schedule linking contract version, subscription/service dates, billings, cash, contract liability, usage-event population, credits/refunds, renewal/modification assessment, and contract-cost asset rollforward. Add separate branches for prepaid/expiring usage credits and refund/service-level credits, plus an explicit commission renewal/clawback example. Preserve the existing distinction between provider revenue, customer implementation, and internal-use software. A KPMG December 2025 guide is a strong discovery candidate for enrichment, but the inspected public page does not establish its detailed accounting examples; licensed full text must remain out of scope absent access rights.

## Unresolved gaps

- No publicly inspected FASB consolidated Topic 606/340-40 text or entity-specific accounting policy; 2014 amendments and firm interpretations do not prove current codification wording.
- The inspected guidance supplies bounded answers on SaaS usage expedients and breakage, but no fact pattern was tested for multi-year rollover credits, substantive minimums, usage floors, refundable balances, customer concessions, or unclaimed-property laws. Current Codification and contract terms remain necessary.
- KPMG's public manual adds substantive ASC 985-20 development-cost interpretation, but provider scope is clarified by KPMG's 2026 interpretation for hosted-only service software, but applying the no-license/substantive-marketing-plan tests still requires project facts; no current Codification or entity policy was accessed.
- Commission renewal commensurateness, expected contract term, clawback, recoverability, and impairment require plan- and entity-specific evidence.
- Tax recognition, state sales tax on SaaS/digital services, contract enforceability, and non-U.S. VAT are separate questions not answered here.
- No operational ledger, contract population, user validation, or professional review was performed. Rights to full text remain with publishers; permissions are unknown.
