# 7. Cost and managerial accounting

Research date: 2026-09-22 America/Chicago; some source retrievals occurred 2026-09-23 UTC. Scope: US-first internal cost measurement, operational control and the boundary to external inventory accounting. The textbook below is educational managerial accounting, not authoritative GAAP.

## Findings

**Job, process and hybrid cost accumulation.** Job-order costing traces identifiable materials and labor and applies overhead to an individual job or service order; it suits heterogeneous work where the information benefit justifies job-level records. Process costing accumulates cost by process/department over continuous or batch production of substantially similar output, then determines unit cost from accumulated process costs and output volume. A system can combine them, for example process-costing components followed by job-costing custom assembly. The design choice follows production/service flow and decision value compared with information collection cost. OpenStax supplies textbook explanations, examples, records and inventory/period-cost distinctions; this is educational managerial guidance, not normative GAAP [cca_openstax_managerial].

**Overhead and ABC.** A conventional predetermined overhead rate pools manufacturing overhead and assigns it using one volume-related driver, often labor or machine hours. That is simpler and can be a reasonable management approximation when the driver tracks resource use. ABC distinguishes unit, batch, product and facility activities, pools costs by activity and uses multiple drivers such as setups, purchase orders, tests or customer contacts. It can change unit-cost patterns, often shifting costs to low-volume or transaction-intensive outputs, but collecting drivers costs money and judgment is needed for facility-level and nonmanufacturing cost treatment. Use ABC where the improved decision signal exceeds measurement cost, and label it a management view. A management ABC model does not itself determine which costs are includable in GAAP inventory; reconcile rather than replace the external reporting basis [cca_openstax_managerial].

**Standards and variances.** The textbook explains a standard cost card as planned input quantities and prices/rates per unit. Compare actual material price and quantity with standard price and allowed quantity; compare labor rate and hours with standard rate and allowed time. For variable overhead, separate rate/spending effects from allocation-base efficiency; for fixed overhead, distinguish spending from production-volume/capacity effects using an explicit budget/activity convention. OpenStax’s worked examples demonstrate the arithmetic and also caution that a favorable or unfavorable label does not explain cause and can distort behavior when detached from organizational goals. Standards support budgets and investigations but must be refreshed as processes and conditions change. These formulas are managerial analysis conventions, not GAAP-mandated variance categories or journal entries [cca_openstax_managerial].

For financial-inventory reporting, current US GAAP is in ASC 330. FAS 151 is historical amendment evidence: fixed production overhead allocation used normal capacity; unusually low output does not inflate per-unit fixed overhead, and abnormal idle facility expense and waste are period costs. It is not a substitute for checking current ASC 330 or inventory-method exceptions. For federal-contract cost recovery, FAR 31.201-1 permits standard costs in total contract cost only with applicable variance adjustment; FAR allowability is a different test from internal performance or GAAP inventory [cca_fas151, cca_far31]. Keep three bridges separate: standard to actual (management), actual/allocable/allowable contract cost (procurement), and inventory-to-COGS classification (GAAP).

**Contribution and allocated profitability.** Contribution margin is sales less variable cost, the amount available to cover fixed costs and then profit. It helps with short-run choices and constraints but is not a fully allocated product margin. A fully allocated view can add traceable fixed, capacity, and shared costs using stated cost objects and defensible drivers; label noncausal or arbitrary assignments, avoid treating allocated sunk cost as avoidable, and show relevant/avoidable costs separately for a decision. Product, customer, service, channel and segment views can differ because they use different boundaries and cost-to-serve drivers. OpenStax teaches contribution margin and ABC product-cost effects, but does not establish one universal profitability report. Existing project performance/allocation guides remain selected internal examples [cca_openstax_managerial].

## Recommended additions

- Add a short internal-costing guide with a shared fact pattern showing job, process, standard and ABC views, cost-object choice, data capture, rate/driver trade-offs and reconciliation.
- Add a standards and variance guide with material/labor/overhead actual-to-standard bridge, capacity convention, investigation evidence and ledger treatment clearly marked as entity policy.
- Keep current ASC 330 technical authority separate from the management text and FAR; inspect current paragraphs and method-specific exceptions before writing external-reporting instructions.

## Unresolved gaps

OpenStax is a substantive textbook read and closes the conceptual mechanics gap, but it is not a current professional standard or technical GAAP manual. Its textbook simplifications must not be elevated into authoritative policy. Current ASC 330 text, normal capacity/abnormal waste detail by fact pattern, standard-cost posting and inventory adjustments, applied/actual overhead journal entries, equivalent-unit calculations, joint products, and mix/yield variance alternatives need technical follow-up. The linked IMA full report remains unread; only its public synopsis was inspected and it is not needed to support the textbook findings. No empirical evidence on costing-system adoption or accuracy was reviewed. The publisher page states CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 with attribution and separately says the book may not be used for LLM training or otherwise ingested into generative-AI offerings without OpenStax permission. That restriction is distinct from reading and independently summarizing source material; no book text is reproduced here, and no blanket permission for corpus reuse is inferred. Check license conditions and the separate restriction before reuse [cca_openstax_managerial, cca_ima_managerial_model].
