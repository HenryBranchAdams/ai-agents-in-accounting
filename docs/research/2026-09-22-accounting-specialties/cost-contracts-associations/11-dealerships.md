# 11. Auto and equipment dealerships

Research date: 2026-09-22 America/Chicago; Lithia 2025 Form 10-K retrieved 2026-09-23 UTC. Scope: US auto and equipment retailers. Public filings show issuer-specific policy and evidence chains, not a universal GAAP result. Analyze role, executed program/contract, title/control, delivery and accounting period.

## Baseline and current coverage

Baseline has one classification guide, `guide-industry-naics2022-441`, plus shared inventory, revenue and principal-agent sources such as `src_ias02inv`, `src_fasb_asu2015_11_inventory`, `src_fasb_asu2016_08_principal_agent`, and `src_fasb_asu2016_04_gift_cards`. No dealer-specific questions or assessments exist. NADA’s public course overview identifies dealer topics but is only a training outline, not a technical authority or full course read [cca_dealer-nada]. The central source added here is Lithia’s SEC-filed 2025 10-K, which gives current dealer-side disclosed policies for inventory, manufacturer receivables, trade-ins, F&I, parts and aftersales [cca_dealer-lithia-2025].

## Findings

**Vehicle inventory and trade-ins.** Lithia reports new/used vehicles at lower of cost or net realizable value using specific identification; unit cost includes equipment added, reconditioning and transport. Parts use FIFO/lower of cost or market in its reported policy, and manufacturer purchase discounts reduce parts carrying cost and COGS when sold. For a trade-in, Lithia measures the vehicle at its stand-alone selling price using third-party pricing sources. Its sale revenue policy requires contract and financing/collectibility conditions and transfer of control. These statements make VIN-level appraisal, customer contract, payoff/title/possession, recon, inventory aging, delivery and cutoff evidence core workflow records. This is one company’s policy and does not resolve all trade allowance accounting or control-transfer facts [cca_dealer-lithia-2025].

**Floorplan, assistance and incentives.** Lithia discloses manufacturer receivables include holdbacks, rebates, incentives and warranty claims. It says holdback, floorplan-interest and advertising assistance reduce vehicle carrying value; specified assistance and rebates tied to vehicles reduce COGS when the related vehicle sells. Track lender floorplan payable/interest separately from inventory and OEM assistance receivable; link vehicle eligibility, OEM program terms, funding, sale date and clawback conditions. Other incentives can depend on volume periods, retail delivery or distinct advertising services, so do not generalize one issuer’s policy to every incentive or infer that customer cash is the same as dealer consideration [cca_dealer-lithia-2025, cca_dealer-group1].

**Warranty claims versus service contracts.** Lithia includes OEM warranty claims among manufacturer receivable categories, a useful starting point for matching repair orders, labor/parts, claim submission, OEM approval, receivable and settlement/chargeback. Its filing says it acts as agent on extended service contracts when third-party providers set price and commission is preset; F&I revenue is net of estimated chargebacks. Aftersales parts/service revenue is recognized on transfer of control; certain service offerings create contract liabilities recognized over time as service is provided. FTC material explains the commercial/legal distinction between included warranties and separately purchased service contracts, but it is consumer guidance rather than accounting authority. Exact claim entitlement, dealer warranty obligations, risk transfer, contract-liability term, cancellations and principal/agent conclusions require the actual arrangements and applicable ASC 460/606 [cca_dealer-lithia-2025, cca_ftc-warranty].

**Service, parts and department reporting.** Repair-order records should connect job/customer/warranty/internal-pay category to technician time, parts and returns, sublet services, discounts, claim approval and invoice. Lithia’s aftersales policy and service-contract balances substantiate separate revenue streams and deferred service obligations, but do not prescribe a dealership chart of accounts or service-department cost allocations. Department gross-profit reports are internal management views; reconcile to consolidated GAAP revenue, inventory/parts COGS, warranty receivables and contract liabilities. F&I is a distinct role analysis: the Lithia example supports agent classification under its specified pricing/commission facts, not all dealer add-ons [cca_dealer-lithia-2025].

**Equipment dealership transfer.** Lithia evidence is automotive-specific. Deere’s manufacturer filing offers equipment-maker-side incentive/warranty context but does not establish dealer recognition [cca_dealer-deere]. Equipment dealers need serial-specific new/used machines, attachments, parts, demo/rental fleets, trade-ins, OEM program terms, warranty claims and floorplan; auto policies should not be transferred without equipment-sector dealer evidence. NADA is auto focused and the course detail was not accessed [cca_dealer-deere, cca_dealer-nada].

## Recommended additions

- Add a dealer close guide keyed by VIN/serial, title/control/delivery, trade allowance and payoff, recon/parts, floorplan, manufacturer program, warranty work and F&I contract.
- Use the Lithia filing as a clearly labelled 2025 issuer example, then seek public current dealer-side technical guidance and an equipment dealer filing before generalizing.
- Review current ASC 330, 606, 460 and relevant 705 guidance for exact program terms, financial statement policy and effective-date conclusions. Keep state title/franchise/sales-tax rules in jurisdiction-labelled legal/tax branches.

## Unresolved gaps

Issuer disclosure is not a standard setter and covers one dealer group, with acquisitions and operations outside the US. It does not resolve accounting for trade-in lien payoffs, all OEM/co-op incentive variations, warranty claim entitlement timing, manufacturer chargebacks, floorplan lender costs or equipment demo/rental fleets. No current publicly accessible dealer CPA manual or actual dealership closing packet was inspected. NADA Fundamentals is only an overview. State title, odometer, franchise, consumer warranty and tax rules vary and were not comprehensively surveyed.
