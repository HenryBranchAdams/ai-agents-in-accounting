# 12. HOAs and condominium associations

Research date: 2026-09-22 America/Chicago; specialist pages retrieved 2026-09-23 UTC. Scope: US common-interest realty associations (CIRAs), with California, Florida and source-specific Washington examples only. State statutes and governing documents affect member rights, reserve use and reporting; they do not by themselves establish GAAP recognition. Tax and contract-liability analysis must remain separate.

## Baseline and current coverage

Area 12 has no baseline guides or questions and only a shared FASB ASC locator `src_1os761s`. FASB’s June 2024 board handout describes Topic 972 as covering CIRA fund reporting, common-property accounting and future major repair/replacement disclosures, while identifying unresolved stakeholder questions about assessments and other issues. It is agenda discussion, not the Codification or a standard amendment [cca_hoa-fasb]. Public specialist CPA sources add useful but nonauthoritative technical interpretation of reserve funds and assessment revenue; the authors disagree about whether replacement assessments can be contract liabilities [cca_hoa_specialist_reserve, cca_hoa_specialist_revenue].

## Findings

**Operating and replacement-fund reporting.** HOACPA, an association CPA practice, quotes ASC 972-205-45-8/-9 and 972-720-25: replacement-fund presentation includes assets, liabilities and fund balance specifically associated with long-term major repair/replacement activity; other CIRA activities are in the operating fund; where fund accounting is used, major repair/replacement expenditures are charged to the appropriate replacement fund. This gives the requested public specialist-level interpretation and an actual paragraph locator. It is a 2018 practitioner FAQ, not current Codification. Verify each quoted paragraph in current ASC and analyze governing documents, board designation, bank restrictions and state-law constraints separately [cca_hoa_specialist_reserve].

**Assessments and accounting disagreement.** The FASB handout reports that stakeholders requested clarification on fund reporting, common-property accounting and revenue recognition for assessments; it does not decide the questions [cca_hoa-fasb]. DesRoches & Company argues that regular assessments continue to be recognized in the period assessed and rejects a PPC Guide position that replacements-reserve assessments should be Topic 606 contract liabilities until spent. The CPA authors reason that board discretion to redirect funds weighs against a present liability and favors members’ equity/fund presentation. That is a substantive public practitioner position, not authoritative GAAP. Because a different professional guide reportedly reaches the opposite result, current assessment terms, enforceable restrictions, promised services, discretion, collectibility and then-current ASC 606/972 must be examined; do not encode either position as universal [cca_hoa_specialist_revenue, cca_hoa-fasb].

**Operating/reserve cash and future work.** A reserve study estimates components, remaining lives, replacement costs and a funding plan. It is planning/legal evidence, not automatically a GAAP liability or proof cash is legally restricted. Distinguish cash account segregation, Topic 972 fund presentation, board designation, governing-document or statutory restrictions, and an actual present obligation. California §5550 requires periodic inspection/study and annual review for covered associations; §5300 calls for annual disclosures including a pro forma accrual operating budget, reserves/funding plan and anticipated special assessments. Florida §718.112 contains reserve waiver/use constraints, pooling and structural-integrity reserve-study rules for qualifying condominiums. These are jurisdiction- and effective-date-specific legal requirements, not standards for revenue, asset capitalization or recognition of future repair cost [cca_hoa-ca5550, cca_hoa-ca5300, cca_hoa-fl718112].

**Special assessments and major repairs.** Preserve approval and member-vote evidence, governing-document authority, purpose, allocation, due/collection dates, any return/use conditions, project contracts and incurred work. California reporting surfaces anticipated special assessments and estimated timing/amount, but disclosure is not GAAP recognition. A future repair plan alone differs from an incurred cost or present obligation. For a funded project, separately assess assessment revenue/contract liability, capital improvements/common property under Topic 972/360, repair expense, construction in progress and cutoff. The public sources do not resolve these fact-specific branches [cca_hoa_specialist_revenue, cca_hoa_specialist_reserve, cca_hoa-ca5300, cca_hoa-fasb].

**Developer transition and reports.** Florida §718.111 identifies accounting records/source documents and statutory reporting thresholds and addresses turnover records and developer obligations. §718.112 supplies relevant reserve controls. These determine legal records, handover and report duties in Florida; opening GAAP balances still require a reconciliation of bank confirmations, assessment ledgers, contracts, payables, common-property title, developer advances/receivables, reserves and transition claims. A reserve study or developer books alone do not prove association-owned assets or complete liabilities [cca_hoa-fl718111, cca_hoa-fl718112].

**Tax is separate.** IRS 2025 Form 1120-H instructions describe a possible §528 election and exempt-function income/expense tests. These tax categories do not determine GAAP assessment revenue, fund balance, or whether a liability exists. Maintain a tax-to-financial-reporting bridge and verify the relevant tax year [cca_hoa-irs1120h].

## Recommended additions

- Add an assessment and fund reporting guide with competing assessment-revenue views, the current ASC 972/606 authority, evidence needed for restrictions and performance obligations, and examples of operating/replacement fund presentation.
- Add state-law overlays for report thresholds, special-assessment voting, reserve use, reserve studies, records, and developer turnover. Mark jurisdiction and effective date; California and Florida are examples, not a US survey.
- Add transition/opening-balance and major-repair project schedules; separate legal fund labels, GAAP presentation, federal tax treatment and bank restrictions.

## Unresolved gaps

Current detailed ASC 972/606 text was not accessible, and FASB agenda discussion does not resolve accounting. The two practitioner interpretations differ and one cites a PPC Guide that was not independently reviewed; date/current applicability and precise facts may change the analysis. HOACPA’s reserve FAQ dates to 2018. California §5300 was read from a secondary reproduction after the official page fetch failed; Florida §718.112 text inspected was 2025 and needs current amendments/applicability verification. Only selected state provisions were reviewed. Common-property conveyance, developer advances, interfund borrowing, capital improvement versus repair, insurance proceeds, construction defects and assessment collectibility need more original and current sources. No actual association statements or audit files were reviewed.
