# 5. Employee benefit plan accounting

Research date: September 22, 2026 (America/Chicago); source retrieval occurred September 23 UTC.

## Baseline and coverage

The inclusive baseline links `guide-q-benefits` and unrelated adjacent `guide-utilities-us-regulated-close`. Benefit questions include `rq-benefits-scope-treatment`, `rq-benefits-evidence-controls`, and `rq-us-i120-benefits-scope-treatment` / `rq-us-i120-benefits-evidence-controls`; it also surfaced environmental, household payroll and unrelated-activity questions, which do not constitute plan-accounting coverage. Existing records include `src_roadmap_ias19_overview` (IFRS employer accounting), `src_1os761s` (FASB ASC landing page), `src_fasb_asu201707_employee_benefits`, `src_i120_fas87_dc_contributions` (historical), `src_dol_ebsa_reporting_disclosure_guide`, and `src_irs_pub15b_2026`. The FASB benefit-plan reporting topics are not covered by those employer-fringe / IAS 19 materials. Existing employer accounting and tax guidance must be distinguished from plan financial statements, plan fiduciary administration, ERISA filings and audit requirements.

## Research findings

**Separate reporting entities and bases.** A defined-contribution plan's plan-level accounts, participant account balances and Form 5500 reporting are distinct from the sponsoring employer's compensation expense, payable, contribution deduction, payroll tax and any employer benefit obligation. The employer's IAS 19, US GAAP or tax treatment does not define plan-level financial statement treatment. DOL/IRS/PBGC Form 5500 series is regulatory annual reporting, not itself US GAAP (`specialist_reporting_benefits_form5500`; `specialist_reporting_dol_form_selector`).

**Plan statements and core populations.** Form 5500 instructions and schedules call for beginning/end assets and liabilities, employer/participant contributions, benefits paid, transfers, investment income and holdings/transactions; DOL's audit overview states audit objectives include fair valuation of covered plan assets, proper presentation of obligations, timely contributions, plan-term-compliant benefits, participant account balances when applicable, tax status issues and prohibited transactions (`specialist_reporting_benefits_dolaudit`, sections on audit requirement/objectives). The plan document/trust agreement, participant census, payroll deferrals, employer match calculations, remittance dates, custodian statements, investment valuation data, benefit elections/eligibility, distribution authorizations and participant ledgers therefore form distinct evidence populations. DOL states employee contributions withheld by an employer become plan assets at the earliest date they can reasonably be segregated; late remittance risk should not be buried in employer payable reconciliation (`specialist_reporting_benefits_form5500`, Form 5500 framework; precise deadline/regulation is case-specific and not reproduced here).

**Reporting and audit scope.** “100 participants” is not a universal count rule. The DOL final rule applicable for plan years beginning on/after Jan. 1, 2023 counts defined-contribution pension-plan participants/beneficiaries with account balances at the beginning of the year for small/large Form 5500 reporting classification (`specialist_reporting_dol_2023_dc_count`, pp. 7-11). The DOL tool preserves the 80-120 continuity rule; other plan types follow their form-specific participant definitions (`specialist_reporting_dol_form_selector`). A small pension audit waiver is a separate branch with qualifying-asset or enhanced-bonding conditions and required disclosures/support on request, not a blanket exemption based on count alone (`specialist_reporting_dol_waiver_faq`, pp. 1-6). The 2025 instructions PDF could not be opened directly (403), so its indexed text is discovery only and contributes no substantive finding; current plan-year instructions must be consulted. DOL describes independent qualified public accountant duties and auditor qualifications (`specialist_reporting_benefits_dolaudit`, paras 93, 97, 99-105). The AICPA's ERISA 103(a)(3)(C) audit primer page (July 10, 2026) identifies limited-scope election, qualified institution certification, management responsibilities, current environment, and audit effect as topics, but the primer download requires a free account; its content was not read, so the details remain restricted (`specialist_reporting_benefits_aicpa_103`). Do not imply an elected 103(a)(3)(C) audit relieves management of responsibility or provides a full opinion on all investment information without reading current standard text and election/certification facts.

**Contributions, investments, distributions.** IRS Form 5500-EZ 2025 instructions explicitly allow cash, modified-cash or accrual basis consistently for Part III; the instructions define total plan assets to include specified investments and unrealized changes, liabilities to include benefit claims payable and operating payables (but not future distributions), and employer/participant contributions as received and/or receivable (`specialist_reporting_benefits_5500ez`, Part III lines 541–569). This reporting choice is specific to that return section and does not override a plan's applicable financial-statement GAAP. IRS explains defined-benefit benefits are promised by formula and do not depend on investment returns, with employer usually contributing and actuarial funding / Schedule SB roles; it states an enrolled actuary signs the schedule (`specialist_reporting_benefits_db`, defined-benefit-plan page). For a participant-directed defined-contribution plan, participant values and allocations must reconcile to plan terms, participant-level records and trust assets; gains/losses, fees, forfeitures, rollovers and distributions should be mapped explicitly. FASB’s public 2015 amendment text identifies plan net assets and investment disclosure, including disclosure of intervening contributions, distributions and significant events when a nearest-month-end measurement date is elected (`specialist_reporting_fasb_asu2015_12`, Parts I-III). FASB’s XBRL implementation guide illustrates statement lines for employer/participant contribution receivables, fair/contract-value investments, investment income, distributions and net-asset rollforward (`specialist_reporting_fasb_ebp_xbrl_guide`, Examples 1a and 2c). The former is dated amendment text and the latter is explicitly illustrative, not authoritative GAAP; both are bounded public evidence, not a replacement for current ASC 960/962.

## Recommended additions

- Replace the generic benefits guide with paired employer-accounting and plan-reporting guides; label separate entity, basis, and report purpose at entry.
- Add plan-close workflow/questions for payroll deferrals and remittance timing, employer match/receivable and vesting, custodian-to-GL investments and fair value, participant-unit/account allocation, benefit/distribution authorization, plan expenses, forfeitures, loans, prohibited transactions, Form 5500 schedules, independent audit/waiver, and subsequent events.
- Add a defined-benefit branch that routes actuarial obligation/funding disclosures, PBGC/Title IV and Schedule SB to qualified actuaries and separate employer accounting from plan statements.
- Add current DOL/IRS Form 5500 instructions as a filing source and a controlled, access-limited AICPA/FASB authority pointer for audit and plan GAAP, with public alternatives clearly scoped to compliance reporting.

## Unresolved gaps

- Current consolidated FASB ASC 960/962 plan financial statement requirements and detailed fair value/participant allocation guidance were not accessed. Existing FASB record `src_1os761s` is a login-restricted Codification landing page and supports no paragraph-level claim. Public ASU 2015-12 and XBRL examples provide bounded context only.
- The AICPA 103(a)(3)(C) primer's full text was account-gated; only its page description was inspected. Need current ERISA audit standard text, regulation and qualified-institution certification to describe election boundary precisely.
- Benefit plan type, participant count, welfare vs pension, ERISA coverage, small-plan waiver, pooled separate accounts/master trusts, collective trusts, multiemployer, 403(b), ESOP, and defined-benefit actuarial/PBGC branches can change rules; not all are resolved here.
- The Form 5500-EZ cash/modified-cash/accrual permission is return-specific; not general plan GAAP. Current annual filing instructions change. No employer-specific tax, fiduciary, audit, or actuarial conclusion is made.
