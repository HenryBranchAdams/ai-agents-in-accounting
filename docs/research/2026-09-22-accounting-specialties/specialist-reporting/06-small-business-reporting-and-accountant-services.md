# 6. Small-business reporting and accountant services

Research date: September 22, 2026 (America/Chicago); source retrieval occurred September 23 UTC.

## Baseline and coverage

The inclusive baseline links `guide-q-reporting-basis`, `guide-q-compliance-assurance`, and `guide-q-professional-governance`. Its questions span IAS 8 policy selection/change, US framework routing, special-purpose/federal-award assurance, AI and professional duties, as well as unrelated state payroll, Canada/UK, nonprofit, education, client-trust and family-office cases. Existing sources include `src_ias08pol`, `src_1os761s`, `src_secfrm26`, GASB/FASAB/IFRS and assurance references, and professional-governance/AICPA records. This provides broad basis/assurance context but no small-business bookkeeping/reporting conversion workflow and no clear preparation-vs-compilation-vs-review distinction for nonpublic entities. AICPA’s current SSARS index and public comparison article were read, along with a public Journal of Accountancy transcript featuring the AICPA ARSC chair. An AR-C 70 PDF surfaced in search indexing, but that text is discovery-only and supports no finding; current standard text was not read.

## Research findings

**Name the basis and purpose before adjusting.** A small entity may maintain cash-basis books, use modified cash internally, prepare accrual financial statements under US GAAP, and separately use an eligible tax method. These are different reporting outputs. A reporting-basis statement should identify its basis and intended users; a tax return method does not automatically establish an external financial reporting framework. Modified cash is not one uniform standard: identify actual recognition/depreciation/inventory/long-term contract conventions, departures, and the framework or contract defining them. For a US GAAP output, accrual adjustments may include unbilled revenue/receivables, customer deposits/deferred revenue, vendor accruals, payroll/benefits, interest, inventory/COGS, fixed assets/depreciation, debt and owner transactions; eligibility and measurement depend on entity facts and applicable GAAP, not just a checklist.

**Cash vs accrual in federal tax.** IRS Publication 538 (revised Jan. 2022, current page inspected) says cash method generally recognizes receipts when received/constructively received and deducts when paid; accrual method generally recognizes earned income and incurred/capitalized expenses under timing rules. It explains method consistency, restrictions/exceptions (including inventory-related exceptions and gross-receipts rules), and Form 3115 for many method changes (`specialist_reporting_smallbiz_pub538`, Cash Method, Accrual Method, Inventories, Change in Accounting Method). Its dollar thresholds are indexed and the publication is dated; verify current law, current IRS instructions, aggregation and taxpayer facts. Publication 583 (Dec. 2024) identifies books supporting financial statements and tax returns, source of receipts, supporting invoices/receipts, separate business bank account, and periodic reconciliation (`specialist_reporting_smallbiz_pub583`, Recordkeeping; Supporting Documents; Reconciling). These are federal tax/recordkeeping sources, not a general GAAP accounting manual. IRS labels Publication 538 as not intended to guide general business and tax accounting rules; it should not be stretched into non-tax reporting conclusions. Deloitte’s technical article and 2024 companion explain special-purpose frameworks including modified cash, describe observed changes such as including only deferred revenue and other entries affecting cash, and warn that there is no standardized recipe; suitability depends on intended users (`specialist_reporting_deloitte_special_purpose`, article sections “Typical special purpose exceptions” and “Common stumbling blocks”; PDF pp. 2-5). Deloitte’s examples focus on financial services and are not a universal small-business basis definition.

**Preparation, compilation, review and audit boundaries.** AICPA’s currently effective SSARS index says SSARS covers preparation and compilation/review reports for unaudited statements or information of a nonpublic entity, current as of August 2026 (`specialist_reporting_ssars_current`). AICPA’s public comparison explains: compilation provides no assurance and independence is not required (lack of independence must be reported if applicable); review requires independence, inquiry and analytical procedures, limited assurance and a conclusion; audit requires independence, internal-control/risk assessment plus verification and substantiation, yielding high but not absolute assurance and an opinion (`specialist_reporting_aicpa_service_comparison`, sections on each service). A Journal of Accountancy interview with the AICPA ARSC chair explains preparation as an engagement to prepare financial statements without a report or assurance, and says the service includes a no-assurance statement and identifies departures from the selected framework (`specialist_reporting_jofa_arc70_explanation`, transcript lines 131-154). This is explanatory, not authoritative standard text; the interview does not establish exact legend placement, current wording, or all exceptions. Do not convert plain-language comparison into a substitute for applicable SSARS/AU-C, state law, engagement facts or report requirements. User, lender, contract or regulation may require a particular service.

**Practitioner scope and client decisions.** For a practical small-business close, agree whether deliverable is management-use cash reports, modified-cash statements, tax-basis statements, GAAP statements, preparation, compilation, review, audit, agreed-upon procedures, or a tax filing. Document reporting period, entity boundary, accounting basis, special-purpose criteria, known departures, owner/personal transactions, inventory, payroll/tax obligations, debt, related parties, estimates, and who approves adjustments. Keep bookkeeping and record cleanup distinct from an assurance conclusion. The client's management retains responsibility for financial statements/records and choices; a tax preparer, accountant or agent must not imply independent assurance without an engagement and applicable work.

## Recommended additions

- Add a US small-business reporting guide with an intake route for entity type, user/purpose, jurisdiction, reporting framework, tax method, inventory, contracts/debt, and requested service level.
- Add a conversion checklist that ties cash records to bank/customer/vendor/payroll/tax evidence, identifies cut-off and accrual adjustments, segregates owner contributions/draws and nonbusiness items, and separately reconciles return-to-book differences.
- Add a service matrix for preparation, compilation, review, audit and agreed-upon procedures. Distinguish objective, procedures, report/no report, assurance level, independence/acceptance, management responsibility, and suitability limits using current AR-C / AU-C primary text before publishing precise requirements.
- Add focused questions: what does cash vs modified cash mean in this engagement; which adjustments are needed for the named reporting basis; which tax method is permitted and how is method change authorized; what did the accountant do and what assurance does the report communicate; what belongs to client management vs accountant; which documentation supports a review or compilation.

## Unresolved gaps

- Current full AR-C 70/80/90, SSARS interpretations, AU-C audit standards and ethics provisions were not substantively read. Public AICPA comparison and the AICPA ARSC chair interview support high-level service distinctions; the official AR-C 70 PDF direct open redirected and indexed text is discovery-only. Exact report elements, omissions, preparation applicability, management responsibilities, compilation independence disclosures, review procedures and special-purpose framework language remain for current standards review.
- No public, comprehensive FASB private-company/small-business GAAP alternative was identified; there is no broad US GAAP exemption solely because an entity is small. Topic-specific elections and private-company alternatives require current ASC access.
- Modified-cash conventions vary; no standard-form definition was established here. State accountancy laws, lender covenants, franchise/regulatory rules, tax elections, S corporation/partnership/LLC basis and inventory/contract facts may control separate branches.
- Publication 538 is dated 2022 and thresholds/rules can change. Check the current Code, regulations, forms and IRS developments before tax-year conclusions. No tax advice, assurance opinion or entity-specific accounting recommendation is made.
