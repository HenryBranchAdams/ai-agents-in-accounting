# 4. Bankruptcy and restructuring accounting

Research date: September 22, 2026 (America/Chicago); source retrieval occurred September 23 UTC.

## Research findings

**Debtor reporting is a separate statutory/court reporting population.** Under the U.S. Trustee Program's uniform reporting rule effective June 21, 2021, non-small-business and non-Subchapter V Chapter 11 debtors-in-possession/trustees use UST Form 11-MOR monthly before confirmation and Form 11-PCR post-confirmation; small-business and Subchapter V debtors use Official Form 425C pre-confirmation and must ask the district U.S. Trustee about post-confirmation requirements (`specialist_reporting_ust_ch11`, overview and forms dates). The UST instructions place responsibility for completeness on the debtor/trustee, require separate supporting attachments and generally impose a 21st-of-following-month filing date subject to local rule/order (`specialist_reporting_mor_instructions`, general instructions). This is case-level regulatory/court reporting, not automatically GAAP financial statements or a replacement for management reporting. Local districts can set procedures and deadlines, and forms/instructions can change; verify current version for each case.

**Books and information architecture.** Close and preserve prepetition records, then maintain a distinct postpetition population supporting estate receipts/disbursements, operational results, cash, assets/liabilities, professional fees, tax, bank reconciliations/statements, and required schedules. The practical reporting objective is to tie the submitted form to underlying books, bank records, payroll, tax filings and court orders while explaining basis and adjustments. Keep debtor entity, estate, subsidiaries, non-debtor affiliates, and jointly administered case entities distinguishable. The accessed UST instructions distinguish report form by debtor status and require supporting documentation, but the exact line-by-line accounting-basis choices and district instructions require current full instruction review in each case.

**Schedules, claims and creditor populations.** Official Forms list nonindividual debtor schedules A/B (property), D (secured claims), E/F (unsecured claims), G (executory contracts/unexpired leases), H (codebtors), and Statement of Financial Affairs, as well as Proof of Claim Form 410 (`specialist_reporting_bankruptcy_forms`, forms list, lines 608–613 and 631). Schedules and claims are legal case documents, not just accounts-payable aging: preserve creditor identity, amount, nature/priority/security, disputed/contingent/unliquidated status, debtor's books vs creditor assertion, supporting instrument, amendments, bar date, objections and resolution. The official form index identifies forms but does not settle claim allowance, priority, valuation, secured status, or distributions; these are Bankruptcy Code/court decisions with counsel and trustee roles. An unexplored U.S. Courts educational excerpt describes claims resolution starting with schedules and official claims, but was not used as primary binding authority.

**Reorganization / fresh-start accounting.** FASB ASC Topic 852 is the current U.S. GAAP authority; detailed current Codification text remains subscription/access restricted. EY’s April 2026 public technical guide reproduces selected ASC 852 text and explains both eligibility conditions: immediately before confirmation, reorganization value must be below postpetition liabilities plus allowed claims, and existing voting shareholders must receive less than 50% of emerging voting shares, with loss of control substantive and not temporary (`specialist_reporting_ey_asc852`, ASC 852-10-45-19; pp. 49-50). EY describes reorganization value as value of the reconstituted entity plus expected net realizable value of assets disposed before reconstitution, before liabilities. If eligible, assign reorganization value to assets/liabilities under ASC 805-20 principles, residual unassignable value is goodwill, deferred taxes are determined under ASC 852-740, and predecessor operations presentation reflects adjustments (pp. 50, 54-55). If criteria fail, asset carrying amounts are not reset and compromised liabilities are stated at present value of amounts to be paid. This is bounded nonauthoritative interpretation of selected paragraphs, not a complete current Codification review. Court-approved plan value, emergence date, valuation assumptions and transaction-specific facts need current licensed ASC and professional review. Keep petition-date claims/disputes, valuation/effective date, creditor settlement/equity allocation and post-emergence opening balances distinct.

**Liquidation basis.** Public FASB ASU 2013-07 gives the actual Topic 205-30 text. Liquidation is imminent when an authorized liquidation plan has been approved and it is remote that others block it, or a plan is imposed and return from liquidation is remote (ASC 205-30-25-1 to -3). Adoption is prospective from that date (45-2). Assets reflect estimated cash/consideration expected from settlement or disposal under the liquidation plan, not presumed fair value; liabilities continue under their applicable Topics adjusted for assumptions changed by the liquidation decision, without anticipating legal release; estimated disposal costs are separately accrued (30-1 to -3). Minimum statements are a statement of net assets in liquidation and changes in net assets in liquidation (45-1); update estimates each reporting date (35-1). A bankruptcy petition or financial distress alone is not the threshold (`specialist_reporting_fasb_asu2013_07`, 25-1 to -3, 30-1 to -3, 35-1, 45-1 to -2). The amendment's full current codified context and later amendments still require verification.

## Recommended additions

- Add a US Chapter 11 operating-report workflow: chapter/small-business/Subchapter V status, pre/post-confirmation route, current official form, entity perimeter, books/bank tie-out, supporting documents, sign-off, filing, and local-rule verification.
- Add a creditor-claims guide distinguishing scheduled claims from filed proofs, claim class/status and evidence reconciliation, amendments/objections, bar dates, and allowed-claim decisions reserved to the applicable process.
- Add separate research questions for ASC 852 reorganization/fresh-start (current paragraph access needed) and Topic 205-30 liquidation basis (imminence, measurement, costs, presentation, disclosures); clarify bankruptcy reporting vs US GAAP vs tax reporting.
- Retain going-concern and subsequent-event guides as adjacent timing work; add current U.S. bankruptcy primary sources rather than stretching IFRS standards to US procedures.

## Unresolved gaps

- Restricted current consolidated ASC 852 was not accessed. EY’s April 2026 public guidance resolves the core eligibility comparison and broad reorganization-value assignment route, but detailed measurement, current disclosures, subsequent amendments, and case-specific valuation/application still require current ASC access and qualified review; the public ASU 2025-12 example alone remains insufficient.
- Current consolidated ASC 205-30 text and all required disclosures were not checked against licensed Codification. Public ASU 2013-07 supplies amendment text but should be checked for later amendments and current integration.
- Bankruptcy Code/Rules, local rules, district UST instructions, case-specific orders, tax/employee/trustee matters and cross-border Chapter 15 were not systematically researched. Deadlines and filing content vary by chapter, district and case order.
- Claims allowance/priority, valuation and plan feasibility are legal/case judgments. No case conclusion, restructuring advice or debtor solvency assessment is made.
